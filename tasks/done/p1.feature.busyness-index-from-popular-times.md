# Measure foot traffic from Google popular times and show whether the buzz filled the place

## Goal
Report venues get a weekly `VenueBusyness` row (the 7×24 popular-times histogram and, when present, the live percent sampled at the venue's peak hour), and the report gets a section "Did the buzz fill the place?": the month's views by week next to the busyness index by week, with the peak day and hour, and the same for the month before.

## Why
Views are vanity until they turn into guests. Google's popular times are the only free, venue-level foot-traffic proxy, and they move: a histogram sampled weekly over months shows whether a viral week lifted the peaks. This is the correlation an owner pays for ("the creator video on the 12th: Saturday busyness went from 60 % to 95 %"). It is also the first signal with a time axis finer than a month, which the owner portal charts need.

## Scope
- In: `VenueBusyness` entity (venue, `sampledAt`, `histogram` jsonb, `livePercent`, `liveText`), a weekly pg-boss cron (`busyness-sample`, Thursdays and Saturdays 19:00 local) running a Places detail scrape by `placeIds` for WON prospects, the current report venues and their competitors; `busynessIndex` = mean of the histogram's top 10 hours (0–100) so weeks compare; `ReportData.busyness` (weekly index, peak day/hour, previous month) and a two-line chart in `ReportView`; the admin venue page shows the latest histogram.
- Out: paid foot-traffic APIs (BestTime and the like), live sampling more than twice a week (cost and Google's own smoothing make it noise).
