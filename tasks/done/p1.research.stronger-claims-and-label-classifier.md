# Measure a stronger claims reader and a decision-model classifier for the claims' labels

## Goal
The eval says, on numbers, (1) whether agent A on a stronger model finds more of the claims and places than on
mini, and (2) whether `ClaimLabelsClassifier` (Jev) labels claims (sentiment, aspect, kind) as well as agent B,
at what share of "sure" answers. With both answers the user picks the production design: A on which model, and B or
the classifier (with the unsure answers sent on to B or an LLM) for the labels.

## Why
- B is ~95% of a review's tokens (one call per claim, its ~1,750-token aspect list each time): ~3.3k tokens a
  claim on the nightly backlog (2026-11-04: 1,078 claims, 3.5M tokens). With the weekly fetch of every venue's
  reviews (~1,300 written reviews a day in Berlin, ~2.8 claims each) and 42k written reviews unread on 2026-11-05,
  A + B on mini needs more than the two accounts' 20M mini tokens a day.
- A decision model picks one option per question with probabilities, no text: B's sentiment, aspect and kind
  are exactly that. Jev bills ~$0.042 a million input tokens: measured $0.00015 a claim (~4.2k tokens, most of it the 62 aspect descriptions), so B's labels for Berlin (~150k claims a month) ~$22 a month.
- Freed from B, the allowance can carry a stronger A, which is where the post metrics lose most (`one-v2`: post
  claims found 86%, places found 88%).
- The rival design is `one-call-reader` (in progress): A and B merged into one gpt-5.4 call. Both are compared on
  the same base.

## Scope
- In: the eval runs and their comparison, logged in `apps/backend/eval/climbs/claims-and-labels/log.md`; the
  decision written into this task for the user.
- Out: wiring the classifier or a new A model into production (a follow-up task once the user picks); foods (a
  classifier names none: they stay B's, or move into agent A in a later change); agent C; matching.
