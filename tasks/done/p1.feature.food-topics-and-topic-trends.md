# Add a food topic taxonomy and pull Google Trends by topic

## Goal
A `FoodTopic` row per kind of food (cuisine, dish, drink, diet, format, meal), with aliases, an optional parent and the Google Trends topic id. Every raw cuisine string (a mention's, a Google category) resolves to zero or more topics. `SEARCH_INTEREST` searches every topic that has a Trends id or query, in the Food & Drink category, and the trends page compares each topic's searches with its share of mentions.

## Why
The pull searched bare English cuisine keys in DE-BE, and Google's own related queries show what got measured: "german" is translation ("english german", "german to english"), "thai" is massage, "american" is American Express, "market" is the stock market, "chinese" includes Chinese New Year and "chinese ai". A Google topic (`/m/01xw9` Chinese cuisine) is language independent (it counts "china restaurant", "chinesisches restaurant") and disambiguated; category 71 (Food & Drink) strips what's left. The data also needs one set of keys: 831 distinct `normalizeCuisine` keys across Berlin's mentions and categories, many compounds ("vegan kebab", "brunch cafe", "korean fried chicken").

## Scope
- In: `FoodTopic` (module `food-topics`), a seed migration (~110 topics from Berlin's keys, mids looked up through Trends autocomplete), `FoodTopicService` (index, resolve, searched topics, the admin read), pure resolver (`util/food-topics.ts`: exact key or alias, else longest spans over words, so a compound gives several topics; closure with parents), `SearchInterestService` on topics (two actor runs: topics with category 71, venues with category 0; mention shares by topic with parents rolled up), `GET /admin/food-topics?cityId=` (topics with counts, unmapped keys), the trends panel's labels and kinds.
- Out (own tasks): editing topics in the admin and constraining extraction (`food-topics-admin-and-extraction`), venue tags and the other `normalizeCuisine` consumers (`venue-food-topic-tags`), cross-topic comparable levels and the public report (`public-food-trends-report`).
