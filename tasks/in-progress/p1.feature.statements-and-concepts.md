# Statements, atoms and one concept tree

**This file is the program's brief**: the goal, the decisions taken with the user and the target model. The
work is six tasks, one per phase, each self-contained and pointing back here by slug (`statements-and-concepts`):
`topics-tree` → `terms` → `statements` → `reread-sources-with-new-agents` → `google-tags-as-statements` →
`talked-about-pages`. A session picking up a phase reads this brief first (the "Analysis" and "Open decisions"
sections are the contract), then its own task. This brief stays in `in-progress/` until the last phase closes;
each phase folds its learnings into the skill when it closes, and the brief's "Analysis" is rewritten into the
skill at the end.

## The entity changes, plainly (ELI5)

Think of three layers: **where something was said** (a review, a post, a Google tag), **what was said** (one
claim), and **what it is about** (a topic). Today the middle layer is missing and the last layer is split in two.

| Today | After | What changes for it |
|---|---|---|
| `FoodTopic` (table `food_topics`): pizza, Turkish, vegan, café… | `Topic` (table `topics`) | Renamed. Gains rows of kind `aspect` (Waiting, Price, Taste, Friendliness… the nine groups as roots, the themes as children) and a `hint` column. Loses `aliases` to the term table. Same tree, one more kind. |
| `FEEDBACK_TOPICS` + `FEEDBACK_THEMES` (lists in code) | rows in `topics` | Deleted from code. An admin edits them like any topic. The agents read the legend from the table. |
| `DishName` (table `dish_names`): one dish wording → one food topic | `Term` (table `terms`) + `TermTopic` (table `term_topics`) | Generalised. Every wording ever seen is a term, as written ("Pommes", "waiting time", "cash only", "Spaghetti Vongole"), linked to **zero, one or many** topics (`term_topics`), decided by a name match, the term agent or an admin, changeable any time. |
| `FoodTopic.aliases` (jsonb), `FoodTopicIgnoredWord` | admin-decided terms | Folded into `terms` (an ignored word is a term decided as "none"). |
| `FeedbackPoint` (table `feedback_points`): one praise or complaint note with its theme, dish, number | `Statement` (table `statements`) + `StatementTopic` (table `statement_topics`) | Replaced. A statement is one claim (text, sentiment incl. neutral, number, a noticed-change flag), from one source (review, post, comment, press, Google tag: one column each), about a subject (the source's place, the city, or none). Its parts are rows: one per topic it is about (an aspect from the agent, foods from its term), each knowing the term it came from. |
| `MentionFoodTopic`, `MenuItemFoodTopic`, `VenueFoodTopic`, `Hashtag.foodTopic` | `MentionTopic`, `MenuItemTopic`, `VenueTopic`, `Hashtag.topic` | Renamed only. Same job (what a mention, a menu item, a venue, a hashtag is about by matching text), but the matching index now includes every decided term, so they learn every term decision for free. |
| `Review`, `Mention`, `SocialPost`, `WebMention`, `PostComment`, `Venue` | unchanged | Their `feedback` collections become `statements`; nothing else moves. Brand, venue and match state stay where they are: a statement's subject is joined through them, never copied. |
| `ReviewAnalysisAgent`, `MentionExtractionAgent` (split + judge in one call) | agents **A** (split: places and claims per source kind) | The big prompts shrink to "what is said, about whom". |
| `FeedbackThemeAgent`, `FeedbackDishAgent`, `FeedbackAmountAgent` (three nightly passes) | agent **B** (parts of a claim: sentiment, aspect, food wording, number) | One pass, run right after A and as one nightly backlog; re-runnable over stored claims without re-reading a source. |
| `DishTopicAgent` (dish names → food topics) | agent **C** (any wording → topics, a list) | One pass, one nightly backlog. |

What does **not** change: venues, brands, matching, corrections, reports' data shape, the portal's series. The
readers of feedback rows (about 35 places, listed under "Readers and writers") change their query, not their
meaning: counts stay "sources, not points", own posts stay decided at read time.

## Why (the user's goal, in their words)

> I should be able to query "who talked about X" where "Y, Z, A, D, SD" etc. should also be able to
> arbitrarily map to X, where we decide that by an LLM, then we can break EVERYTHING to their atoms.
> Google's topic should be inserted as "Y, Z, A, D, SD" or whatever, should still be able to be
> mapped to "X", and then I can query "Which brand's Google review tags mention this".

> I think we are missing a middle ground to generalise analysis by LLM, an "entity" like "Feedback"
> or "Statement", and then an LLM comes and breaks that down to atoms like which venue, what topic,
> what unit, etc.

Today the analysis can't answer "who talked about X" across every source, because:
- what a source says is stored as a feedback note fused to one venue/brand, always praise or complaint;
- the "X" lives in two separate vocabularies (food topics in a table, topics/themes in code) and only
  dish wording is mapped to a concept by an LLM (`DishName`); themes are picked directly at extraction;
- Google's "people often mention" tags (`Venue.details.reviewsTags`) are not a source of anything —
  they sit as JSON on the venue and link to nothing (except the read-only stopgap below);
- a claim with no venue ("Berlin has increasing prices") is dropped, and a neutral fact
  ("fries for 10 euros", no judgement) is not kept.

## Scenarios the model must hold (the user's examples)

Sources about the brand "Batu Burger" (a venue / brand):

1. A Google review: "I waited darn 50 minutes".
2. A Google review: "Damn good Doner, but man they were for 15 euros".
3. A post about Batu Burger praising "Damn good fries for 10 euros".
4. A blog article: "Berlin has increasing prices in the last years", "Batu Burger has the best fries",
   "It also has the best waiters".
5. Google's tags on Batu Burger's place page: "waiting time" (×4,098), "Dürüm" (×551), "friendly
   staff", "cash only", "juicy burgers", … (dev Berlin: 1,011 distinct tags over the venues with details;
   most common: outdoor seating, fresh ingredients, atmosphere, portions, friendly staff, cash only,
   veggie burger, waiting time, cheese fries, vegan options, meat counter, half chicken, garlic sauce,
   hand pulled noodles, cookies, smash burger, matcha, cheesecake, biang biang noodles, cleanliness,
   kid-friendly, baklava, cocktails, spicy food, hummus, noise level, sommelier, dumplings, dürüm, milkshake).

What they must break down into (the user's sketch, kept as given; exact field names are for the design):

- The obvious: Batu Burger as brand and venue; Google reviews; posts; creators; "fries" and "doner" as food.
- "Waiting" as an umbrella concept.
- "Waited", 50, minutes, sentiment bad — an atom.
- "Doner" as a food category.
- "Damn good" + "Doner" + "Batu Burger" — an atom (praise, taste, the dish, the brand).
- "Cost", amount 15, unit money, sentiment bad — an atom (about the doner, at Batu Burger).
- "Fries" as a food category.
- "Cost", amount 10, unit money, sentiment neutral — an atom (kept even with no judgement).
- "Increasing price", subject city Berlin — an atom with a city as its subject, no venue.
- "Waiter", brand Batu Burger — an atom (service is fine as its concept; "waiter" survives as the term).
- Google's "waiting time" on Batu Burger's venue → maps to Waiting; "which brands' Google tags mention
  Waiting" is a query.

## Decisions already made (2026-10-29)

- **Statement size: one claim each.** "Damn good doner, but 15 euros" is two statements: "good doner" and
  "15 euro doner". The LLM splits a text into claims; each claim has its own atoms.
- **Neutral facts are kept** (gap 1): an atom's sentiment may be neutral; "fries for 10 euros" is stored.
- **A statement's subject may be a city, or nothing** (gap 2): the LLM extracts city-wide claims too; blogs
  and news about the city are expected to fill these later.
- **Money stays `money`** (gap 3): no currency or euro-specific field.
- **Roles stay words** (gap 4): "waiter" maps to service; no staff-role model.
- **No separate table just for Google tags**, and **Google's words never go into food topics or into dish
  names as they are named today** ("dish names" holding "waiting time" or "cash only" is not acceptable).
  Google's tags become statements of their venue (with Google's count) whose terms map to concepts like
  any other wording.
- **Themes and food topics being separate vocabularies is an issue in itself** (the user: "I believe an
  issue in itself in general anyway"). Direction: one concept tree. To confirm in the design (below).

The first draft that stood here was superseded by the decisions; the model is the "Analysis" section below

Checked against the code and the dev database (2026-10-29), what the table above left out:

- **Volumes (dev Berlin)**: 40,872 `feedback_points` — 33,042 points (21,097 positive, 5,031 negative),
  7,462 dish-only rows (the rows whose sentiment is `neutral` (1,841) or `mixed` (54) are these: a listed
  dish's own sentiment), 368 noticed changes; **0 unfiled** (every point has a theme, so moving themes into
  a table needs no LLM); 17,805 rows name a dish, 12,855 of them linked to a food topic; 299 state minutes,
  678 money. `dish_names`: 7,205 (125 by name match, 7,080 by the agent, 4,211 of those linked; no admin
  decision yet). Sources: 16,192 Google reviews (9,646 written, 7,173 analysed; no TripAdvisor row on dev),
  5,146 posts (3,697 TikTok, 1,177 Instagram, 272 blog articles; all analysed), 6,302 mentions (4,663 with a
  brand), 33 `web_mentions`, 1,446 `post_comments` (all analysed), 166 venues with `reviewsTags` (1,620 tag
  rows, 1,011 distinct titles). Topics: 138 (62 cuisine, 47 dish, 8 drink, 5 diet, 13 format, 3 meal);
  hashtags 6,523 (166 → a food topic, 391 → a brand). No food topic key clashes with the nine feedback topic
  keys.
- **"Term → concept" exists in six shapes, not one.** Besides `DishName`: `FoodTopic.aliases` (jsonb on the
  topic: an admin's wordings, whole-name matched by `FoodTopicIndex`); `Hashtag.foodTopic` (pure `exact`
  match of the tag); `MentionFoodTopic` (a mention's `cuisine` text, else its venue's Google category, through
  `resolve`: word-run matching); `MenuItemFoodTopic` (a delivery menu item's name, else its section, through
  `resolveMenuItem`; the user, 2026-10-29: "things in the menu should also link to topics", i.e. by a decided
  term, not only by pure matching); `VenueFoodTopic` (computed from categories, posts, menus, review tags);
  `FoodTopicIgnoredWord` (an unmapped word dismissed); and the read-only `reviewTagTopic` stopgap. All but
  `DishName` are pure matching; only dish names get an LLM decision, and that decision reaches nothing else
  (a menu's "Spaghetti Vongole" is not pasta although the dish name is).
- **A third vocabulary**: `PostCommentQuestionTopicEnum` (location, price, hours, menu, dietary, payment,
  reservation, other) is what a comment's question is about — aspects again, in another enum.
- **Agents' output schemas are static**: `BaseMastraAgent` passes `config.outputSchema` to every `generate`;
  a theme list built from the table at call time means `z.string()` + a code check against the index (how
  `DishTopicAgent` already takes a topic key), not a per-call `z.enum`.
- **`FeedbackPoint.topic` is derived data**: always the theme's topic (`realign` keeps it so); readers use it
  for grouping (report praise/complaint counts, portal review filter, facets) and the passes for selection
  (`f.topic in ('food','drinks')`, `AMOUNT_UNITS[topic]`).
- **Catch-all themes** (`food_other`, …, `other`, 9 of the 60) exist only because a point needs a leaf; readers
  skip them (`isCatchAll`). In a tree a point filed at the parent is the same thing.
- **Readers that need theme labels without a session**: the report view (`/r/<token>`, the print page) gets
  labels inside `ReportData` already (`ReportChangeTheme.label`, `"price / price increase"` prompt keys); the
  admin and portal pages read `FEEDBACK_THEME_LABELS` from the frontend's copy of the vocabulary.

How today's model handles the scenarios:
- 1, 2, 3 and the venue parts of 4: held as notes (theme wait_for_food + 50 minutes; taste + dish Doner →
  Döner & kebab; value_for_money / price_level + 15 money; friendliness for "best waiters").
- "fries for 10 euros" with no judgement: lost (a note is praise or complaint).
- "Berlin has increasing prices": lost (no venue → no mention → no note).
- Google tags: not analysed at all.
- "who talked about X" works per vocabulary only: the theme page (`/admin/trends/themes/[theme]`, all
  sentences of a theme across reviews and posts) and the food topic page (`/admin/trends/[key]`); not
  across both, not for Google tags, not for cities.

## Gaps to close

1. Neutral facts: atoms with neutral sentiment (and the extraction asked to keep them).
2. Subjects beyond the source's venue: city (and none); city-wide claims extracted from blogs and news.
3. A statement layer between the source and its atoms (one claim each).
4. One concept tree for food and aspects (themes out of code), editable in the admin.
5. A general term → concept table (dish names generalised), LLM-decided, admin-correctable.
6. Google's tags as statements of their venue, with their count, through the same term mapping.
7. Queries and pages over the new layers ("who talked about X", "which brands' Google tags mention X").

## Analysis (2026-10-29): the consolidated model

Three tables replace today's two tables, two code vocabularies and six link shapes. Each is one idea.

### 1. `Topic` — one tree (replaces `FoodTopic` + `FEEDBACK_TOPICS` + `FEEDBACK_THEMES`)

- Table `topics`, entity `Topic`, kinds `cuisine | dish | drink | diet | format | meal | aspect`. The nine
  feedback topics become aspect roots (`food`, `drinks`, `service`, `vibe`, `price`, `payment`, `wait`,
  `cleanliness`, `other`), the themes their children, keys kept (`wait_for_food` stays `wait_for_food`), so
  stored `theme` text keys join the table as they are. The nine `_other` catch-alls are not rows: a point
  "about price, nothing finer" is filed at `price` itself (migration: `price_other` → `price`; `other` is the
  root `other`); "catch-all" becomes "filed at a root".
- New column `hint` (text, nullable): what the aspect covers, told to the agents (today's `hint`); food topics
  may get one later. An aspect's key is a snake_case code key, not a `normalizeCuisine` key; an admin creates,
  relabels, re-parents and rehints aspects in the topic dialog like any topic. Deleting an aspect moves its
  statements to its parent (counts stay whole, no LLM); an admin who wants them re-filed runs the themes
  backfill on the parent.
- The agents get the legend from the table per call (`TopicService.index().aspectLegend()`), answer the key as
  `z.string()`, and the code checks it against the index (an unknown key = unfiled, the `feedback-themes`
  backlog files it, as today). `FeedbackPoint.topic` (the root) stays derived until phase 3 drops it.
- Readers lose `FEEDBACK_THEMES`: labels, roots, legend and "is a root" come from the index (backend) or the
  API (frontend: a topic list store replaces `FEEDBACK_THEME_LABELS`; reports already carry labels in
  `ReportData`).
- Rename: `FoodTopic` → `Topic`, `food_topics` → `topics`, `*food_topic_id` → `*topic_id`, `FoodTopicService` →
  `TopicService`, `FoodTopicIndex` → `TopicIndex`, module `food-topics` → `topics`. 131 files name it. One
  mechanical commit before the aspect rows go in, so "topic" means one thing everywhere (the admin already
  calls `/admin/trends/[key]` a topic's page; the nine feedback "topics" become topics too, so the word stops
  being ambiguous rather than more so).

### 2. `Term` — every wording ever seen, kept as said, linked to topics like a graph (replaces `DishName`, `aliases`, `FoodTopicIgnoredWord`)

- Table `terms`, entity `Term`: `key` (unique: `dishKey`-style normalisation — accents and punctuation folded,
  plurals folded per word, another script kept), `name` (the spelling seen most, in the source's language:
  "Pommes" stays "Pommes"), `decidedBy` match | ai | admin | null (waiting), `decidedAt`, `suggestion` (the
  agent's label for a missing topic). Its links are rows: **`term_topics`** (term × topic, `rank`): one wording
  maps to one or many topics ("fries" → Fries; "vegan döner" → Döner & kebab, Vegan; "Turkish breakfast" →
  Turkish, Breakfast; "waiting time" → Waiting; "cash only" → Cash only; "meat" → none), and a link can be
  added, changed or removed at any time without re-reading a source (the user: "like a graph"). Decided by a
  name match (the wording is a topic's key or label as a whole), else by agent C (every kind of topic
  offered, a list answered, a suggestion for a missing topic), else by an admin, whose links nothing changes.
- **A wording is never dropped**: every wording the agents answer is upserted as a term, linked or not (an
  "atom" waiting for its links). Analysis runs on the topic layer ("how much of the posts talk about Pasta")
  while a wording stays countable on its own ("how much talk about *vegan pasta*").
- **Decided terms enter the index** (`TopicIndex` is built from topics + decided terms): so every pure matcher
  that already exists — `resolve` for mention cuisines and Google categories, `resolveMenuItem` for menu items
  and sections, `exact` for hashtags, the Google tags' lookup — gets every agent and admin decision for free
  through the relink job it already runs after; "Spaghetti Vongole" on a Wolt menu becomes pasta the night the
  wording is decided. This is what makes "things in the menu link to topics" without a second mechanism.
  `FoodTopic.aliases` (jsonb) become `admin` terms; the unrecognised-words tab's "Add to aliases" creates one;
  `FoodTopicIgnoredWord` becomes a term decided as none by an admin. Hashtags keep their row (posts, brand,
  source links) and keep reading `exact`.
- The `feedback-dish-names` backlog becomes `terms` (any pending term, most used first); `sync` reads the
  wordings from the statements' parts and from the venues' `reviewsTags`.
- A term decides a *wording*; a claim's *aspect* is decided by agent B with the claim in view (§4), since
  "waited", "took forever", "slow", "Wartezeit" would otherwise be hundreds of terms each waiting a night for a
  worse, context-free decision.

### 3. `Statement` + `StatementTopic` — one claim, and the parts it is about (replaces `FeedbackPoint`)

- Table `statements`, entity `Statement`, module `statements` (replacing `feedback`): one row per claim.
  **Table `statement_topics`** (the atoms / parts): statement × topic × `role` aspect | food, with the `term`
  the part came from (null for an aspect agent B chose directly) and a `rank`. A claim about several things
  has several parts ("great vegan döner": Döner & kebab and Vegan from the term "vegan döner", taste from
  B). "Who talked about X" = parts whose topic is X or a descendant; "vegan pasta" = parts with that term, or
  a statement with both Vegan and Pasta.
- **Source** (where it was said): `sourceKind` review | post | comment | press | google_tag, with one FK each
  (`review`, `post` (+ `mention` when the claim is about a place the post names), `comment` (+ `mention`),
  `webMention`, and for a Google tag the `venue` whose details carry it), check constraint "exactly one"; a new
  source is a new kind and column, never a new shape. `city` and `month` copied from the source (never change;
  `month` null for an all-time source such as a Google tag), `saidAt` (the review's date, the post's, the
  article's, the details scrape's), `count` (Google's count; 1 otherwise).
- **Subject** (who it is about): `subject` place | city | none. For `place` the subject is **joined through the
  source, never copied**: a review's venue, a post's or comment's `mention` (brand always, venue when the
  branch is known; a re-match or a merge moves every statement with no row rewritten, as today), a press
  article's venue, a Google tag's venue. `city` is the statement's own `city` ("Berlin has increasing
  prices" from a blog: `post` set, `mention` null, `subject` city).
- **On the row**: `text` (the claim, an English rendering, as today's `detail`; null on a dish-only statement,
  Google's title on a tag), `sentiment` positive | negative | neutral | mixed | null (not judged: a Google
  tag), `change` (a noticed change since an earlier visit), `amount` + `unit` minutes | money, `partsAt` (when
  B read it: a claim without parts is B's backlog).
- A listed dish no claim names stays a dish-only statement (text null, the dish's sentiment, one food part), so
  dish counts don't move. Migration without an LLM: point → statement (`detail` → `text`; theme → an aspect
  part; `dish` → a term by key and a food part per topic its term links; `change` → `change`), dish-only row
  → dish-only statement; every reader moved; `feedback_points` and `dish_names` dropped.

### 4. Three specialised agents (replaces the two extraction agents and the four nightly passes)

- **A split**, one call per source, one agent per source kind: for a post or article the places it names
  (today's mention fields: name, hint, cuisine, city, visited, new opening, promotional) and the claims, each
  with its subject (a named place, the city, none); for a review the claims (the subject is its venue). A
  claim is one thing said; neutral facts are kept ("fries for 10 euros"); a noticed change is a claim flagged
  `change`.
- **B parts**, one call per ~100 claims from any source (mini): sentiment, the aspect key (checked against the
  index; unknown = left for the next run), the food wording(s) as the source spells them (never translated),
  the number and its unit, the change flag. Runs in the same pipeline step right after A, and as the nightly
  backlog `statement-parts` for any claim without `partsAt` (a prompt change re-reads every stored claim
  without touching a source: cheap, nothing lost). Replaces `feedback-themes`, `feedback-dish-links`,
  `feedback-amounts`.
- **C terms**, one call per ~100 pending wordings (mini): the topics each wording is (a list), or a
  suggestion. The nightly backlog `terms`. Replaces `feedback-dish-names`.
- All three carry the POC rule: never drop a claim or a wording because no category fits.

### Future sources this holds

- **Comments** (`PostComment`, 1,446 on dev): "overrated", "they changed the recipe" are statements by a
  commenter about the post's venue (`comment` + `mention`); a question is a statement with an aspect
  (`PostCommentQuestionTopicEnum`'s price / hours / payment / reservation / menu / location map onto aspect
  topics, one vocabulary less). Not in this task; the model has the column.
- **Press** (`WebMention`): statements from the snippet or a fetched article (`webMention`, subject place or
  city). A press article read in full is the same shape as a blog article (a `SocialPost` of platform `blog`);
  whether press should become posts is a later decision.
- **Google tags**: phase 5 as planned, through terms.
- **TripAdvisor, Yelp, Reddit, Instagram comments, owner replies**: reviews, posts, comments — no new shape.
  Delivery platform ratings are numbers, not claims.

### What this deletes

`FEEDBACK_TOPICS`, `FEEDBACK_THEMES` and every helper on them, `FeedbackTheme` / `FeedbackTopic` types,
`FEEDBACK_THEME_LABELS` (frontend), `DishName` + `DishNameService` (folded into `TermService`), `DishTopics` /
`reviewTagTopic`, `FoodTopic.aliases` and `FoodTopicIgnoredWord` (terms), `FeedbackPoint` + `FeedbackService`
(statements), the four feedback backlogs and their agents (`FeedbackThemeAgent`, `FeedbackDishAgent`,
`FeedbackAmountAgent`, `DishTopicAgent` → agents B and C), the praise / complaints / dishes / changes lists in
`ReviewAnalysisAgent` and `MentionExtractionAgent` (→ claims from agent A), the theme page as a separate route
(phase 6: a topic's page).

## Open decisions (for the user)

Each with the recommendation; the answer is recorded under it.

1. **One topic tree** — themes and the nine topics into the same table as food topics (one tree), or two
   tables under one "concept" reference? Recommended: one table (Analysis §1), catch-alls folded into the
   roots, a deleted aspect's statements moved to its parent.
   *Answer (2026-10-29):* **one table.** (Catch-alls folded into the roots and a deleted aspect's statements
   moved to its parent: taken as the implementation's call, not asked.)
2. **Naming and the rename** — "Topic" for all (`FoodTopic` → `Topic`, 131 files, one mechanical commit first)
   vs adding kind `aspect` to `food_topics` and leaving the name. Recommended: rename now; the word
   "topic" then means one thing everywhere, and a later rename touches everything twice.
   *Answer (2026-10-29):* **"Topic" for all, rename now** (first commit of phase 1).
3. **Statement and atoms** — one `statements` table with the atoms as columns and two topic slots
   (`aspect`, `object`), or a separate atoms table / a list of topics per claim. Recommended: one table, two
   slots (Analysis §3).
   *Answer (2026-10-29):* **claim row plus parts rows** — `statements` (text, sentiment, change, amount, unit,
   source, subject, date) + `statement_topics` (statement × topic × role aspect | food, the term it came
   from). Chosen on the example "Waited for the 15 Euro döner for 15 minutes, but it was totally worth it"
   (three claims, six parts) and because "great vegan döner" is Vegan + Döner & kebab + taste. Consequence:
   a term may point at several topics ("vegan döner" → kebab, vegan): `term_topics`, not one `topic` column.
   Analysis §3 is to be rewritten to this shape before implementation.
4. **Terms or topics from the extraction** — the extraction decides the claim's aspect (key, checked against
   the index) and writes the object as a wording (term); bare wordings (dish names, Google tags, menu items,
   aliases) are decided once in the term table, and decided terms feed the index so every pure matcher
   learns them. Alternative: the extraction writes an aspect wording too and the term table decides it.
   Recommended: the former (Analysis §2, last bullet).
   *Answer (2026-10-29):* **three specialised agents** (the user: "specialised agents — blogs → sentences →
   statements → something"): **A split** (one call per source, knows the source kind: the places it names
   with today's mention fields, and the claims, each with its subject: a named place, the city, or none);
   **B parts** (one call per ~100 claims from any source: sentiment, aspect key, food wording(s), number and
   unit, noticed-change flag); **C terms** (one call per ~100 wordings, once per wording ever: wording →
   topics, every kind). A new source needs only its A; B and C are shared; B re-reads stored claims on a
   prompt change without extracting the source again (so B replaces the four nightly backlogs: themes, dish
   links, amounts → B; dish names → C). B runs in the same step right after A (a claim is whole within
   minutes) and as a backlog for any claim without parts.
5. **Terms feed the index** — aliases and ignored words become terms, decided terms enter `TopicIndex`
   (menu items, mention cuisines, categories, hashtags and Google tags all read them). Recommended: yes, in
   phase 2; hashtags keep their row.
   *Answer (2026-10-29):* **yes, everywhere.** The user's framing: every wording ever seen is kept as said (an
   "atom"), even one nothing links yet; a later layer links it to one or many topics (Pasta; Vegan and
   Pasta for "vegan pasta"), and the analysis runs on that layer ("how much of the posts talk about Pasta")
   while a wording stays countable on its own ("how much talk about *vegan pasta*": the term's statements,
   or statements whose parts hold both Vegan and Pasta). Consequence: `statement_topics` keeps the term each
   part came from; the term agent answers a list of topics per wording; a wording's links are changeable
   at any time and the relink job spreads them to menu items, cuisines, categories, hashtags and Google
   tags.
6. **Statement text language** — English phrase only (as today), the source's own words, or both.
   Recommended: English only; the source row holds the original text.
   *Answer (2026-10-29):* **the claim's text is an English rendering; the wording (term) is kept as written
   in the source's language.** A German post saying "Pommes" gives the term "Pommes" (never translated to
   "fries"), which links to Fries, and later may also link to another topic ("Fried"): wordings map to
   topics like a graph (many to many, `term_topics`), so a wording can be re-mapped or mapped further
   later. Consequence for agent B: the food wording is answered as the source spells it, never translated
   or normalised; the term key folds only accents, punctuation and plurals. Topics keep one parent each
   (a tree) unless a case needs more.
7. **A Google tag's sentiment** — `neutral`, or null ("not judged"). Recommended: null; neutral means "stated
   without judgement" (a price), a tag is a frequency.
   *Answer (2026-10-29):* the question was too technical for the user; their instinct: "a table of Google's
   'people often mention' linking to the topics". That is what a statement of source kind `google_tag` is

   Google tags. Taken as the implementation's call: **sentiment null** ("not judged") on a tag statement.

   *Answer (2026-10-29):* **re-read, for sure.** The user's framing, now a rule in the root `CLAUDE.md`: this
