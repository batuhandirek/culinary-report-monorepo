# Claims extraction fixes

**Needs `claims-eval-set` done**: every change here is scored before and after on the reference set, and kept only when
its field gets better without another getting worse beyond the measured noise.

## Goal
Agent A writes claims that stand on their own and say something; agent B files them under an aspect that fits, with a
vocabulary that covers what people actually say; a post's claim about the place it shows is about that place.

## The fixes (each its own scored change, in this order)
1. **Pronouns resolved (A, reviews and posts):** a claim names what it is about ("the Gemüse kebab is incredibly
   delicious", never "it's incredibly delicious"; "the dürüm and the box were delicious", never "both were"), so
   "what is said about the Gemüse kebab" finds it and B gets its food wording.
2. **Fragments dropped (A):** a claim says something about food, drink, a place, its prices, service, atmosphere or the
   city's food scene; "with friends", "now I understand why", "Sabaya brunch preparations" are none. Kept as said when
   it is one ("came with friends on a Tuesday" is a neutral fact about the visit only if it carries the visit).
3. **Missing aspects (rows, the admin topic dialog or a seed migration; B's legend reads them):** an overall verdict
   ("highly recommend", "avoid at all costs", "best kebab in Berlin"), loyalty and return visits ("we come back every
   time"), hype and reputation ("overrated", "the rumours were true"), events and anniversaries ("30 years, two-day
   anniversary"), deals (with the task `deal-announcements`). Check against the clusters of `claim-embeddings` ("Missing
   aspects" tab) before choosing names; the hints say what each is not (`location` is where a place is, never "the best
   in Berlin"; "worth the wait" is `wait`, not `value_for_money`).
4. **The subject of a post's claims (A):** in a post about one place (or a venue's own post), a claim about a dish or
   a drink there is about that place, not `none`; `none` is food or drink in general ("the burger uses fried onions" as
   a fact of cooking, not of that kitchen).
5. Whatever else the baseline report shows as the largest miss.
