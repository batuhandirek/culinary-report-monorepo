# Research: the city's food events, and posts linked to them

## Goal
A written decision on (1) where we learn of the city's food events from, (2) how one is stored (consolidated with
what exists), (3) how a post or claim is linked to the event it talks about, and (4) what reports and public pages do
with them. The output is this file's answer section plus the implementation tasks it names.

## Why
Posts announce and report on events all the time, and today they leave no trace but a claim of kind `event`. Example
(Instagram, a Peruvian sandwich stall, 2026-11):

> Starting 11 November, you'll find us every Wednesday at Markt Rixdorf in Neukölln! 🇵🇪 … 📍 Markt Rixdorf,
> Neukölln 📅 Every Wednesday, starting 11 November ⏰ 11am - 6pm

What the pipeline does with it now: agent A lists the stall (Cumbia) as a place; Markt Rixdorf is not a place (its
rules: "a market day … is never a place"); the schedule becomes a claim of kind `event` with no date, time,
recurrence or location as data. So we cannot answer "what is on this week", "which stalls are at Markt Rixdorf",
"did the venue's buzz rise around its pop-up", or show a venue its events in its report.

## Questions (to answer in this file)
1. **What counts as an event for us**, and which kinds matter: one-off (food festival, pop-up, tasting, anniversary
   party), recurring (a weekly market, a stall's regular spot and hours, a monthly supper club), a venue's own (a
   wine evening, a guest chef). Where is the line to a `deal` (a time-limited offer) and to a place's opening hours?
2. **Sources to learn of them from**, ranked by coverage, cost and effort, Berlin first:
   - the posts themselves (announcements like the example: free, already scraped);
   - city listings and magazines (berlin.de, visitBerlin, tip Berlin, Mit Vergnügen, Berlin Food Stories event
     posts), market operators' sites (Markt Rixdorf, Markthalle Neun's Street Food Thursday, Neuer Markt …);
   - event platforms (Eventbrite, Meetup, Resident Advisor for food parties), Facebook / Instagram events (hard to
     scrape); what Apify actors exist and what they cost on the Free plan (`apify-free-plan-only`).
3. **Storage, consolidating first** (the POC rule: no new entity when one fits): extend `CityEvent` (`cities`
   module: kind `school_holiday` | `event`, name, start and end date, entered by an admin today) with what a food
   event needs (a location (a venue, a market as a named location, or free text), hours, a recurrence (weekly on a
   day, from a date), the venues or brands taking part, its source), or a separate shape if `CityEvent`'s meaning
   (things that move traffic city-wide) does not stretch. Keep the event's own words as said (atoms), link later.
4. **Reading events out of posts**: does agent A give event fields (date, time, recurrence, where, who) on a claim of
   kind `event`, or does a specialist read only the `event` / `deal` / `new_item` claims (one claim a call, the
   `one-item-per-llm-call` rule)? Relative dates ("starting next Wednesday") need the post's date.
5. **Linking**: a post or claim to an event (by name, date window and place), many posts to one event (dedupe),
   an event to the venues and brands in it; a market (Markt Rixdorf) as a location that is not a venue, so the
   rule "a market day is never a place" stays and the stall stays the place.
6. **Uses**, to size the work: the venue's report (its events, and buzz around them: `correlations`), the city's
   month and the front page ("food events this week"), trends (events per district), the admin's Explore page.
7. **Eval**: which cases to add to the ingestion eval so event extraction is scored (posts announcing events,
   recurring stalls, festival round-ups).

## Scope
- In: the research and its written answers, a small sample (say 50 posts with `event` claims from the dev DB) read
  by hand or by Opus to see what share of posts carry events and what fields they give, the source survey with
  costs, and the follow-up implementation tasks.
