# List a place as "just opened" from posts only when more than one signal agrees

## Goal
An opening with the source `posts` appears in the Trends panel, the report's neighbourhood line, the "new venues" filter and the lead score's new-place bonus only when the evidence is more than one loose post: an explicit opening statement, posts from more than one author, or a second source that agrees. The date shown says what it is (the first post's date), not an opening date.

## Why
`VenueStatusChangeService.postOpenings` lists any open place under `LEAD_NEW_PLACE_MAX_REVIEWS` (300) reviews that one `isNewOpening` mention names, dated `min(p.posted_at)`. One post is enough, and it may be the place's own account.

Example from dev: Shiraz Restaurant & Bar (Oranienstraße 172) is listed as opened on 12 Sept. The listing rests on one post from its own TikTok (@shiraz.resturant.berlin), captioned "Ein neuer Foodspot in Berlin, den ihr probieren müsst!". That is promotional wording, not an opening statement. The same account's near-identical post 22 minutes later ("Noch ein Foodspot in Berlin…") was not flagged. The place already has 91 Google reviews, no scrape saw it open, and none of its reviews are stored.

The spot check in `measure-opening-flag-on-stored-posts` found about 1 flag in 5 wrong. The other errors were "six months already" and a seasonal reopening.

Posts from July to October 2026 on dev flag 51 Berlin venues, all under 300 reviews:
- 15 have flagged posts from 2 or more authors.
- 13 have one of their brand's own-account posts flagged.
- 11 rest only on their own account's posts.

## Scope
- In:
  1. **Stricter wording.** In the `isNewOpening` description, say "true only when the post says the place has opened or is opening (eröffnet, Neueröffnung, grand opening, just opened, soft opening); calling it 'new', 'neu' or a 'new spot' is not enough". Then re-flag July–October through `POST /admin/mentions/reflag-openings`. Running it again after a wording change means calling it without `analysedBefore`, or with a later one; check the route allows that.
  2. **Corroboration rule in `postOpenings`.** A venue counts when at least one of these holds:
     - (a) flagged posts from 2 or more distinct authors;
     - (b) a flagged post by an account that isn't the venue's brand's own (`ownPostSql`), together with any second signal;
     - (c) a second source agrees.

     Candidate second sources, cheapest first:
     - **The earliest stored Google review** falling within ~6 weeks of the first flagged post. Google gives no opening date, so the first review is the best stand-in. This works only where reviews are stored; a per-place review scrape for the rest is a paid Apify run, so it waits for the user's call (see Open questions).
     - **Review-count growth in `venue_snapshots`.** Low and rising fast counts as agreement. Snapshots only start on 2026-10-15, so this helps from now on, not for the past.
     - **A Google Maps scrape** that saw the place open (`VenueStatusChange`), which is already a source of its own.

     Own-account posts alone never count.
  3. **The label.** Wherever the panel and the neighbourhood line print a `posts` opening's date, write it as the first post's date ("first post 12 Sept"), not a bare date that reads as the opening day.
- Out: writing `VenueStatusChange` rows from posts. The new-branch question (`new-branch-as-opening`) is separate, but if both are picked up, do the wording edits together and run one re-flag.

## Open questions
- Should we pay for a review scrape of flagged places that have no stored reviews, to read their first review's date? It would be a small Apify run per place, within the Free plan. Assumption if unanswered: **no**. Use stored reviews and snapshots only; a place with no second signal and one outside author is not listed.
- Should one outside author with an explicit opening statement count on its own (rule 2b without a second signal)? Assumption if unanswered: **no**. Every listing needs two signals.
