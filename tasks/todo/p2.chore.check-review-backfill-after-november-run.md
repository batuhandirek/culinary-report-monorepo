# Check the review backfill on the first real monthly run

## Goal
After the first monthly run that uses the backfill (Berlin's October run on 2026-11-02), we know from the run's own figures whether the split and the $12 default budget hold. The brands new to the top 20 get reports with `previous.reviews` and, where they have 10+ reviews in the month and 3+ posts, `correlations`.

## Why
The budget arithmetic rests on September's dev numbers: $0.0006 a review, ~47 reviews a venue a month, ~6,300 for the month across 127 venues, and about 30 new venues a month at most. If the backfill run hits its $4.32 cap every month, the venues it misses wait a month each time, and their first reports still have no history. If the month run comes near its $6.48, the shares need moving.

## Scope
- In: read the `SCRAPE_REVIEWS` run result (`backfilled`, `backfillSince`, `backfillSaved`, `saved`) and both `apify_runs` rows (`google-reviews 2026-10…` and `… from 2026-04-01`): cost against cap, items, and PARTIAL or a cap stop. Open two or three of the new brands' October reports: `previous`, the patterns section. If the backfill hit its cap, decide whether to raise the step's default or the share (`REVIEW_BACKFILL_BUDGET_SHARE`), and record the arithmetic in the skill rule "Review backfill".
- Out: changing which venues count as having history; TripAdvisor.

## Acceptance
- [ ] The October `SCRAPE_REVIEWS` result and its two Apify runs are recorded in the log: cost against cap for each run.
- [ ] The new brands' October reports have `previous.reviews`, or the log says why not (for example, the cap stopped the backfill before reaching them).
- [ ] The budget or the share is changed if a run hit its cap, and the docs and the skill follow the change.

## Open questions
- None. If the backfill hits its cap, the assumption is to raise the default budget (it is a cap, not a spend) rather than lower the month's share.
