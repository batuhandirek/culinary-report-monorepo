# Decide the shape, voice and research of the monthly report (the overhaul)

## Goal
A recorded decision on (1) the document's shape, (2) the writing contract and how it is enforced in code, (3) what the research step does, (4) what moves to the portal and the appendix, with the follow-up tasks written in the order to build them.

## Why
What an owner gets today reads like a dashboard printed out. Measured on dev Berlin, September 2026 (28 report brands; median 4 posts by others and ~20 reviews a month; four brands with 100+ reviews):

- A one-place brand's full report draws about 13 sections, 6 tables or matrices, 10 tiles and ~400 printed figures (Mezame Matcha; a median brand still ~300). Everything that passes a floor is shown, whether or not it matters.
- The highlights are stored unchecked (`report.service.ts` stores `highlights[i].object` as is; only the analysis goes through `checkAnalysis`). The prompt asks for "the evidence, with numbers, short quotes and the source" on every point, "quote both values and the change", a competitor finding, a branch finding; its examples are the house style. The inputs arrive pre-sentenced as `from 80.5k to 151.9k (+71.4k, +89%)` (`summarize-report.ts` `comparison()`) and `2 of 4 posts (50%)` (`report.service.ts` `shares()`, no floor), and the number guard rewards copying them verbatim. Result, real: "you had 3 posts versus a 4.5 median and 151,898 views versus a 238,088.5 median, ahead of 8% on posts", "whatMovedAndWhy says one likely reason is…", "source: TikTok and blogs", "creators went from 1 to 6 (+5, +500%)".
- The schema forces three findings and two or three actions whatever the month, so a 4-post month gets a "weak spot" against a city median and a 0-post month gets "Reach vanished". There is no quiet month.
- The research loop barely earns its place: of 28 reports, 12 ran no agent (no observation passed a floor), 6 made no tool call, 7 made one; only DREI. (33 reviews, stars 4.8 → 4.3) got something from it (review excerpts that made the dip concrete). On a 3-post month the hypotheses paraphrase the checks, and sibling observations (posts, views, creators) make one TikTok look like three findings.
- Cross-kind comparisons make no sense to any owner: a steakhouse against the median of 20 venues of every kind, a fine-dining room's "neighbours" being a matcha bar and a dessert shop, "worth inviting" a halal-chicken creator, "rising in the city: burgers, cookies" to a tea house.
- Data gaps print as facts: Grill Royal (3,167 Google reviews all time) shows "0 reviews · 0 with text" and a headline saying guest signals were absent; the September reviews were not read.
- Two bugs feed false moves: dish spellings not folded before the `dish_share` observation ("Döner kebab went from 0% to 30%", August had "doner kebab" 168 times), and a chain's noticed changes carry no branch (Burgermeister's remodel complaints sit at Kottbusser Tor and Schlesisches Tor, ten each; the report cannot say so).

What works and should carry the new report: changes guests noticed; a complaint theme against peers (Rüyam: cash only in 20 reviews, 0 % at peers); the all-time rating benchmark ("4.3, below 79 % of Mitte"); the comments under the top post (Grill Royal: a third of 51 comments arguing it is overrated, not in the highlights at all); press (an award in tip and Berlin Food Stories, filed as "two blog posts with 0 views"); Google Maps search ranks; reply drafts against a 0 % reply rate; a chain's branch matrices at 10+ written reviews; openings on the street.

## The proposal

### 1. Document shape (fixed order, same names every month)

1. **Cover**: brand, month, district. One sentence, at most two numbers. At most three tiles chosen by rule from the data (the move, the guests, the standing: search rank, benchmark or cuisine rank), not by configuration. A quiet month says so on the cover and shows two tiles.
2. **The letter**: 150–250 words (80–120 on a quiet month), the holistic read in the second person: the one story, how the month felt, what to ignore, what we looked for and did not find. At most six figures in the whole letter.
3. **Worth your attention**: 0–4 cards, one point each, one figure as a stamp beside the text, a pointer "Appendix B, table 2". Fed by: the surviving observations with their best check, noticed changes, critical reviews with text, the questions under the top post, a search-rank move, an opening on the street, an own-account move, press, a price gap.
4. **Around you**: three short paragraphs, never empty, since it is the city's data, not the brand's: *your cuisine* (the brand's primary food topic in the city: share of posts, searches, rising dishes of that kind only, where the brand ranks among the places of that kind people posted about), *your kind of place* (the competitors and the same-kind peers as prose: who got posts, who answers reviews, what they are praised for that you are not), *your street* (the top three neighbours, openings and closures on the street and in the district, creators who covered the district and not you, same-kind only). This is the "what happens around my cuisine and my type of restaurant" section.
5. **Try this**: 1–3 actions, each tied to a card or a letter sentence, one figure at most, what to do and why.
6. **Your places** (chains only): one table, one row per branch: reviews, stars, the one theme that stood out (a word, not a count), a flag (needs a reply, moved, quiet). The letter names at most three branches (the one that moved, the best and the worst on a share at 10+ written reviews and 15+ points from the brand). No per-branch pages; a branch's tracked blocks go to the appendix only where something is tracked.
7. **Appendix**, lettered and stable: A posts · B reviews (list, bars, five stars against the rest) · C figures (the six tiles with last month, star shape, sentiment bar) · D competitors (table, matrices) · E creators (both lists) · F dishes · G places (busyness, prices, search tables, neighbourhood ranking, one block per branch) · H what we checked (observations, checks, patterns tables) · I the city (rising lists, searches, themes) · J reply drafts (for a brand without a portal member). Collapsed on screen (`<details>` with anchors the cards link to); in the PDF a divider page with its own contents and a running header per letter. Chromium prints no `target-counter`, so pointers name the letter and table, never a page.

**Portal**: anything that is a series, a queue or a picker leaves the report: patterns (the Patterns tab draws the same `correlations`), the busyness grid and weekend tables, the reply drafts, the creator lists, search-rank history, own-account followers. The report keeps one sentence or card and a pointer.

**Templates**: the narrative (cover, letter, attention, around you, try this) is in every report; a configuration chooses a lens (`focus` social | reviews | competition, a weight on card ranking) and the appendix parts. Section kinds become `COVER`, `LETTER`, `ATTENTION` (`limit`, `focus`), `AROUND` (`parts`), `ACTIONS`, `PLACES`, `APPENDIX` (`parts`, each with today's settings: posts' `platforms`, dishes' `limit`, media's `limit`, creators' `nearby`); the other kinds survive as appendix parts or card sources. `trimForSections` and `PromptScope` keep their job: the letter never cites a block the appendix leaves out. One migration rewrites stored `sections`; `ReportData` stays (the portal reads it); September is rebuilt.

### 2. Writing contract and how code enforces it

Rules (each verified against real September output):

1. One number per sentence at most, and only when it changes what the owner does. "Five people posted about you in September, and one of them was seen by about 440 thousand people."
2. Magnitudes in words; the exact figure lives in a tile or the appendix. "About twice as many people saw posts about you as in August", never `from 80.5k to 151.9k (+71.4k, +89%)`.
3. Changes are direction and size: "roughly the same", "up by half", "about three times", "half as many".
4. Below ten sources, counts in words and never a percentage: "Four people posted; three talked about the food, two about how varied the menu is."
5. One standout post is a person, not a share: "Nearly all of those views came from one TikTok."
6. Uncertainty sits in the verb, never as a label word: "The most likely story is…", "we can't tell from the data whether…".
7. Attribution is inside the sentence ("one TikTok creator called it…", "a reviewer on Google wrote…"), never "source:" and never an input key.
8. The city comparison appears only when the brand is far from the middle, in words, and only against places of its kind: "an ordinary month among Berlin's döner places".
9. Neighbours are context, never a scoreboard.
10. Guest wording beats topic keys: "the recurring complaint is the döner itself: small, dry, 'not special'", never "food / portion size in 7 of 31 (23%)".
11. Zero is absence, and a gap is a gap: "Nobody posted about you this month" / "We could not read your September reviews, so there is nothing new on what guests think", never "0 reviews" or "reach vanished".
12. Months by name; the report month is implied.
13. An action says what to do and why, not the figure again.
14. The honest flat line: "Nothing much moved this month", never a weak spot manufactured from a median.

Enforcement (the recommended option: code computes the words, the guard checks them):

- `summarize-report.ts`: `comparison()` returns `{ from, to, said }` with `said` from a magnitude table (ratio ≥ 2.5 "about N times", 1.75–2.5 "about twice", 1.35–1.75 "up by half", 1.1–1.35 "a bit more", 0.9–1.1 "about the same", mirrored down; zero before → "none in August"). `shares()` returns `{ count, said }` with `said` only at a denominator of 10+ ("more than half", "about one in four", "a handful"); below ten only the count in words. The city baseline becomes a band word from the percentile (≥ 80 "far above most", 20–80 "an ordinary month", ≤ 20 "far below most") and no raw median; neighbour and creator views go through the short form before the prompt.
- `shared/util/stated-numbers.ts`: a closed vocabulary of quantity words (`twice|double|half|third|quarter|one in \w+|times|most|nearly all|handful|about the same`) that must appear in a fed `said` string; placeholders and a free vocabulary are rejected (a letter with holes cannot read holistically; a free "twice" for 1.4× cannot be caught).
- One shared `copyLineProblem(text, rules)` for the front page (`landing-copy.ts` `lineProblem`), the analysis (`analysis-check.ts`) and the new `checkHighlights`: at most one stated number per sentence, ban `(+…)`, `→`, `source:`, "from the checks", "observation", every key name of the prompt input (taken from `Object.keys` recursively, no hand list), no decimals on a value ≥ 1,000 unless written with k or M, no `%` when the fed denominator was under 10, the brand's banned words, no causal word.
- `checkHighlights` drops a failing card and cuts a failing letter sentence; the fallback letter is code-written from the data (as `fallbackLines` does for the front page), so a report is never empty.
- The month's kind is decided in code, never by the model: `story` when an observation survives `OBSERVATION_FLOORS`, or a post has 10k+ views, or 8+ written reviews raise a theme 3+ times, or any noticed change; else `quiet`, which gets a short letter, 0–1 cards, at most one action, and the instruction to say plainly that little moved and not to compare with the median or the neighbours.
- Schema: `highlights` becomes `{ kind, title, letter, attention[] (title, text, figure { label, value, against? }, where, appendix), tryThis[] (title, text, because, confidence?) }`; `title` stays for the cover and the leave-behind. The view renders figures as stamps and drops "Moving with it: views 80.5k → 151.9k" (`ReportAnalysis.svelte`) for the sparkline it already draws; the KPI delta chips stay on tiles, never in prose.

Example letters written to the contract (from the real data):

> *Verōnika Berlin, September.* A quiet month. Four people mentioned you on TikTok and in blogs, and only one of those was a video anyone really watched: a TikTok that called you a cosy New York date-night place with sweet, attentive service. The blog mentions were lists, the kind that bring a reader now and then but no views. No Google review came in, so there is nothing new on what guests think. Nothing here asks for a change. If you want more of the kind of attention that one TikTok brought, the creators who covered Mezame Matcha and Choc-Spot nearby this month have not been to you; an invitation is the one thing worth trying.

> *Imbiss Olympische Brücke, September.* Nobody posted about you on TikTok or Instagram this month, which for a stand like yours is normal rather than a warning. Google is where your month happened: sixteen reviews, ten with words, and the friendly service and the currywurst are what people keep praising. Three reviews were unhappy, and they agree on one thing, the taste, "too sour" and "like cardboard". None has an answer from you yet. A short reply to each, and a check on the sauce and the sausages, is all this month asks.

> *Grill Royal, September (a gap month).* The month's news was Nancy Großmann: Sommelière of the year at the first CHEF:IN Awards, picked up by tip and Berlin Food Stories. Nobody on TikTok mentioned it. One TikTok from a small creator did reach a lot of people, and the comments under it turned into an argument about whether you and Borchardt are still worth it; about a third said no. You sit at 4.3 on Google, below three in four Mitte places. We could not read September's reviews, so we cannot say why yet; October's report will.

### 3. Research: narrow the loop, add briefs

- **Replace the per-report tool loop with an evidence pack and one wording call.** Code already runs the playbook; add `gatherEvidence(observation)` that pulls the two useful tool answers deterministically by kind (stars, complaint theme, review week, rating → the reviews tool at ≤ 3 stars and the theme, six excerpts; views or posts when no single post carries the month → the five most viewed posts with summaries). Then one `maxSteps 1` call words the hypotheses; `checkAnalysis` is unchanged. Cost falls from ~10k tokens and 30 s to ~6k and 10 s, and the trace stays. The tool loop stays behind "Analyse again" for an admin.
- **Floors and collapse**: skip the model when every observation group is explained by one `supports` check of the carries-the-month kind (one post, one week): the observation sentence plus the check is the card. Collapse sibling observations before rendering, as `pickInvestigated` already does for the agent. Two hypotheses at most, each with at least one evidence line. A posts or creators spike needs `to` ≥ 10 or `from` ≥ 5; a dish shift needs the dish in both months after folding spellings; `competitor_reach` and `dish_share` are off at today's volumes.
- **Drop** the `history` and `weekly_series` tools from the build (every brand has one stored report; they restate checks).
- **A research-brief registry** (`reports/briefs/<kind>.brief.ts`): one class per research type with `floor(ctx)` (why it does not apply, or null), `gather(ctx)` (services and SQL, cached per city and month across the build), `facts(evidence)` (code-written sentences, the fallback), optional `prompt(evidence)`, `check(line)`. The build runs every brief whose floor is null, stores `ReportData.briefs`, then one copy call over all briefs with a prompt (the `LandingCopyAgent` pattern, `checkCopy`-style fallback to the facts). The existing analysis becomes the `moves` brief. The highlights prompt gets briefs as pre-written paragraphs, the pattern that already works.
- Briefs ranked by owner value over cost; the data for the first six exists and needs no new actor:
  1. **Scene** ("around you"): `TrendService.month`, `TopicInsightService.detail` (posts by month, searches, related queries, menu price of the kind), `ThemeInsightService.city(..., food)` (a theme at places of the kind against the brand's own share), `VenueStatusChangeService.quarter` (openings by district and by food topic), `branches[].neighbours`, `creatorsNearby` filtered to the kind. Code only. Build first.
  2. **Your dish in the city**: `DishSearchService.forVenue` (Google Maps top ten by dish and district; moves from the second snapshot), `MenuTopicService.pricePosition`, the topic's search line and related queries. Code only.
  3. **What guests say changed**: `reviews.changeThemes` as a paragraph instead of a table, with the branch for a chain (needs the branch on change rows).
  4. **Waiting at your place against the city**: `FeedbackService.waitComplaints` and `FeedbackPoint.amount` (299 minute rows, typical stated wait ~40 min); needs a per-brand cut. Code only.
  5. **A competitor's month in one paragraph**: `ReportCompetitor` as a template.
  6. **Review language mix as a visitor proxy**: `Review.language` is stored and unused (September: de 1,644, en 1,247, pl 200; 2,332 null, so a floor on the known share).
  7. **Who your guests compare you with**: not stored; a `comparedWith` field in review extraction (a mini-model backlog) once volumes grow.
  8. **Same month last year**: impossible for a year except a topic's 12-month search line and the quarterly `prevYearLevel`; say only "searches for X are usually lower in October".
  9. **Best posting hours in your category**: computable from own-account posts but confounded by count age; skip for now.

### 4. Fixes whatever is decided

- Fold dish spellings (`dishKey`, the food topic) before the `dish_share` observation and the dish table.
- Put the branch on a chain's noticed changes and critical reviews (`reviews.list` and `changes` carry none).
- "Not read" is never "0": a brand with all-time reviews and no review scraped in the month shows the gap, on the cover and in the prompt.
- Same-kind filters everywhere a comparison is made: the city baseline, neighbours, worth-inviting creators, rising dishes in the city block, openings nearby (same street or same food family, else one line).
- Floors in the view: a delta needs 10 posts or 20 reviews; competitor matrices need two rivals at 20+ written; the competitor table is hidden when no rival is tracked (one sentence instead); critical reviews need 15+ words; dishes 3+ at 10+ mentions; complaint times a cell of 10+ reviews at twice its usual share; the weather line only when the month is unusual.

## The benchmark: Nhas City Chicken, September 2026 (the user's call: this report must be great)

What the month was, from the stored data (report `fd815eac…`, three Berlin branches, 250 reviews, 6 posts by others, 1.2M views):
- A hype wave: the brand's own TikTok (403k followers) posted a 990k-view video on 11 Sept; @navito_halal (493k followers) 627k views on the 23rd, a creator praising the owner; @kemal_ueres (606k) 346k views on the 28th, a business video on how much the queue earns and how to serve faster; the Cologne opening the same week with five-hour queues, seven press features (Bild, RP Online, TAG24, Mit Vergnügen Köln, food-service.de) and a Tagesschau passing mention.
- A risk nobody in the report names: a 170k-view TikTok on 28 Sept and a DerWesten feature say Berlin prosecutors are investigating the owner. Under the top post, comments calling it overpriced, overrated, hype.
- Early warnings in the reviews, at 4.8 stars with 1 of 250 answered: "you're not what you used to be, €16.90 for that", "quality declined", a cluster of one-star delivery orders (Uber Eats, drinks missing, bags open, nobody answers the phone, "manager refused a refund and blamed the driver"), Sonnenallee guests saying it is pricier and the portions smaller than the other branches.
- What followed: October's 256 reviews average 3.9 stars with 55 at one or two stars, against 7 in September. The September report was the moment to say "the mood under the hype is turning".
- Standing: first on Google Maps for "fried chicken Mitte", not in the top ten for "chicken Mitte", where CityChicken "the original since 1996" (the namesake, a competitor on the list) sits above; chicken was the most posted-about kind of food in Berlin this month, and Nhas is why.
- Data gaps the report shows as facts or hides: `ownAccounts` null (no snapshot closed the month, so 990k own views are invisible to the prompt); a fourth Berlin branch (Karl-Marx-Platz 3, 210 reviews, found 25 Oct) and the Cologne branch (no city) are outside the report; reviews carry no branch, so the branch comparison guests make themselves cannot be said; `creatorsNearby[].covered` is null in the stored data.

What the report said instead: "Hype brought reach, price blocked stars", "price was criticised in 9 of 21 written reviews (43%), led by price level in 8 of 21 (38%)", "Posts by others jumped from 1 to 6", "whatMovedAndWhy says…", a competitor finding against rivals with 7 written reviews, and an action to "check what happened after 23 September". The analysis investigated "views +503%" with a "visit spike" hypothesis and one tool call; the allegation, the press wave, the own account and the delivery failures never reached it, because none of them is a metric and the observations only know metrics.

Lesson for the strategy: the model is asked to summarise metrics with a checklist of findings; it should be asked to read the month. The research that pays here is reading text (the top posts and their comments, the press, the low-star and "changed" reviews, each branch's own complaints), not investigating a percentage. The user's call (2026-10-28): defer "voice enforced in code"; trust the model's language, keep at most the existing "a number must be one it was given" check.

## Options

1. **The full shape** (letter, cards, around you, try this, lettered appendix; narrative fixed in every template; portal takes the series and queues), built in the slices below.
2. **Voice only**: keep the sections, rewrite the prompt and inputs, add `checkHighlights` and the quiet month. Cheapest; the ~400 figures stay.
3. **Voice and floors**: option 2 plus the same-kind filters, the floors and the fixes in §4; the section order stays.

## Assumption if unanswered
Option 1, built as slices 1 → 6 below, each its own task, so the voice lands first and the shape follows.

## Suggested slices (each a follow-up task)

2. `report-floors-and-same-kind`: §4 fixes, the view floors, same-kind filters, the cover tiles by rule.

4. `report-analysis-evidence-pack`: the pack, one wording call, sibling collapse, the raised floors, tools behind "Analyse again".
5. `report-briefs-scene-and-dish`: the registry, the scene brief and the dish brief; then changes, wait, competitor paragraph, language mix as `report-briefs-second-set`.
6. `report-to-portal-moves`: patterns, busyness, drafts, creator lists and follower series out of the report, with pointers.
