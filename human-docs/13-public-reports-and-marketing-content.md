# Public reports and marketing content

What we can publish for the public from data we already collect, to market the product: to diners (reach and brand), to owners (leads), to press (credibility). Every item says what data it needs, whether that data exists today, the cadence, and the legal or taste caveats.

## Ground rules for anything public

- **Never republish review texts or Google ratings** as our content. Google's reviews are Google's; quoting them in a private report to the venue's owner is one thing, a public page is another. Public content works with **our own counts** (posts, views, creators, shares, rising/falling) and with public venue names and districts.
- **Never show OSM-derived data** (licence).
- **Creators are public figures posting publicly**, but they are people: credit by handle, link to the original, use the platforms' official embeds rather than re-hosting video, and honour a removal request the same day.
- **Venues are businesses**; a factual "most posted about this month" list is fair comment. A "fastest falling" list is not something we publish; negatives stay in the private report.
- **Marketing e-mail needs consent (double opt-in)**; our mail provider (Brevo) is set up for transactional mail today. A newsletter is a product in itself. Gated content (a PDF behind an e-mail form, with a marketing tick box) is the clean way to earn that consent, and it is the only legal way around the cold-e-mail problem the sales doc describes.

## Which to do first

1. **Top hits (1)** is built, with its share images, which the **Instagram account (10)** can post as they are: it is the product's demo in public, and every listed venue is a warm lead ("you were #4 in Berlin in September").
2. **Blog data stories (8)**: free, SEO, end on the pitch.
3. **Gated benchmark (9)**: the mechanism that turns readers into consenting e-mail contacts.
4. **Trends (2)** is built: publish a quarter once its searches are pulled. **District guides (4)** once a second month of comparable data exists.
5. **Openings (3)** and **weekend tables (7)** when their data exists (the extraction flag now, the samples by December).

## Production notes

- Every public piece should be **published by an admin**, never automatically: a list with a wrong match (a namesake, an own-account post counted as word of mouth) in public is worse than a late post. The admin's mention corrections apply before publishing.
- Build public pages on the server-rendered frontend (the blog's pattern: SSR, meta tags, sitemap), one URL per city and month, so they index.
- Share cards: one renderer, three sizes (1200×630 for links, 1080×1080 and 1080×1350 for Instagram). The top-hits page has them at `/<city>/<month>/og.png`, `square.png` and `portrait.png`, once the month is published; the food trends page at `/<city>/trends/<YYYY>-q<N>/og.png` and the same two, once the quarter is.
- How the food trends page works: an admin opens Trends › Food trends report, picks the city and the quarter (only one that is over) and presses **Pull searches**. That asks Google Trends about every kind of food five at a time, each group with pizza in it, over the two years to the quarter's end: about a cent a kind of food, a few minutes, in the background. Then **Build** (free) and check the preview. The searches section says how much each kind of food is searched against pizza (100 = pizza's average week), and what rose against the same three months a year earlier, so a summer favourite never reads as a trend. Google's rising queries are Google's users' own words and may name a place; queries about closures, recalls or inspections and anything on the city's do-not-list are left out. Posts and openings are counts by kind of food, never a place's name. **Publish**, **Unpublish** and the share images work as for the top hits.
- How the top-hits page works today: an admin opens Trends › Public highlights, picks the city and month, presses **Build** (it reads the month's posts as they stand, corrections included) and checks the preview, which is exactly what the public will see. **Publish** is possible only once the month is over, and puts the page on the site and in the sitemap; **Unpublish** takes it down at once. Lists are positive only, a venue's own posts never count, and the most viewed post plays through TikTok's or Instagram's own player (loaded only when the reader taps play) with the creator's handle and a link to the original, and no wording of ours about it.
- The city keeps a **do-not-list** of venues and creators who asked to be left out; it sits next to the Build button. A name added there disappears from the lists and the top post at the next build (the month's totals still count their posts, since a total names nobody). A removal request is honoured the same day: add the name, rebuild.
