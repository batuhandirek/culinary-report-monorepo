## Rules

- **Every city is a row, never code.** Everything a step, the cron, a query or a prompt knows about a place comes from its `City`: sources (`CitySource`, enabled ones only), `locationQuery`, `tiktokRegion`, `instagramEnabled`, districts and borders, name stopwords, chain deny-list, budgets, `topBrands`, the name the prompts use (extraction and highlights; reports stay English until the `report-language` decision reads `City.language`). No `BERLIN_*`-style constant under `apps/`; a new city is the `/admin/cities` form. Venues, posts, lookups and pipeline runs carry their city (not null); mentions and reviews reach it through the venue or the post, reports carry it (brand × city), prospects reach it through their brand's branches. Matching, `topBrandIds`, `topBrands` (won prospects too), neighbours, competitor suggestions, trends and the lead score stay inside one city: a district name or a namesake in another city never counts. A known venue keeps its city when another city's scrape finds it again, and a new place is created only by a scrape that names its city (`saveGooglePlace(item, now, city)`; `refreshDetails` updates known venues only).
- **Sources keep their yield**: a post's `source` is `#value` or `search:value` (`sourcePost`), so `CityService.sourceYield` counts per source the posts it found first, those with a matched mention, and views over the last three months (the Sources tab on `/admin/cities/[cityId]`). Dead sources are disabled, not deleted, so the history stays readable.
- **Suggestions are never enabled by themselves**: "Suggest sources" stores the agent's hashtags and searches (city language and English, existing values skipped case-insensitively) as disabled sources marked `suggested`, the reason in `note`; switching one on or off is its review and clears the flag. "Suggest from venues" saves nothing: each postal code goes to the district most of the city's venues under it are in (a code is listed once), a new name needs 3+ venues, a district without borders gets those it shares a postal code with; the admin ticks what to keep and the list replaces `City.districts`.
- **The month's context is free data, never a cause**: `CityDay` is one row per city and local date, refetched for the last 7 days every morning (the archive revises its newest days; an empty newest day keeps the old figures); holidays are the country's nationwide ones plus `City.region`'s. A weekend is rainy when its Saturday or Sunday had ≥ 1 mm; weather figures are absent under 90 % coverage, so a gap never reads as dry. `ReportData.context` is set at build; the prompt may use it in one clause only to place a quiet or busy month.
- **Google search interest is per term, never across terms**: each term is pulled alone over the 12 months up to the month, so 100 is its own peak; a month is the mean of the weekly points that start in it and have data, needing at least `MIN_WEEKS_WITH_DATA` (2) of them (in Berlin a third of the topics, small cuisines, have data in a stray week only), and no data is `null`, never 0. Seasons move these lines too (ice cream falls every autumn): a "rising" claim beyond month on month needs the same months a year earlier. The terms are every `FoodTopic` with a `trendsTopicId` (else `trendsQuery`) plus won prospects' names (`SEARCH_INTEREST`, before `BUILD_REPORTS`, $2 default). The page draws searches and mention share as two lines on their own axes.
- **Food is searched as Google topics, in Food & Drink**: a bare English word measures other things in a German region (on 2026-10-18 "german" was translation searches, "thai" massage, "american" American Express, "market" the stock market). A topic id is language independent ("china restaurant" and "chinesisches restaurant" count for `/m/01xw9`) and disambiguated; category `71` strips the rest. Venue names are searched in all categories (`0`): 71 dropped most of a small venue's weeks (Markthalle Neun: nothing). So a pull is two actor runs, each capped at least at the actor's $1 floor (`splitTrendsBudget`). Restaurant-intent phrasings ("thai restaurant") are too sparse at region level (39 of 53 weeks without data in DE-BE) to be the default. Topic ids come from Trends' free autocomplete (`trends.google.com/trends/api/autocomplete/<q>`, strip the `)]}'` prefix; check the title and type, e.g. "Cuisine", "Dish").
- **Raw cuisines resolve to topics, several for a compound**: a mention's cuisine (else its venue's Google category) goes through `normalizeCuisine`, then the whole key or an alias (both looked up as `normalizeCuisine` and as `cuisineWords` joined), else the longest known runs of its words ("Vegan Döner" → vegan, kebab; "Snack bar" → street food, not bar). Unknown words are skipped; a string with nothing known (Restaurant, Hotel, "/") has no topic. A topic's mention share counts its children (`withParents`: ramen for Japanese) and a mention once per topic, so shares don't sum to 100 (unlike `TrendService`'s tables). A topic's key wins over another's alias; the seed suite asserts no alias is shadowed and every label resolves to its own topic.
- **Topics are edited, never re-keyed**: the key is fixed at creation (`normalizeCuisine` of the given key or label; 409 when taken) because stored trends rows carry it; label, type, parent, aliases and Trends fields change. An alias that already names another topic as a whole is refused (400, `FoodTopicIndex.exact`), and so is a parent that sits under the topic. Unmapped words are the city's mention cuisines and *food* venues' categories (`isFoodVenue`) that resolve to nothing; a new topic or alias applies to every read at once, as nothing stores resolved topics.
- **A venue's food topics are worked out on read, never stored**: its main Google category's topics score 1, its other categories' 0.5, a kind of food its posts by others give `MIN_TAG_MENTIONS` (2)+ times and on `MIN_TAG_SHARE` (25 %)+ of its posts with one scores that share; an admin's tag scores 1 and an admin's exclusion removes the topic whatever gives it. Only the admin's words are rows (`VenueFoodTopic`), so a new alias or topic retags every venue at once and no step recomputes anything. The primary (the one kind of food it is known for) is the first cuisine or dish of: the admin's tags, the main category, the posts' (most first); else the first of any kind in that order ("Cocktail bar": cocktails); so "Asian restaurant" stays Asian even when posts say pho, until an admin tags it.
- **A topic's figures are supply, buzz and demand**: venues = open food venues (`isFoodVenue`) whose tags, with parents, include it ("mainly": its primary, or a child's, is theirs); posts = the month's mentions by others resolving to it or a child, share in points against last month; searches = its stored 12-month line and the month's change. Topics with none of the three are left off the list. The shared `MonthChart` lives in `components/charts` (portal trends and patterns use it too).
- **Extraction is told the topics' labels** (`MentionExtractionAgent` context `foodTopics`): a mention's cuisine uses them where one fits, comma-separated, most specific first ("Pho, Vietnamese"), another short English word only when none fits, so the unmapped tail stops growing without forcing a new kind of food into a wrong topic.
- **Trends come from `signalbench/google-trends-scraper`** (Google's JSON endpoints, no browser, ~$0.004 a term; it refuses a cap under $1 per run). `apify/google-trends-scraper` was dropped: its `geo` takes countries only, and for `DE-BE` through explore URLs Google failed nine page loads in ten, ~$0.30 a term in retried proxy traffic. `geo` is `City.region`, else `country`; the window is `customTimeRange`. Items echo the searched text in `searchTerm`, the key they map back by; commas are dropped from a term first (`trendsQuery`), since Google returns nothing for one with a comma. Its weekly points carry no "has data" flag, so a 0 is too little data (Google's own meaning on a single-term query) and is left out. A term the run returned nothing for (the cap stopped it, Google failed it or has no data for it: the actor returns no item then) is not pulled: its stored months and related queries stay untouched, never set to null.
- **Related queries come with the same item at no extra cost**: up to 25 top (0–100 against the most searched one) and 25 rising (percent growth against the window before; "Breakout" past +5,000 %), stored in Google's order, each query once per list. They cover the pull's whole twelve-month window, not the month alone. A pull replaces a fetched term's rows for that month. The trends page opens them under a row ("Related searches").
- **Budgets**: a step spends `params.budgetUsd` (an admin's run), else the city's `stepBudgetsUsd[step]`, else `DEFAULT_STEP_BUDGET_USD` (`stepBudgetUsd`). The TikTok budget splits 60/40 between hashtags and searches, all of it to one kind when the city has none of the other. A search run's cap must reach its actor's $4 floor: 40 % of `SCRAPE_TIKTOK`, half of `SCRAPE_VENUE_SOCIAL` ($8 default for that reason); below it Apify refuses the run (a FAILED `ApifyRun` saying so) and the step goes on without searches.

- **Test runs under $10 need no asking** (the user's standing permission): run a small actor run to confirm input and item shapes before building on them, with a `maxTotalChargeUsd` well under $10.
- **A start Apify refuses for the plan's memory waits** (`isMemoryLimitError`, 16 GB across all runs on Free; Apify answers it with a 402): `runActor` tries again every `MEMORY_RETRY_SECONDS` (30), `MEMORY_RETRIES` (20) times. A step that runs several small runs side by side asks for less memory (`memoryMbytes`; the comments scraper takes 1 GB).
- **Every Apify call goes through `ApifyService.runActor` with a `maxCostUsd`**: Apify enforces it, and the run is recorded with its cost for the Data page's Apify costs. Apify settles a run's usage late (one read $0.0008 eight seconds after it ended, $0.037 half a minute later; the run `call` returns often says $0), so the cost is read once right after the items are fetched and again `COST_SETTLE_DELAY_SECONDS` (3 min) later by the `settle-apify-run-cost` job; pay-per-event charges are in `usageTotalUsd`. A run an actor limited for Free users still ends SUCCEEDED and says so in its status message (apidojo: "subscribe to a paid plan") or only in its log (apify/instagram-scraper: "limited to one page for free users"), so `runActor` reads both (`planLimitNote`, wording checked against every pipeline actor's logs): such a run is recorded PARTIAL with `Free-plan limit: <line>` in `error` (the Note column of the Apify runs table) and logged as a warning. Some actors print their Free limit on every run (scraping_solutions' hashtag pro, stanvanrooy6), so for them the note means "limited on Free", not necessarily "cut short". The account is on Apify's **Free plan and stays there** (the user's decision; never upgrade), with its monthly usage limit raised to $250 (`APIFY_MONTHLY_CREDIT_USD`; `maxMonthlyUsageUsd` in `GET /v2/users/me/limits`, which also gives this cycle's spend). Default step budgets are `DEFAULT_STEP_BUDGET_USD`. What the plan limits is actors, not money: some publishers cap Free runs at 10 items (apidojo, epctex, xmolodtsov: the run logs "Monthly run limit exceeded per user") or mask fields (memo23). Before adopting an actor, read its README for a Free-plan section and verify with one small run that it returns more than 10 items without that status message.
- **A pipeline run is never retried by pg-boss** (`retryLimit: 0`): a retry would pay again. Runs go one at a time, **oldest queued first** (`nextQueuedRunId`): a job only wakes `RunPipelineWorker`, which works through every queued run, so a resumed run goes before the ones queued after it and a run queued again never waits for a job of its own. A step that fails marks its `PipelineRun` FAILED with the error; rerun it from the Data page.
- **A restart never loses a run or pays twice.** Each step's result is stored as it finishes under `"<n>. <STEP>"`; `execute` skips the keys already there. Each step runs inside `apifyRunContext` (pipeline run id + step key, `apify/util/apify-run-context.ts`); `ApifyService.runActor` `start`s the actor and stores `apifyRunId` (with `inputHash`, `pipelineRunId`, `stepKey`) before `waitForFinish`, and inside a context re-attaches to a run an earlier attempt of the same step started with the same input (`earlierAttempt`, not FAILED, not already used by this process) instead of starting one. On boot `PipelineRecoveryService` queues every RUNNING run again (`resumes` + 1; at `MAX_PIPELINE_RESUMES` (5) it fails instead, a crash loop's stop), skipping the runs this process already picked up (`PipelineService.executing`); `RunPipelineWorker` waits for `PipelineRecoveryService.ready` before picking a run, since pg-boss may hand it a job before the hook ran and it would overtake the interrupted run, settles the Apify runs no resumed step will re-attach to with Apify's own status (`settleOrphans`, "Items not stored"), and wakes the worker for runs queued before the restart. On SIGTERM / SIGINT `processShutdown.requested` is set: no new actor run starts (`ShuttingDownError`), no new step starts, and a step that ends during the shutdown is not recorded (it may have swallowed a refused run, e.g. a search that "yields nothing" on failure), so the run stays RUNNING for the next process.
- **"Finish the month"** is a `MONTHLY` run with `params.onlyMissing`: a paid step (`isPaidStep`: one with a `DEFAULT_STEP_BUDGET_USD`) that already succeeded for the city and month (`latestStepStates` over the other runs, pure in `pipeline/util/step-states.ts`) is recorded as `SKIPPED_RESULT` instead of paid again; the free steps (extraction, matching, analysis, reports, scores) always run, as new data came in. `MONTHLY_STEPS` lives in `pipeline.constant.ts`.
- **OpenAI calls rotate over several accounts** (`OPENAI_API_KEYS`, `OpenAIAccountsService` in `modules/llm`): an account serves `OPENAI_ROTATE_AFTER_REQUESTS` calls, then the next takes over. An account OpenAI says is out of credits, or whose key it rejects, is blocked for that model for `OPENAI_BLOCK_HOURS` (24) and the call is retried on the next; the block is per model because the complimentary allowance is. Blocks live in memory, so a restart tries a blocked account once more. Only when every account is blocked for the model does the call fail, with OpenAI's messages, which `isFatalProviderError` matches.
- **An LLM account that fails fails the step.** `runPool` stops and rethrows on `isFatalProviderError` (no credits, a wrong key, quota used up, a 401; not a bare 402, which Apify uses for its memory refusal) instead of skipping every item, so a step never reads "done, 0 analysed". Matching keeps its exact and fuzzy matches, leaves the close calls undecided (`matchedAt` restored, so the next run retries them), flushes, then throws.
- **TikTok subtitle links expire within days**, so `SocialPostService` downloads subtitles while saving, right after the scrape, and prefers the original-language track over a machine translation (`source: MT` garbles names).
- Actors: TikTok hashtags through `clockworks/tiktok-scraper` (its search fails on device registration). TikTok searches (city queries and venue names) through `novi/advanced-search-tiktok-api`: TikTok's own video objects, `region` = `City.tiktokRegion`, `publishTime: THREE_MONTH`, by relevance; `limit` is per keyword (minimum 30), so one run covers all of a step's queries; `includeSearchTerms` puts the query in `__search_terms.keyword` (kept as `search:<query>`); subtitle links in `video.cla_info.caption_infos`, the tagged place in an `anchor_poi` anchor; it refuses a cap under $4. (`stanvanrooy6/tiktok-search-scraper` also works on Free, up to 100 rows a run, but has no subtitle links.) Instagram places through `scraping_solutions/instagram-location-posts-local-creator-finder`, whose name search can resolve to a namesake (another country, the city itself) — every place is checked with `isSameVenue`. Instagram hashtags through `apify/instagram-scraper` on tag URLs ($0.0027 a result on Free), `onlyPostsNewerThan` the month's start: items name their tag in `inputUrl`, so one run covers every hashtag; `resultsType: posts` returns the tag feed's images and carousels only, `reels` its top reels (a few per tag, the high-view ones), so the step runs both; on Free each tag stops after one page (5–27 posts, the newest; the log says so), so a busy tag's month is only its last day or two; `id` is the media pk; no follower counts (`authorFans` null); hidden like counts come back as -1; an empty tag returns one charged `{ error: 'no_items' }` item; pinned posts pass the date filter. TikTok comments through `clockworks/tiktok-comments-scraper` (see Comments). Free plan: 5 concurrent actor jobs (`maxConcurrentActorJobs`); a sixth run is refused, so a step runs at most 4 side by side.
- **The monthly run scrapes the city on both platforms**: `SCRAPE_TIKTOK` ($15) then `SCRAPE_INSTAGRAM` ($8, the city's Instagram hashtags in two runs, posts on three quarters of the budget and reels on a quarter, each run's per-hashtag `resultsLimit` sized to its share so Apify's cap never stops it before the last hashtag; Berlin's lean to brunch / cafés / bakeries, whose crowds post on Instagram; skipped where `instagramEnabled` is off), so the top list isn't TikTok-only. On Free a hashtag gives one page of posts a run, so the month's posts come from `SCRAPE_INSTAGRAM_RECENT` ($2, not in `MONTHLY`, the daily cron): the posts run alone, keeping the last `INSTAGRAM_RECENT_DAYS` (2) of posts whatever the run's month (a label), so the turn of a month leaves no gap; a busy tag (#berlinfood, ~20 a day) fills about one page a day. An Instagram post is `ig_<media pk>` whichever actor found it, so the hashtag and place scrapes share one row; the first-seen `source` stays.
- **Matching**: names are compared after `normalizeVenueName` (accents, punctuation and generic words like "restaurant" and the city's `nameStopwords` (its own name, local words like "imbiss") removed) with trigram similarity, boosted when the creator tagged that place or the hint names its district or street. Auto-accept at 0.9 and 0.1 ahead of the runner-up; otherwise the LLM picks among up to 5 candidates or says none (a chain whose branch is unknown is offered as the brand, with its branch count and its branches' `locations`, and the post's `author` goes along: a mention giving an address none of a candidate's branches is on, or posted by an account spelling another name, is another venue — "Vegan Garden, Frankfurter Allee 21" by @vegan.garden.berlin is not the chain Vegang). A wrong LLM match also hides the place from `LOOKUP_VENUES`, which searches only unmatched (`none`) names. Only food and drink categories are candidates (`isFoodVenue`); a chain's branches are one (see the brand rule). A mention in another city is `other_city` and never retried; a `none` is retried only after venues were added later than its last attempt (a lookup).
- **The brand is the unit; the venue is the address.** Every venue is a branch of exactly one `Brand` (a brand of its own until an admin merges it into a chain). Reports (brand × city × month), prospects (one per brand), portal access (`BrandMember`), the lead score, the monthly top list, competitors and the brand-name searches are keyed by the brand; Own accounts are the brand's too (`SocialAccount.brand`, several, a branch's own marked). Google data, reviews, snapshots, busyness, open tables, delivery, search ranks, status changes, press and food-topic tags stay on the venue. There is no main branch: where one place must stand for a chain (a report's place sections, the pipeline's place samples, outreach's phone and website) it is the most reviewed open branch in the city, worked out when needed (`busiestBranch`, `BrandService.branchesIn(...)[0]`), never stored.
- **Matching gives the brand always, the branch when known**: `Mention.brand` is set on every match, `Mention.venue` only when the post says which branch (a brand-only mention has no venue; `match` retries `brand: null`). Candidates carry `brandId`, `brandName` and `branchCount` (the brand's open branches in the city); a one-branch brand is a plain venue. A chain's branches are one candidate (`rankCandidates`): the branch is known when the creator tagged exactly one, or — when the post names more than the brand ("Burgermeister Kottbusser Tor") — the closest name is `BRANCH_LEAD` (0.05, uncapped) ahead, or — when it names only the brand — a hint naming one branch's district or street puts it that far ahead (on the name alone a branch called just like the brand would win). A street counts without its house number and with "Straße" / "Str." and "Platz" / "Pl." spelled one way (`streetKey`, `hintNamesStreet` in `util/venue-names.ts`; keys under 5 letters never count). **What the code can't place, the LLM may**: each chain location it is shown carries the branch id, and `VenueMatchAgent` returns `branchId` (the street, the neighbourhood it is in, a landmark next to it; null when the post names several, none or only the city). A sure name match to a chain (no LLM for the name) whose hint the code couldn't place gets one call with that chain alone; a hint with no words ("/", a postcode) gets none. A hint naming the streets of two or more branches gets no call, and any `branchId` for it is dropped (`namesSeveralBranches`): mini picked one of the listed addresses in 5 of 7 such posts despite the prompt. A `branchId` of another brand is ignored. Extraction's `locationHint` is only what the post states; on 2026-10-19 ten dev hints were the model's own guesses ("Schöneberg (implied from venue reputation, not in source)"), which the match agent is told to ignore. Counting by brand (`topBrandIds`, the lead score, the report, the portal's posts, trends) reads `m.brand_id` with the post's city (`p.city_id`); reading by place (districts, neighbours, a venue's tags) reads `m.venue_id` and leaves brand-only mentions out. A brand change rematches both brands' non-admin mentions (`rematchBrands`).
- **Merging is the only way to make a chain** (`BrandService.merge`, the Suggested tab's confirm, and `addBranches`): the brand that stays has the prospect, else the most reports, else the biggest venue; the others' venues, mentions, reports (a clash of city and month keeps the kept brand's), prospect, members (no doubles), competitors (no doubles; competition inside the new chain goes; rivals pointing at a merged brand point at the chain) move over in one transaction (`absorb`), search interest rows are dropped, and the brands are deleted. Two prospects among them is 409: take one off first. `removeBranch` and `split` give the places brands of their own (named like them, their own mentions follow them); the records stay with the old brand; a brand's last branch can't be taken off.
- **Searches by name use the brand's name** (press, TikTok search, Google Trends for won brands: `venueSearchName` with `brand` populated), since `@burgermeister` is not found in "Burgermeister Schlesisches Tor" and a one-branch brand may be renamed.
- **Competitors are a brand's** (`BrandCompetitor`, `CompetitorService`): each a place or a whole rival brand, for the whole brand or for one branch (a chain's branch competes locally). A place or rival is on a brand's list once; `MAX_COMPETITORS` (5) per subject (the brand-wide list, each branch's own). Never one of the brand's own branches. A report in a city compares the brand-wide ones there plus the branch-only ones whose branch is there (`inCity`); a rival counts with its open branches in the city together (`ReportCompetitor.branches`), a branch-only one is labelled (`against`) and the prompt compares it with that branch.
- **A brand's report is all its branches in one city** (`ReportService.build(brandId, cityId, month)`): `social`, `posts`, `ownPosts`, `reviews`, `dishes`, `previous` and the reply drafts cover every open branch there (brand-only mentions included); `ReportData.venue` is the described branch (most reviewed), and the place sections (busyness, open tables, own accounts, comments, press, search ranks, neighbourhood, rating trend) are its. A chain gets `ReportData.brand` (`name`, `brandOnlyPosts`, `branches`: each cut like a competitor from the posts that named it and its reviews, `described`), drawn as "Branch by branch" (`views/report/ReportBranches.svelte`, the cover titled by the brand); neighbours and nearby creators leave its branches out; the prompt compares branches on percentages among those with 5+ written reviews and never credits `postsNamingNoBranch` to one. **A brand in several cities gets a report per city, or one across all of them** (`Brand.reportScope` `all`, the brand page's "Reports" picker): `build(brandId, null, month)` covers every open branch (posts of every city, `ReportBranch.city`), its `Report.city` is null (Postgres lets two null keys through, so `build` looks the row up first), and it has no `data.city` (one city's peers don't compare with a brand across cities); its place sections are its most reviewed branch's, in that branch's city, and competitors are `inCities` (a rival counts with its branches in all of them). The monthly `BUILD_REPORTS` builds it only in the run of the last of the brand's monthly-cron cities (`otherCitiesDone`: every other one has a succeeded `MONTHLY` or `BUILD_REPORTS` run of the month; the queue runs one run at a time) and counts the others as `waiting`. The portal's series and patterns read the across-cities reports for such a brand (no city picker); the mail goes out for the newest of the same scope.
- **Brand suggestions save nothing** (`suggestBrands`, worked out on read, most mentioned first): venues of one city that are a brand of their own (one branch), with the same `brandKey` (parentheses, slogans and city stopwords dropped), or a key and longer names starting with it ("Burgermeister Leopoldplatz"), or one website (not a platform page; an Instagram or TikTok profile counts) with the same first word. A one-word key extends only at `BRAND_PREFIX_MIN_LENGTH` (8) letters and when it is not a common word (inside `COMMON_WORD_NAMES` (2)+ other names not first: "Cafe & Bäckerei Olive"), since a word for food sweeps in strangers ("burger" → "Burger Vision"). A key is one suggestion (the admin page and dismissals are keyed by it): a website group whose shared words are a key already suggested joins that group. Keys of chains and of `BrandDismissal`s are skipped; a dismissal is final. Same-name independents ("Asia Imbiss") still come up: the admin dismisses them.
- **A brand that is one for sure merges by itself, as its venues come in** (`BrandService.autoMerge`, queued per city as `BrandAutoMergeJob` by every `VenueScrapeService` save — city scrape, lookups, brand search, detail pages — on a `stately` queue with the city as `singletonKey`; "Find branches" runs it before it stamps `branchesSearchedAt`). The proof is the site (`util/brands.ts`): `ownSite` reads a Google website as its registrable domain (`registrableDomain`: "pberg.benedict.world" → "benedict.world"), a shared host's own part ("veracruz-berlin.eatbu.com") or an Instagram/TikTok profile; `siteNamedAfter` says whether it is named after the brand (the name, maybe with what sites add — "nilakitchen.de", "loveatfirstbite_berlin"; it holds the name or its first words; or the name holds it), which a listing or delivery site never is ("speisekartenweb.de"), and a single word for food never names anyone ("pizza.de"). First every chain takes the city's lone food venues of its name (`isBrandName`) whose site has the letters of one of its branches' named sites ("losteria.net" = "losteria.de"; "roma-berlin" ≠ "romaberlin"), or no site where the chain has a branch and its name is distinctive (`isDistinctiveBrand`: not only food and place words — "Asia Haus" is not), never one in `excludedVenueIds`. Then each suggestion with `oneBrandSite` becomes a chain: one named site given by 2+ of its venues, no second domain or profile, a profile beside the domain named after the brand too, venues without a site only for a distinctive name. One `BrandChangedJob` for the run; two prospects or a taken key are logged and left to the admin. On dev Berlin (2026-10-19) it merged 126 of 287 suggestions. **An admin's word stays**: `split` dismisses the chain's keys (`BrandDismissal`), and `removeBranch` always puts the branch in `excludedVenueIds`, so neither is undone by the next run.
- **Branches are found by a brand search; only those on the brand's site join by themselves** ("Find branches" queues `BrandSearchJob`, worker `BrandSearchWorker`, never retried, since a run takes ~30 s; the page polls `branchesSearchedAt`): `searchBrands` is one `compass/crawler-google-places` run for the brands' names in the city, `BRAND_SEARCH_RESULTS` (40) places each (Berlin, 2026-10-18: Burgermeister 15 branches + 25 look-alikes, Brammibal's 9 + 3, $0.21 both); every place is saved as a lookup (never an opening), the places on the brand's own site join it (`autoMerge`), and `candidates` (on read, any city) offers the other open food venues that are a brand of their own and whose name `isBrandName`, minus `excludedVenueIds`. Unlike `lookupNames` (3 results a name, never repeated: why chains were missing) it repeats: "Find branches" on the brand page, and `SEARCH_BRANCHES` ($2), which the monthly cron queues after each quarterly `SCRAPE_VENUES` for every chain with a branch in the city.
- **A brand has no ownership or size signal**: who decides there (independent or corporate) is not recorded, and the lead score never weighs how many branches a brand has. Big chains and institutions are handled by the city's `chainDenyList` alone.
- **An admin's correction is final**: `PATCH /admin/mentions/:id` (`MentionMatchingService.correct`) sets `manual` (linked, or unlinked with no venue) or `ignored` (not a venue; never has one), with an optional `matchNote`. `match()`, even with `rematch`, never selects a `manual` or `ignored` mention, so a new matching rule can't undo a fix. A report shows the correction only after it is rebuilt (the Rebuild action on the venue's Reports tab). The picker is `dialogs/admin/ChangeMentionVenueDialog.svelte` (post page, venue Mentions tab); `/admin/posts?unmatchedOnly=true` lists posts with a `none` mention to link by hand. Closed venues are never auto-matched, so posts from before a closure need a manual link.
- **City hashtags pull in other cities**: extraction (told the post's city name) records each mention's `city`; `isInCity` compares it with the city's name and `aliases`. Mentions without a city count as in the city.
- **A brand's own account is not word of mouth, and that is a stored link** (`SocialAccount.brand`), never a name guessed on read: every count of posts by others (`topBrandIds`, the brand list and summary, the lead score, trends, topic mentions, food tags, comments, the report, the portal) leaves out a post whose account is the brand's — SQL through `ownPostSql(post, brandColumn)`, TypeScript through `post.account.brand` (`SummaryMention.own`, `NeighbourPost.own`). So an admin's correction on an account moves every count at once. `summarizeReport` lists own posts separately; the highlights prompt is told so. `topBrandIds` ranks by posts from others in SQL; a post naming two branches counts once.
- **Own accounts are matched once, stored, and an admin's word is final** (`AccountMatchingService.match`, free, after every `MATCH_MENTIONS` run and on a brand change, and "Match now" on the Social page): a branch's Google website that is an Instagram or TikTok profile links it (`website`, creating the account before anything it posted was scraped; one branch's own when another branch of the brand gives a different profile); outreach's `Prospect.instagram` links in `SCRAPE_OWN_ACCOUNTS` (`prospect`); every other account of the city goes through `matchAccount` against its open food venues' names and their brands' (`handle`). The scorer strips the city's words (stopwords, names, districts' words), digits at the ends and venue-type affixes (`restaurant`, `cafe`, `official`, `bln`…) off the handle, and compares it with each name whole, without generic words, and without kinds of place at its ends ("Imbiss Olympische Brücke" → "olympischebruecke", in all three umlaut spellings). It **links** an exact, distinctive name of 5+ letters (8+ when it is a trimmed name) that no other brand of the city has, or any exact name the account's posts named (`postedAbout`: brands its mentions were matched to) when no other brand they named has it; a near name (trigram Dice ≥ 0.85) or a contained one (the handle is a run of the name's words, or holds the name on its word breaks: "midori" in "east_of_midori", never "vegang" in "vegan.garden") links only when the posts named it. Anything else close is a **suggestion** (`suggestedBrand`), for the Social page's "To review" tab or the brand page. A name made only of food words ("Ramen", "Dönerhaus") is never linked or suggested by itself. On dev Berlin (2026-10-19) this linked 340 accounts by handle and 178 by website, with ~40 suggestions; the old name check had "matched" 774 handles city-wide, many wrongly. The matcher never touches an admin's, a website's or outreach's link, and never a brand in `rejectedBrandIds` (an admin's "Not theirs", final); its own earlier links it re-decides each run. A chain's branch account: the handle names one branch and not the brand.
- **Creators are people**: every account belongs to a `Creator`. A new account joins the creator of the one account on the other platform with the same letters (`creatorKey`, 6+ letters: `polina_iz_berlina` / `polina.iz.berlina`), at creation only, so an admin's split stays split; an admin merges ("Theirs too") and splits ("Not the same person") on the creator page. Creator counts (the brand list's and summary's `creators`, the lead score's buzz, trends' creators table) count creators, so one person's two platforms count once. A brand's own accounts have creators too (named like the account); creator lists leave out creators whose every account is a brand's unless asked.
- **The month's report brands are `PipelineService.topBrands`**: the top N (`City.topBrands`) of `topBrandIds` plus every `WON` prospect's brand with a branch in the city whatever its rank (`ProspectQueryService.wonBrandIds`), since the report is what a subscriber pays for; each `{ brand, branches, venue }` (its open branches in the city, most reviewed first; `venue` the described one). Steps on names take one venue a brand (`SCRAPE_VENUE_SOCIAL`, press, search ranks, busyness, delivery, own accounts: the described branch); `SCRAPE_REVIEWS` and `REFRESH_VENUE_DETAILS` take every branch; competitors are `competitorVenueIdsOf`. A `LOST` or `OPTED_OUT` brand still gets a report when it ranks (the data serves neighbours and trends); only outreach stops. `PipelineModule` imports `OutreachModule` for this, so outreach never imports the pipeline.
- **Reports are data, rendered by the frontend.** `ReportData` is computed once at build; rebuilding replaces it. Highlights must only state numbers present in the data — the input groups noticed changes per review so the model counts reviews, not changes.
- **Topic counts are sources, not points**: `praise` / `complaints` count the reviews (or posts) that raised a topic, once each, however many points the LLM split it into. The prompt gets them as `"19 of 34 written reviews"` strings so the model can't call them complaints; only written reviews can raise a topic. Dishes count once per post or review, grouped accent-insensitively.
- **Competitors**: `ReportService.collect` summarizes each competitor's same month with `summarizeReport` and cuts it down with `summarizeCompetitor` into `ReportData.competitors`. `reviewsTracked` says whether we scrape its reviews at all, so an untracked competitor shows "—", never 0. The monthly `SCRAPE_REVIEWS` step adds the report brands' competitors inside the same budget. The prompt compares by name, on percentages, only with tracked competitors and groups of 5+ written reviews; the report's matrices use the same 5-review floor. Stored `ReportData` missing a required field (`competitors`, `creators`, `reviews.list`, `venue.ratingTrend`, …) fails the response type: rebuild old reports after changing `ReportData`.
- **Google Maps details** (`Venue.details`, typed `VenueDetails`; read it, don't re-derive it from items): hours, amenities (the actor's `additionalInfo` flattened to section → option → offered), the all-time star distribution, photos count, reservation link, temporarily closed, places tags, `reviewsTags` ("people often mention": all-time counts of what reviews name — dishes, drinks, staff — detail page only; the place page lists them) — and, from detail pages only, popular times (histogram per `Mo`…`Su`, live percent), menu, delivery platforms (`orderBy`), `peopleAlsoSearch` (titles, no place id). Every `saveGooglePlace` merges what the item carries: an empty placeholder (`{}`, `[]`, `null`) counts as absent, so a cheap list scrape never erases a detail page's fields, and amenities merge section by section (a list scrape returns mostly Accessibility). An absent field is unknown, never "none"; popular times are often missing even on a detail page. `detailsAt` is when details last changed from any scrape. The monthly `REFRESH_VENUE_DETAILS` step (before `BUILD_REPORTS`, $2 default) re-scrapes `topBrands` ∪ their competitors by `placeIds` with `scrapePlaceDetailPage: true` and `scrapeOrderOnline: true` (the "Order online" links have their own flag), `maxReviews` / `maxImages` / `maxQuestions` 0 (Q&A is personal data); it also writes the day's rating snapshot. `bin/backfill-venue-details.mjs` filled dev from the old repo's saved list-scrape datasets. `ReportData.venue.amenities` (`offeredAmenities`: our section order, options alphabetical, since jsonb keeps no key order) and `reviewsDistribution` are optional, absent when unknown; the cover shows them.
- **Rating trend from the review scrape**: every `compass/Google-Maps-Reviews-Scraper` item repeats its place's `totalScore` and `reviewsCount`, so `scrapeMonth` writes each venue's snapshot (dated the run day) at no extra cost; a venue without a review since the month began returns no item and gets none. `ReportData.venue.ratingTrend` keeps, per month, the latest snapshot that closes it (one dated in a month's first week closes the month before, as the monthly run does), six months at most up to the report month; the report draws it from two points.
- **Month over month**: `ReportData.previous` is the month before through the same `summarizeReport` (no LLM call) plus that month's report actions; `undefined` when the venue had neither posts nor reviews then, so the view shows no deltas, and its `reviews` is absent when no review of that month is stored (not scraped, not zero). The prompt gets `compareMonths` (both values and the change pre-computed: the model quotes, never computes) and is told to follow up on each previous action.
- **Sources appendix**: `reviews.list` holds every review of the month (newest first, 240-char excerpt of the translated text, topics, replied); `posts` / `ownPosts` already hold every post. The actor's `reviewUrl` is personal data (`personalData: false` drops it), so the appendix links the venue's `googleUrl` instead. It prints after the footer and is collapsed on screen.
- **Creators**: `creators` is the top eight authors by views among posts by others; `creatorsNearby` the top eight by followers among authors with a non-promotional post about a district food neighbour this month and none about the venue, a neighbour's own account excluded (`selectNearbyCreators`). The prompt may name one in an action.
- **Weekly review watch** (`WATCH_REVIEWS`, not in `MONTHLY`, $1 default): the city's `WON` prospects' open venues only, reviews published in the last `REVIEW_WATCH_DAYS` (10) whatever the month (the run's `month` is a label), analysed, then `claimAlerts` stamps `alertedAt` and publishes `review-alert` in one transaction for each written review of 3 stars or fewer that is unanswered (no `ownerResponse`, no `markedRepliedAt`) and not alerted yet, so a review alerts once. Outreach's `ReviewAlertWorker` → `ReviewAlertService` mails the prospect's `contactEmail` (`ReviewAlertEmail`, en/de) with a draft from one `ReviewReplyAgent` call, kept on `Review.replyDraft`, and logs a touch (template `review-alert`); a venue no longer `WON` gets nothing.
- **Reply drafts**: `reviews.replyDrafts` covers unanswered written reviews, lowest stars first, ten at most (`replyCandidates`), from one `ReviewReplyAgent` call per report given the original text and language. A failed call leaves the report without drafts rather than failing it; rebuilding the report regenerates them.
- **Competitor suggestions never add themselves**: `suggest(brandId, cityId?, limit, branchId?)` (the Competitors tab, one click each, the place or its whole chain) scores, around the brand's most reviewed branch in the city (or `branchId`), open food venues by the food they share (`sharedFood` over both venues' topic tags: 3 for the same primary, 2 when one's primary is among the other's food tags with parents or both primaries share a parent (ramen and sushi: Japanese), 1 for any overlapping cuisine or dish; formats like fast food or café never count as food, but two venues whose primary is a place type (two bars) match on it), 0.5 per shared diet ("both vegan"), in the same or a neighbouring district, plus same price level and `log(mentions)`, plus 2 when Google lists one under the other's "People also search for" (`googleAlsoSearched`: titles only, compared after `venueAliases` with the city's stopwords at 0.85 similarity), which also lifts the district condition; a venue with nothing in common to eat is never suggested, however close or however Google pairs them. The only automatic adder is the explicit bulk action (`POST /admin/pipeline/fill-competitors`, the Competitors panel on the Data page's Runs tab), which fills the month's report brands that have **no** brand-wide competitors and leaves the rest alone, so a comparison stays stable month to month. Run it before `SCRAPE_REVIEWS`, which scrapes competitors' reviews.
- **One competitor list per brand, whoever edits it**: the admin's Competitors tab (venue page and brand page), the owner's portal Settings and the bulk fill all go through `CompetitorService.add` / `remove` on the same rows; `pickedBy` records who (shown in the portal as "your pick" / "our pick"). `MAX_COMPETITORS` (5) caps every add per subject, admin's included (400 `TOO_MANY_COMPETITORS`); an owner may only pick an open food venue in one of the brand's cities (the branch's, for a branch), or a rival with a branch there. Since the fill skips brands that have any brand-wide competitor, an owner's picks are never overwritten.
- **Trends are shares, never counts**: the scrape budget moves the counts. Every table sums to 100 within each month (the tail folds into "Other"), and a delta is in percentage points. Only matched mentions count (so in the city), a venue's own posts never; a mention's cuisine row is its one topic (`mentionTopic`: the first cuisine or dish its cuisine names, else its first topic, else its venue's category's the same way; labels from the topics). Dishes count once per post or review (`dishKey`, so "döner" and "döner kebab" are separate rows), and reviews exist only for scraped venues, so dish shares lean to report venues and competitors. Rising venues: the month's top 50 by posts from others against last month's, biggest climb first.
- **The report's city section** (`ReportData.city`, `ReportCityService.section`, pure `reports/util/city-section.ts`) is absent below `CITY_SECTION_MIN_MENTIONS` (200) matched mentions in the city's month. Rising rows: up to five cuisines and dishes with a positive share delta and 3+ sources, never "Other". The venue's kind of food is its primary food topic (`VenueFoodTopicService.tags`, admin tags included); its rank is by views among the venues posts named for that food. The baseline compares posts by others, views, the month's average stars and reply rate with the median of the month's report venues (`BUILD_REPORTS` passes `topBrands` as `peerVenueIds`; a single rebuild takes the venues with a report of that month), the percentile over the others that have a value, ties half; a venue without reviews that month has no stars or reply rate there, never zero.
- **Openings and closures** are recorded only by `VenueService.saveGooglePlace`: a known venue whose stored state (`closed`, else `details.temporarilyClosed`) changes writes one `VenueStatusChange`; a new place writes `opened` only from a city-wide scrape (`SCRAPE_VENUES`) after the city's first one (`City.venuesScrapedAt`, stamped by `scrapeCity`) and with fewer than `OPENING_MAX_REVIEWS` (150) reviews, since a capped city scrape returns a different slice each time and an old place it missed before is not new. Name lookups never count as openings. `at` is when a scrape saw it, never an opening date. Windows are the three months ending with the report month shifted a week (`quarterWindow`: a scrape in a month's first week closes the month before). `ReportData.neighbourhood.changes` counts the district (the venue left out) and the venue's street (`streetName`, house number dropped), absent without a district or before the city's first city-wide scrape. The lead score's new-place bonus needs an `opened` change within `LEAD_NEW_PLACE_MONTHS`, never `createdAt` (a venue a lookup found late is not new).
- **Search ranks** (`TRACK_SEARCH_RANKS`, before `BUILD_REPORTS`, $3 default): up to three queries per report venue: its food topic keys (its tags, primary first, then their parents: "ramen", "japanese"; fast food, street food and halal left out) × its district; no district, no queries. One `compass/crawler-google-places` run for all of them, the city name appended to each search and no `locationQuery` (a map grid would not be Google's own order), `maxCrawledPlacesPerSearch` 20; items carry `searchString` and `rank`. A venue not in the results stores `rank: null`; a search that came back empty stores nothing; a rerun replaces the month's rows. `ReportData.searchRanks` holds the month's searches best first with up to five earlier months each.
- **5 stars against the rest** (`reviews.split`) is the report's sharpest finding: what 5-star reviews praise next to what the reviews below 5 criticise. The prompt tells the model to lead with the topic criticised by the largest share below 5. The report shows it when both groups have 3+ written reviews.
- **The PDF is the frontend page printed by headless Chrome.** `ReportPdfService` hands the report to `/print/report` on `window.__REPORT__` (the page holds no data and needs no session), waits for `data-report-ready`, prints A4 with the page box and running footer from that page's `@page` rules. `ReportView` switches to two columns at `sm`, which the A4 column clears; page breaks are `break-*` utilities in it. In production Chrome is Puppeteer's own download, made by `npm ci` into `PUPPETEER_CACHE_DIR=/app/.cache/puppeteer` (`nixpacks.toml` `[variables]`, so it ships in the image); `nixpacks.toml` lists Chrome's apt libraries and fallback fonts (DejaVu, Noto emoji) itself, without `"..."`, because the Node provider's Puppeteer defaults add apt `chromium`, a snap shim on Ubuntu. A launch error naming a missing `lib*.so` means a package to add there. `PUPPETEER_EXECUTABLE_PATH` overrides the binary.
- **Own accounts are tracked, not just recognised**: the monthly `SCRAPE_OWN_ACCOUNTS` step (after `REFRESH_VENUE_DETAILS`, $2 default) links outreach's handles of the month's report brands, then scrapes each of their linked accounts once (`apify/instagram-profile-scraper`, one item per profile; `clockworks/tiktok-profile-scraper`, ten latest videos, pinned ones out; the budget splits by paid items) into the month's `SocialAccountSnapshot`, and the account takes the profile's display name and followers. A brand may have several accounts; a branch's own (`branch`) counts in its city's reports and portal series only, named by the branch. A profile that returns nothing gets no snapshot (unknown, not zero). A merge moves the merged brands' accounts and suggestions to the brand that stays and drops their rejections; a branch taken off takes its own accounts to its new brand. `ReportData.ownAccounts` gives followers, the change since the month before's snapshot (absent without one), `branch`, and `creatorViewsMultiple` (others' average views per post on that platform over the account's own). The admin shows them on the brand page's Overview and the branch page's Place tab (`components/admin/BrandAccounts.svelte`: figures, the matcher's suggestions to confirm, rejections to undo) and every city's on the Social page's "Own accounts" tab.
- **Comments under the top posts**: `SCRAPE_COMMENTS` ($13 default) takes each report venue's five most viewed TikTok posts of the month by others with comments, never one scraped before (`commentsScrapedAt`, set even when nothing came back; a post whose run failed stays unscraped), up to 100 top-level comments each (TikTok serves them most liked first) through `clockworks/tiktok-comments-scraper` ($0.00125 a comment on Free; `topLevelCommentsPerPost`, `maxRepliesPerComment: 0`; items name their post in `videoWebUrl` and carry no language, so `PostComment.language` stays null). One run per post on its share of the budget, its limit sized to that share. `ANALYSE_COMMENTS` classifies the city's unread ones one post per call (the agent is told the venue): tags-only and emoji-only comments need no model (`plainIntent`); a question gets a topic and a short English wording; the post keeps one line on what the disagreeing comments say. `ReportData.comments` counts intents per post and lists the three most asked question topics; one asked 5+ times (`COMMENT_QUESTION_ACTION_MIN`) carries a fixed action ("tag the location in your own posts"). Instagram comments are not scraped.
- **Press**: `SCRAPE_PRESS` ($3 default, 60 % news) searches each report venue's quoted name with the city on Google News (`scrapewise/google-news-scraper`, `dateFrom` / `dateTo` = the month) and Google (`apify/google-search-scraper`, `afterDate` / `beforeDate`, one page), keeps results that can be press (`isPressCandidate`: not a platform, directory, booking or delivery site nor the venue's own domain, and the title or snippet names it), once per URL (a page found again keeps its first venue), then classifies them a venue per call; namesakes are `unrelated` and never shown. Every searched venue gets `pressSearchedAt`: without it its press is unknown, not none. `ReportData.press` lists features, then lists, then passing mentions.
- **TripAdvisor** (the tourist side): `SCRAPE_REVIEWS` gives a tenth of its budget to the report venues' TripAdvisor side. A venue's page is found once (`tripadvisorSearchedAt`, found or not) by a Google search `site:tripadvisor.com "<name>" <city>`, taking the first `Restaurant_Review` URL whose title is the venue (`isSameVenue`) in the city (`isInCity`); then `scrapeunblocker/tripadvisor-reviews-scraper` ($0.40 / 1k, newest first, `since` the month) stores the month's reviews (title then text, no reviewer) and the page's all-time rating on the venue. `ANALYSE_REVIEWS` reads both platforms with one `ReviewAnalysisAgent`. The report's review counts cover both; `reviews.byPlatform` splits them (present only for a venue with a TripAdvisor side) and `platformGaps` names topics whose share differs by 20+ points with 5+ written reviews on each side; appendix items carry `platform: 'tripadvisor'`. The portal's review explorer filters by platform (`facets.platforms`).
- Long LLM loops run their calls side by side on one entity manager and flush once at the end: never flush inside a `runPool` worker; when the pool rethrows a provider failure, flush what finished first, then rethrow.
- **Models by volume** (each OpenAI account's daily complimentary allowance: 10M tokens on `gpt-5.4-mini`, 1M on `gpt-5.4`; past it the account is blocked for that model, and once every account is, the step fails): the bulk agents run on `openai/gpt-5.4-mini` — `MentionExtractionAgent` (~1,400 tokens a post; 641 posts took 0.9M on the full model), `ReviewAnalysisAgent` (batches of 10 reviews, since mini reads a long batch less closely), `VenueMatchAgent`, `CommentIntentAgent` (80 comments a call), `PressClassificationAgent`. What reaches an owner stays on `openai/gpt-5.4`: `ReportHighlightsAgent`, `ReviewReplyAgent`, `PitchHookAgent` (and `CitySourceSuggestionAgent`); ~20 reports a month fit its 1M. Moving an agent between them is a cost decision: check the volume first.
- **Website icons are plain fetches, never Apify**: `VenueFaviconCron` takes `FAVICON_BATCH` (40) due open venues an hour: never looked for first (`saveGooglePlace` clears `faviconCheckedAt` when the website changes), then found ones after `FAVICON_REFRESH_DAYS` (30), then ones where nothing was found after `FAVICON_RETRY_DAYS` (7). A website that is a platform profile (Instagram, Linktree, Lieferando, …: `faviconSite`), an IP or a local name has no icon of its own (`no_site` drops a stored one). Candidates, best first: the page's `<link rel=icon|apple-touch-icon>` by declared size (top `FAVICON_MAX_LINKS`), `/favicon.ico` on the host the page redirected to, Google's `s2/favicons` copy (404 = none). Every fetch has a 10 s timeout, a size cap and checks each redirect hop (`isPublicWebUrl`); a file is an icon by its bytes (`faviconFormat`), not its name or content type. Unchanged bytes (sha256 `faviconHash`) are not uploaded again; a replaced icon's `Image` row and ImageKit file are removed (`ImageService.removeImage`). A failed fetch keeps the stored icon. ImageKit reads no dimensions from some ICO files: `createImageFromBytes` deletes the upload and throws `UnreadableImageError`, and the next candidate is tried. Any other upload error (missing keys, an outage) is no verdict on the venue: the run stops, the venues it didn't finish stay due (no `faviconCheckedAt`), and the job fails after flushing what it stored. **The same bytes on `GENERIC_FAVICON_HOSTS` (3) different hosts are a platform default** (WordPress's logo, Wix's `pfavico.ico`), not the venue's: they are dropped from every venue holding them and the hash is kept, so the count stays and the venue waits the refresh period; a chain's branches on one host count once. The admin venue list, competitor lists and venue header show `favicon`; every route serializing it must populate it (`forceObject` turns an unpopulated relation into `{ id }`, which fails `ImageOutputDto`).
- **Lead score** (0–100, `/admin/venues` column and "Prospects only" at `LEAD_PROSPECT_SCORE` 60, breakdown on the venue Overview tab): five weighted signals in `LEAD_SCORE_WEIGHTS` over the report month and the one before — buzz (posts by others, views, creators as percentiles among venues others posted about, since scrape budgets vary), already spends (own posts, promotional posts by others, website), nameable problem (half the strongest, half the mean of complaint share, unanswered critical reviews, reply rate under 50 %, noticed changes, negative or mixed posts; definitions as in `summarizeReport`), reachable, press (best classified press of the window: a feature 1, a listicle 0.75, a passing mention 0.25; unknown for a venue `SCRAPE_PRESS` never searched in the window, and `missing` says "press not searched"; `press` is optional in `LeadSignals`, since older scores lack it) — plus a new-place bonus. **Without reviews in the window the problem signal is unknown (`null`), counted at half, and `missing` says so**: missing data neither sinks nor lifts a venue. `LeadSignal.value` is optional in the type because `serialize({ skipNull })` drops that null inside the jsonb too, and a required `number | null` fails the response type. A name on the city's `chainDenyList` keeps half its score, and `LeadSignals.denyListed` says so (the Sales tab's panel shows it). A brand without an open branch has none. The score is the brand's (`Brand.leadScore`): each city's `SCORE_LEADS` scores the brands with an open branch in the city over all their open branches in every city (posts, reviews, press summed; a phone or website on any branch; new only when every branch is), against the buzz of the city's brands; the venue list shows and sorts by its brand's. The frontend mirrors the threshold in `lib/culinary.ts`.
- Months are `YYYY-MM`, UTC.
- **A shared report link is the token alone**: 16 random bytes as 22 base64url characters, unique; anyone holding it reads the report with no login. Revoking is final (a new link replaces it), and a revoked or unknown token answers the same 404. Every share route goes through `ReportShareService`.

The count is one `update … open_count + 1`.
- The public PDF (`/reports/shared/:token/pdf`) is public Chrome work, so `SharedReportsController` keeps the last 8 printed PDFs per report version (id + `updatedAt`) and shares an in-flight print; the repo has no rate limiter. The `/r` page links to it with a plain `<a download>` (a phone handles an attachment better than a blob).
- **Log as sent**: the admin report page's Share panel logs a link through the outreach admin routes (`listProspects({ brandId })`, then `logOutreachTouch` → `OutreachService.log`, kind EMAIL or DM, the link in the body, template `report-link`) when the brand is a prospect that hasn't opted out. `OutreachModule` imports `ReportsModule`, so the reports module never imports outreach.

- **Busyness** (Google popular times as a foot-traffic proxy): `busynessIndex` = mean of the histogram's ten busiest hours, 0–100. Google scales each histogram to the venue's own peak, so the index compares a venue with itself over time, never venues with each other. A sample is a detail-page scrape (`VenueScrapeService.scrapeDetailPages`, which also refreshes `Venue.details`) of the report venues and their competitors; a venue whose page shows no popular times gets a row with `histogram: null`, one the scrape returned nothing for gets none. The report's section ("Did the buzz fill the place?") is absent without a sample with popular times; its weeks are Mondays in the city's time zone (`monthWeeks`, `weekStart`), each measure on its own row and scale (views, busyness, booked out), never two on one axis.
- **Booked out compares between venues; busyness only as a change.** A booked-out share is the same question (a table for two, 18:00–22:00) asked of every venue, so it is shown as a level next to others'; Google's busyness index is scaled to each venue's own peak, so a competitor's busyness is only ever its change on the month before (points, "+6" / "−3"), never a level beside another venue's. `ReportSignalsService.sections` also returns `competitors` with `demand` (`{ booked, evenings }`, the month's mean over its sampled evenings) and `busyness` (`{ index, previousIndex? }`), each absent without samples, never zero. `ReportDemand.district` / `city` (`{ median, venues }`) is the median of the other venues' booked-out shares on the venue's own sampled evenings (`VenueReservationService.cityEvenings`: one query, latest sample per venue and evening; pure `bookedMedian`, `eveningsBooked`, `meanBooked` in `util/reservations.ts`), absent below `BOOKED_MEDIAN_MIN_VENUES` (5). The report's "Weekend tables" tile lists the competitors' median, the district and the city under the venue's figure (the weekly grid stays the venue's own); the competitor table gains "Weekend tables" and "Foot traffic" columns when any row has them. The prompt gets `weekendTables` only when the venue, a sampled competitor and the district all have figures, and `competitorFootTraffic` as changes in points.
- **Open tables** (`SAMPLE_RESERVATIONS`, Thursdays 19:00 city time, for the coming Friday and Saturday): every open venue of the city whose `Venue.bookingUrl`, Google `reserveTableUrl` or website (in that order, `reservationUrlOf`) is a page we can sample (`reservationPlatformOf`): Google's own booking page (`google`), TheFork or OpenTable. `PipelineService.reservationVenues` orders them won prospects, the month's other report venues, their competitors, then the rest by `leadScore` in tens, each band shuffled, and `RESERVATION_SAMPLE_MAX_VENUES` (600) cuts the list; the run result gives `venues`, `noPlatform`, `capped`, `samples`, `byPlatform`. The evening is 18:00–22:00 in quarter hours (16 slots); `booked` = share of closed slots. **Google's booking page** (`GoogleReserveAvailabilityService`, free, no Apify, no browser): a plain fetch of `google.com/maps/reserve/dine?c=<merchant id>&ld=<YYYYMMDDTHHMMSS>&ps=2&hl=en`, whose first response holds the slot grid; `ld` is read as **UTC** (`zonedTimeToUtc` of 19:00 and 21:00 local: two loads per date, each grid an hour either side); from Europe it needs the `SOCS` consent cookie or it redirects to consent.google.com. The pure `util/google-reserve.ts` reads the date label, one grid per bookable area (slot buttons' `aria-label`: "8:15 PM", "Selected: 8:00 PM"; a closed slot has none; some venues list 5- or 10-minute slots, rounded down to their quarter hour) and the footer's partner ("in partnership with OpenTable", or "Booking providers supply these times" + several, stored sorted and comma-joined); a quarter hour is open when any area lists it in either load; a page showing the date with no grid is 0 open, one showing another date is no sample. Every Google sample rewrites `Venue.reservationPartner`. Pacing: two loads in flight, a jittered 1–2 s pause before each (~1 h for 600 venues); a failed load, a redirect or the captcha ("unusual traffic", `/sorry/`) stops the provider, keeps the samples taken (`PartialAvailabilityError`) and fails the run. `bookingUrl` is the hand-set link (a column, not in `details`, which scrapes merge into), edited on the admin venue page's "Demand and delivery" panel through `PATCH /admin/venues/:id` → `VenueReservationService.setBookingUrl`, which accepts only an http(s) Google booking, TheFork or OpenTable page (400 otherwise); owners don't set it. Quandoo shut down on 30 September 2026 and has no provider. The paid providers split the step's budget: TheFork (`clearpath/thefork-scraper`, restaurant URLs, reviews and photos off, ~$0.006 a venue) gives seven days of bookable windows without a party size (`partySize` null; a date it lists with no window is fully booked, a date it doesn't list is not sampled); OpenTable (`canadesk/opentable`, `process: "ga"`, by exact name, for two at 20:00, kept for hand-set OpenTable links) is one run per date and domain, each charged at least 1,000 units (~$0.26). One platform failing keeps the others' samples and fails the run. The report names the platform "Reserve with Google (via OpenTable)" (`ReportDemand.partner`).
- **Delivery and prices** (`SCRAPE_DELIVERY`, in the monthly run after `REFRESH_VENUE_DETAILS`, $7: Lieferando's actor refuses a cap under $5): Wolt per restaurant from the Wolt link in `Venue.details.orderBy` (`needy_hammock/wolt-restaurant-menu-scraper` direct-URL mode, prices in cents, rating = `ratingScore / 2`); Lieferando for the venues not on Wolt from one city-wide listing (`studio-amba/takeaway-scraper`, which takes no restaurant URL), matched by name and postcode, rating and fees only (its menus come only for a whole search). A month's scrape replaces that month's `MenuItem`s. `pricePosition`: median main course (drinks, desserts, sides, extras left out by `isMainCourse`) against the median of the competitors' medians, else the district's menus; dishes (the report's top five) match menu items as whole-word runs. Price complaints read against tracked competitors with 5+ written reviews.
- **Correlations** (`util/correlations.ts`, pure, `ReportData.correlations`): buzz to reviews (views by week against new Google reviews by week; the week after the most viewed week against the venue's usual pace from the six months before, over months that have any review, so unscraped months don't count as zero), complaint times (by the hour a review was posted, the report month and the five before; a topic is placed only where 3+ of its reviews gather at 1.5× the share of all reviews posted then, since evenings carry most reviews of any kind), paid against organic (median views, reviews in the seven days after). Floors: 10 reviews, 3 posts; below them a relation is absent. Wording is "followed by", never "caused"; the prompt gets the pre-written sentences (`signalsPromptInput`) and is told to quote them.

## Portal

The venue's own product: `/portal` on the site, `modules/portal` on the API (`PortalController`, `PortalService`; imports auth, reports, outreach, venues and social, none of which import it).

- **Who gets in is a brand membership** (`BrandMember`, `BrandMemberService`, `BrandAccessGuard`, all in `auth`: `domain-identity-and-auth`); an admin invites from the prospect page and opens any brand's portal. Every `/portal/brands/:brandId/*` route carries the guard (the portal spec checks it), and a report id of another brand answers 403 `NO_BRAND_ACCESS`, an unknown one 404.
- Routes: `GET /portal/brands` (the person's memberships, each brand with its open branches and cities), `/brands/:id` (the brand card), `/reports` (every city's months, no bodies, each with its city), `/reports/:reportId` (the shared link's DTO: `toOutput`, no `sentAt`), `/reports/:reportId/pdf` (printed per request), `/series?months=&cityId=`, `/patterns?months=&cityId=` (6 by default; one city, the brand's first without `cityId`: its reports, and the branch its newest report there describes for busyness, open tables and own accounts), `GET/PATCH /settings` (the prospect's `contactEmail` and `language` through `OutreachService.forBrand` / `update`; `editable: false` when the brand is no prospect), `/reviews` (paginated; `month`, `stars`, `topic`, `answered`, `language`, `needsReply` = written, ≤3 stars, unanswered, lowest first), `/review-filters` (months, languages, topics with counts, the needs-reply count), `PUT /reviews/:reviewId/replied`, `/posts` (`month`, `platform`; `paid` = ad or promotional, `own` = the post's account is the brand's), `/creators`, `/post-filters`, `GET/POST /competitors` (a place or a whole brand, optionally for one branch; + suggestions while under the cap), `DELETE /competitors/:competitorId` (its list id), `/competitor-search?q=` (places in the brand's cities, each with its brand's branch count).
- **The portal is the whole brand**: reviews, their filters and "I answered it" cover every branch (each review's `branch`: name · street, for a chain), posts and creators are the brand's mentions (`BrandPostsService`), reports are per city (Months shows the city when there are several); Trends and Patterns read one city at a time (`?city=`, `CityPicker`).
- **A review's draft and "answered"**: `answered` is `ownerResponse` or the owner's `markedRepliedAt` (kept even after a scrape confirms it); `replyDraft` shows on unanswered written reviews only, the review's own (the alert's) before a report's. No reviewer names: the entity stores none.
- **The series reads stored reports only** (`util/portal-series.ts`, pure): one row per month, oldest first, ending with the last finished month or a newer report; `null` where a month has nothing; a month without a report but followed by one gets the counts from its `previous`; the all-time rating comes from every report's `ratingTrend`, else the report's own figure. Fields a stored report may lack (`previous`, `ratingTrend`, `competitors`, …) are optional there. Booked out is read from `VenueReservationSample`, not reports (`addBooked`: the venue's month `booked`, each competitor row's `booked`, and `districtBooked`, the district median on the venue's own evenings at 5+ venues; all absent without samples). Busyness is read from `VenueBusyness`, not reports (`addBusyness`: the month's mean index over samples with popular times, the peak of their mean histogram; absent, not zero, for a month without one); own-account followers are read from `BrandAccountSnapshot` (`addOwnAccounts`: each month's accounts with followers, posts and views per post; absent for a month without a snapshot). The series' `calendar` (one `MonthContext` per month) comes from `CityDayService.months`, not from reports; the Trends tab shades holidays and events inside each month's band on the count charts.
- **The patterns read stored reports, never recompute** (`util/portal-patterns.ts`, pure, `PortalService.patterns`): per month the report's `correlations` as stored (a relation absent where the report lacks it, `reportId` null without a report); per week (Mondays, city time, every week touching the window) posts by others and views from the reports' `posts`, new Google reviews from `reviews.list` (TripAdvisor items skipped), booked out from `demand.evenings`, busyness from `VenueBusyness` samples. A week spanning two reports sums both (each report counts only its own month); a week with days in a month without a report is `partial`, one with none has no counts, and a covering report without a review list leaves its reviews unknown — absent, never zero. The page (`[brandId]/patterns`) draws them with the shared `MonthChart` (`components/charts`) (its `tickLabel` / `longLabel` / `tableLabel` / `othersLabel` props put weeks on the x axis): views, reviews, busyness and booked out by week; the buzz ratio and paid-against-organic reviews after a post by month; complaint times as a table; the picked month's sentences above.
- Frontend: `routes/(portal)` (English, its own layout: `setUiLabels`, `lang="en"`), `portal/login`, `portal/(app)` (`requireLogin`; `/portal` opens the only brand or lists several), `[brandId]` (header with the brand switcher and its places, link tabs Report · Months · Reviews · Posts · Trends · Patterns · Settings; Reviews puts "Needs a reply" above the filtered list, each card with copy-the-draft and "I answered it" (undo on the toast); Posts toggles posts and creators; Settings holds the competitor picker), the report through `ReportView` as is, PDFs fetched as a blob with the session's token. Trends (`trends/components`): one measure per chart with the venue in `primary-darker` and competitors as `strip-muted` lines (emphasis, one y axis; a `dashed` comparison is a baseline, the district median on the "Weekend tables" chart under Foot traffic), sentiment as 100% columns in the report's colours, topics and dishes as rows of sparklines on a shared scale; a month chip or a tap picks the month on every chart and opens its report; every chart has a table and arrow-key month stepping.
