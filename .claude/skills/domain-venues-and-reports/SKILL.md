## Rules

- **Every city is a row, never code.** Everything a step, the cron, a query or a prompt knows about a place comes from its `City`: sources (`CitySource`, enabled ones only), `locationQuery`, `tiktokRegion`, `instagramEnabled`, districts and borders, name stopwords, chain deny-list, budgets, `topBrands`, the name the prompts use (extraction and highlights; reports stay English until the `report-language` decision reads `City.language`). No `BERLIN_*`-style constant under `apps/`; a new city is the `/admin/cities` form. Venues, posts, lookups and pipeline runs carry their city (not null); mentions and reviews reach it through the venue or the post, reports carry it (brand × city), prospects reach it through their brand's branches. Matching, `topBrandIds`, `topBrands` (won prospects too), neighbours, competitor suggestions, trends and the lead score stay inside one city: a district name or a namesake in another city never counts. A known venue keeps its city when another city's scrape finds it again, and a new place is created only by a scrape that names its city (`saveGooglePlace(item, now, city)`; `refreshDetails` updates known venues only).
- **Sources keep their yield, from every find**: each time a source finds a post, new or known, the post gains a `PostSource` row (`SocialPostService.upsert`; a scrape's rows of one post are merged with `mergeFinds` first, so two tags or queries of one run are two rows), linked to the `CitySource` it is by `linkPostSources` after the flush (`finishSave`) and whenever a city gains a source, so a source added again gets its history back. Finds that are no city source keep `kind` + `value`: a venue's TikTok search (`venue_search`, `venueSearchQuery`) and Instagram place page (`venue_place`), with the `venue`. `CityService.sourceYield` counts per source over the last three months its posts, those with a matched mention, **the matched posts only it found** (no other enabled source, no venue search and no watched brand profile found them: what turning it off loses) and views (the Sources tab on `/admin/cities/[cityId]`, "Only it found"). Finds before `ALL_FINDS_SINCE` (2026-10-23) kept only a post's first source, so unique counts read high until three months after; the tab says so. Dead sources are disabled, not deleted, so the history stays readable.
- **Hashtags are rows** (`Hashtag`, one per tag across platforms: lowercase, no `#`; `PostHashtag` in the caption's order, written with a new post; the extraction prompt reads them through `hashtagLinks`). `HashtagService.relink` is the one writer of what a tag names, pure, no LLM, over every tag after each save, after a matching run, from `FoodTopicRelinkJob` (re-linker `hashtags`) and from `BrandChangedWorker`: the food topic it names whole (`FoodTopicIndex.exact`: #smashburger is burgers), else the one brand whose name or own handle it is, letters and digits only, 4+ characters, and only once a post carrying it is matched to that brand or by its own account (`hashtagLinks`); a city's name or alias is neither (#berlin is no German food, though "berlin" is a cuisine alias). A hashtag source points at its tag (`CitySource.hashtag`). `CityService.hashtagSuggestions` (`GET /admin/cities/:id/hashtag-suggestions`, the Sources tab's "Hashtags from the data") lists, free, the tags on the city's matched posts of the last three months with 5+ posts of 3+ brands that the city has no source for on that platform, not `ignored` (`PATCH /admin/hashtags/:id`, undo on the toast) and not the city's own name; "Add" creates the source switched off.
- **Suggestions are never enabled by themselves**: "Suggest sources" (hashtags and searches only: a model's blog URLs and handles are guesses, so blogs and creators are added by hand) stores the agent's hashtags and searches (city language and English, existing values skipped case-insensitively) as disabled sources marked `suggested`, the reason in `note`; switching one on or off is its review and clears the flag. "Suggest from venues" saves nothing: each postal code goes to the district most of the city's venues under it are in (a code is listed once), a new name needs 3+ venues, a district without borders gets those it shares a postal code with; the admin ticks what to keep and the list replaces `City.districts`.
- **The month's context is free data, never a cause**: `CityDay` is one row per city and local date, refetched for the last 7 days every morning (the archive revises its newest days; an empty newest day keeps the old figures); holidays are the country's nationwide ones plus `City.region`'s. A weekend is rainy when its Saturday or Sunday had ≥ 1 mm; weather figures are absent under 90 % coverage, so a gap never reads as dry. `ReportData.context` is set at build; the prompt may use it in one clause only to place a quiet or busy month.
- **Google search interest is per term, never across terms**: each term is pulled alone over the 12 months up to the month, so 100 is its own peak; a month is the mean of the weekly points that start in it and have data, needing at least `MIN_WEEKS_WITH_DATA` (2) of them (in Berlin a third of the topics, small cuisines, have data in a stray week only), and no data is `null`, never 0. Seasons move these lines too (ice cream falls every autumn): a "rising" claim beyond month on month needs the same months a year earlier. The terms are every `FoodTopic` with a `trendsTopicId` (else `trendsQuery`) plus won prospects' names (`SEARCH_INTEREST`, before `BUILD_REPORTS`, $2 default). The page draws searches and mention share as two lines on their own axes.
- **Levels across kinds of food come from anchored comparisons, never from the per-term rows** (`FoodTrendsReportService.pullSearches`): Google compares up to five terms on one scale, so every searched kind of food (kinds of place left out) goes in a group of four plus the anchor `ANCHOR_TOPIC_KEY` (pizza), over the 24 months ending with the quarter (one window, so a quarter compares with the same quarter a year earlier on one scale), in `City.region` and Food & Drink. Each group is rescaled so the anchor's mean over the window is 100 (`anchoredLevels`); in a comparison a week's 0 is a real low and counts, a month with no week above 0 is null (below what the comparison resolves); a group whose anchor came back empty is left out, never zero. The actor is `steadyfetch/google-trends-scraper` (`compare: true`, `timeRange` custom, `category` 71 as a number, `surfaces: ['interestOverTime']`; $0.009 a keyword on Free, cap ≥ $0.05), one run a group at `COMPARE_GROUP_CAP_USD`, three side by side: `signalbench`'s actor has no compare mode. Berlin: 28 groups, ~$1.30 a quarter. Whether it takes `/m/` topic ids in compare mode is not yet confirmed by a run.
- **Food is searched as Google topics, in Food & Drink**: a bare English word measures other things in a German region (on 2026-10-18 "german" was translation searches, "thai" massage, "american" American Express, "market" the stock market). A topic id is language independent ("china restaurant" and "chinesisches restaurant" count for `/m/01xw9`) and disambiguated; category `71` strips the rest. Venue names are searched in all categories (`0`): 71 dropped most of a small venue's weeks (Markthalle Neun: nothing). So a pull is two actor runs, each capped at least at the actor's $1 floor (`splitTrendsBudget`). Restaurant-intent phrasings ("thai restaurant") are too sparse at region level (39 of 53 weeks without data in DE-BE) to be the default. Unknown words are skipped; a string with nothing known (Restaurant, Hotel, "/") has no topic. A topic's mention share counts its children (`withParents`: ramen for Japanese) and a mention once per topic, so shares don't sum to 100 (unlike `TrendService`'s tables). A topic's key wins over another's alias; the seed suite asserts no alias is shadowed and every label resolves to its own topic.
- **Topics are edited, never re-keyed**: the key is fixed at creation (`normalizeCuisine` of the given key or label; 409 naming the topic that has it: "Coffee" makes `cafe`, the café's) because stored trends rows carry it; label, type, parent, aliases and Trends fields change. An alias that already names another topic as a whole is refused (400, `FoodTopicIndex.exact`), and so is a parent that sits under the topic. Unmapped words are the city's mention cuisines and *food* venues' categories (`isFoodVenue`) that resolve to nothing (`overview`, the one reader of raw food text). **A topic change reaches everything through `FoodTopicRelinkJob`** (`stately`, one key, queued by `FoodTopicService.create` / `update`): `FoodTopicRelinkService` runs every re-linker its owner registered at module init (`mention-topics`, `menu-item-topics`, `dish-names` (the dish names' topics and the rows', see "A dish name has one food topic")), then refreshes every city's venue tags; pure matching, no LLM. A new re-linker registers there; nothing else re-resolves.
- **Food topics are stored links, never matched on read** (no reader calls `resolve` / `resolveMention` / `resolveMenuItem`; a reader joins the rows and expands parents with `withParents`): a mention's topics are its cuisine's (source `cuisine`, in the order the text names them: the trends' cuisine row is the first), else its venue's Google category's, else for a brand-only mention its brand's most reviewed branch's in the post's city (source `category`); they are re-resolved after extraction, after matching and an admin's correction (a re-match moves a category row), and by the re-link job. A menu item's topics are written with the scrape. A venue's tags are stored computed rows, refreshed for the city (`FoodTopicCityRefreshJob`) after extraction, matching, account matching, a Google Maps save and a delivery scrape, in the pipeline itself before `FILL_COMPETITORS`, `TRACK_SEARCH_RANKS` and `BUILD_REPORTS` (so a report never reads stale tags), for one venue at once after an admin's change, and for every city by the re-link job; every venue of a city gets them, closed and non-food too (brand food and the openings view read those).
- **A venue's tags** (`venueTopics`, run only by `refresh`): its main Google category's topics score 1, its other categories' 0.5, a kind of food its posts by others give `MIN_TAG_MENTIONS` (2)+ times and on `MIN_TAG_SHARE` (25 %)+ of its posts with one scores that share, the same for Google's review tags (`details.reviewsTags`, weighted by their counts; source `reviews`), and a kind of food on `MIN_MENU_ITEMS` (3)+ and `MIN_MENU_SHARE` (25 %)+ of its latest delivery menu's items with one (source `menu`; menus and review tags never give a kind of place); an admin's tag scores 1 and an admin's exclusion removes the topic whatever gives it. The primary (the one kind of food it is known for) is the first cuisine or dish of: the admin's tags, the main category, the posts', the menu's, the review tags' (most first); else the first of any kind in that order ("Cocktail bar": cocktails); so "Asian restaurant" stays Asian even when posts say pho, until an admin tags it.
- **A topic's figures are supply, buzz and demand**: venues = open food venues (`isFoodVenue`) whose tags, with parents, include it ("mainly": its primary, or a child's, is theirs); posts = the month's mentions by others whose stored topics are it or a child, share in points against last month; searches = its stored 12-month line and the month's change. Topics with none of the three are left off the list. The shared `MonthChart` lives in `components/charts` (portal trends and patterns use it too).

- **Trends come from `signalbench/google-trends-scraper`** (Google's JSON endpoints, no browser, ~$0.004 a term; it refuses a cap under $1 per run). `apify/google-trends-scraper` was dropped: its `geo` takes countries only, and for `DE-BE` through explore URLs Google failed nine page loads in ten, ~$0.30 a term in retried proxy traffic. `geo` is `City.region`, else `country`; the window is `customTimeRange`. Its weekly points carry no "has data" flag, so a 0 is too little data (Google's own meaning on a single-term query) and is left out. A term the run returned nothing for (the cap stopped it, Google failed it or has no data for it: the actor returns no item then) is not pulled: its stored months and related queries stay untouched, never set to null.

Runs go one at a time, **oldest queued first** (`nextQueuedRunId`): a job only wakes `RunPipelineWorker`, which works through every queued run, so a resumed run goes before the ones queued after it and a run queued again never waits for a job of its own. A step that fails marks its `PipelineRun` FAILED with the error; rerun it from the Data page.
- **A restart never loses a run or pays twice.** Each step's result is stored as it finishes under `"<n>. <STEP>"`; `execute` skips the keys already there. Each step runs inside `apifyRunContext` (pipeline run id + step key, `apify/util/apify-run-context.ts`); `ApifyService.runActor` `start`s the actor and stores `apifyRunId` (with `inputHash`, `pipelineRun` (the `PipelineRun`, set null when it goes) and `stepKey`) before `waitForFinish`, and inside a context re-attaches to a run an earlier attempt of the same step started with the same input (`earlierAttempt`, not FAILED, not already used by this process) instead of starting one. On boot `PipelineRecoveryService` queues every RUNNING run again (`resumes` + 1; at `MAX_PIPELINE_RESUMES` (5) it fails instead, a crash loop's stop), skipping the runs this process already picked up (`PipelineService.executing`); `RunPipelineWorker` waits for `PipelineRecoveryService.ready` before picking a run, since pg-boss may hand it a job before the hook ran and it would overtake the interrupted run, settles the Apify runs no resumed step will re-attach to with Apify's own status (`settleOrphans`, "Items not stored"), and wakes the worker for runs queued before the restart. On SIGTERM / SIGINT `processShutdown.requested` is set: no new actor run starts (`ShuttingDownError`), no new step starts, and a step that ends during the shutdown is not recorded (it may have swallowed a refused run, e.g. a search that "yields nothing" on failure), so the run stays RUNNING for the next process.

- **Matching**: names are compared after `normalizeVenueName` (accents, punctuation and generic words like "restaurant" and the city's `nameStopwords` (its own name, local words like "imbiss") removed) with trigram similarity, boosted when the creator tagged that place or the hint names its district or street. The monthly `BUILD_REPORTS` builds it only in the run of the last of the brand's monthly-cron cities (`otherCitiesDone`: every other one has a succeeded `MONTHLY` or `BUILD_REPORTS` run of the month; the queue runs one run at a time) and counts the others as `waiting`. - **Brand suggestions save nothing** (`suggestBrands`, worked out on read, most mentioned first): venues of one city that are a brand of their own (one branch), with the same `brandKey` (parentheses, slogans and city stopwords dropped), or a key and longer names starting with it ("Burgermeister Leopoldplatz"), or one website (not a platform page) with the same first word, or one Instagram or TikTok profile whatever the names (reason `same Instagram` / `same TikTok`: the profile is the business's own; on dev Berlin `kinza.berlin`, `thenoodletown` were listed by brands of other names). A profile group whose names share no words is keyed by the profile (`instagram:kinza.berlin`, `isProfileKey`), so its dismissal sticks, and `autoMerge` never merges it: one owner's two concepts may list one profile. A one-word key extends only at `BRAND_PREFIX_MIN_LENGTH` (8) letters and when it is not a common word (inside `COMMON_WORD_NAMES` (2)+ other names not first: "Cafe & Bäckerei Olive"), since a word for food sweeps in strangers ("burger" → "Burger Vision"). A key is one suggestion (the admin page and dismissals are keyed by it): a website group whose shared words are a key already suggested joins that group. Keys of chains and of `BrandDismissal`s are skipped; a dismissal is final. Same-name independents ("Asia Imbiss") still come up: the admin dismisses them.
- **A brand that is one for sure merges by itself, as its venues come in** (`BrandService.autoMerge`, queued per city as `BrandAutoMergeJob` by every `VenueScrapeService` save — city scrape, lookups, brand search, detail pages — on a `stately` queue with the city as `singletonKey`; "Find branches" runs it before it stamps `branchesSearchedAt`). The proof is the site (`util/brands.ts`): `ownSite` reads a Google website as its registrable domain (`registrableDomain`: "pberg.benedict.world" → "benedict.world"), a shared host's own part ("veracruz-berlin.eatbu.com") or an Instagram/TikTok profile; `siteNamedAfter` says whether it is named after the brand (the name, maybe with what sites add — "nilakitchen.de", "loveatfirstbite_berlin"; it holds the name or its first words; or the name holds it), which a listing or delivery site never is ("speisekartenweb.de"), and a single word for food never names anyone ("pizza.de"). First every chain takes the city's lone food venues of its name (`isBrandName`) whose site has the letters of one of its branches' named sites ("losteria.net" = "losteria.de"; "roma-berlin" ≠ "romaberlin"), or no site where the chain has a branch and its name is distinctive (`isDistinctiveBrand`: not only food and place words — "Asia Haus" is not), never one it excluded (`BrandExcludedVenue`). Then each suggestion with `oneBrandSite` becomes a chain: one named site given by 2+ of its venues, no second domain or profile, a profile beside the domain named after the brand too, venues without a site only for a distinctive name. One `BrandChangedJob` for the run; two prospects or a taken key are logged and left to the admin. On dev Berlin (2026-10-19) it merged 126 of 287 suggestions. **An admin's word stays**: `split` dismisses the chain's keys (`BrandDismissal`), and `removeBranch` always excludes the branch (`BrandExcludedVenue`), so neither is undone by the next run.
- **Every Google Maps search saves what it returns** (`VenueScrapeService.searchPlaces` → `saveFound`): known venues are updated from the item, and a new place joins the city when Google's `city` is the city's (`isInCity`; a search with the city in its text can cross the border) and its category serves food (`isFoodVenue`); never an opening (`source: lookup`). Search ranks, OSM lookups, name lookups and brand searches all keep their places; only detail pages, which fetch known place ids, add none. A venue is only ever written from Google items (`saveGooglePlace`).
- **The city-wide list is a capped slice, OpenStreetMap the checklist** (`SYNC_OSM_PLACES` free, `LOOKUP_OSM_PLACES` $10, both monthly: the lookup only pays for places never searched, so once the gap is closed a month costs cents): `SCRAPE_VENUES` stops at 1,000 places a term ("restaurant", "bar", "cafe"), and Google picks which, so places like Baba Pirzola (3,000 reviews) were missing. The sync lists every food amenity and shop inside the city's OSM relation through Overpass (Berlin, 2026-10-21: 13,786 named places against 4,991 venues), stores them as `OsmPlace` and links each unlinked one to a venue within `OSM_MATCH_RADIUS_M` (150 m) whose name is alike (`osmNameScore` ≥ 0.6 over `venueAliases`, or one name holding the other at 5+ letters; closed venues count, a link stays). On dev Berlin it linked 3,092; the wrong ones seen ("Hackescher Hof" to "Hackescher Markt") only spare a place its search. The lookup searches Google Maps for the unlinked places by name and address (`osmSearchString`, one result, no `locationQuery`), sit-down kinds first (`osmLookupOrder`), as many as 80 % of the budget covers at $0.004, stamps `lookedUpAt` (never searched twice, even when Google had nothing that close) and matches again. A place OSM stops listing keeps its row; `coverage` counts only the last sync's (`listed`, `matched`, `notFound`, `toLookUp`), the step results say it. **OSM data never reaches a venue, a report or the portal**: it is a to-do list, so its licence (ODbL) asks nothing of us; showing an OSM field anywhere would need its attribution, and publishing a database built on it its share-alike.
- **Branches are found by a brand search; only those on the brand's site join by themselves** ("Find branches" queues `BrandSearchJob`, worker `BrandSearchWorker`, never retried, since a run takes ~30 s; the page polls `branchesSearchedAt`): `searchBrands` is one `compass/crawler-google-places` run for the brands' names in the city, `BRAND_SEARCH_RESULTS` (40) places each (Berlin, 2026-10-18: Burgermeister 15 branches + 25 look-alikes, Brammibal's 9 + 3, $0.21 both); every place is saved as a lookup (never an opening), the places on the brand's own site join it (`autoMerge`), and `candidates` (on read, any city) offers the other open food venues that are a brand of their own and whose name `isBrandName`, minus the ones it excluded (`BrandExcludedVenue`). Unlike `lookupNames` (3 results a name, repeated only once the name is `LOOKUP_RETRY_MONTHS` stale: why chains were missing) it repeats every time: "Find branches" on the brand page, and `SEARCH_BRANCHES` ($2), which the monthly cron queues after each quarterly `SCRAPE_VENUES` for every chain with a branch in the city.
- **A brand has no ownership or size signal**: who decides there (independent or corporate) is not recorded, and the lead score never weighs how many branches a brand has. Big chains and institutions are handled by the city's `chainDenyList` alone.
- **An admin's correction is final**: `PATCH /admin/mentions/:id` (`MentionMatchingService.correct`) sets `manual` (linked, or unlinked with no venue) or `ignored` (not a venue; never has one), with an optional `matchNote`. `match()`, even with `rematch`, never selects a `manual` or `ignored` mention, so a new matching rule can't undo a fix. A report shows the correction only after it is rebuilt (the Rebuild action on the venue's Reports tab). The picker is `dialogs/admin/ChangeMentionVenueDialog.svelte` (post page, venue Mentions tab); `/admin/posts?unmatchedOnly=true` lists posts with a `none` mention to link by hand. Closed venues are never auto-matched, so posts from before a closure need a manual link.
- **City hashtags pull in other cities**: extraction (told the post's city name) records each mention's `city`; `isInCity` compares it with the city's name and `aliases`. Mentions without a city count as in the city.
- **A brand's own account is not word of mouth, and that is a stored link** (`SocialAccount.brand`), never a name guessed on read: every count of posts by others (`topBrandIds`, the brand list and summary, the lead score, trends, topic mentions, food tags, comments, the report, the portal) leaves out a post whose account is the brand's — SQL through `ownPostSql(post, brandColumn)`, TypeScript through `post.account.brand` (`SummaryMention.own`, `NeighbourPost.own`). So an admin's correction on an account moves every count at once. `summarizeReport` lists own posts separately; the highlights prompt is told so. `topBrandIds` ranks by posts from others in SQL; a post naming two branches counts once.
- **Own accounts are matched once, stored, and an admin's word is final** (`AccountMatchingService.match`, free, after every `MATCH_MENTIONS` run and on a brand change, and "Match now" on the Social page): a branch's Google website that is an Instagram or TikTok profile links it (`website`, creating the account before anything it posted was scraped; one branch's own when another branch of the brand gives a different profile); outreach's `Prospect.instagram` links in `SCRAPE_ACCOUNTS` (`prospect`); every other account of the city goes through `matchAccount` against its open food venues' names and their brands' (`handle`). The scorer strips the city's words (stopwords, names, districts' words), digits at the ends and venue-type affixes (`restaurant`, `cafe`, `official`, `bln`…) off the handle, and compares it with each name whole, without generic words, and without kinds of place at its ends ("Imbiss Olympische Brücke" → "olympischebruecke", in all three umlaut spellings). It **links** an exact, distinctive name of 5+ letters (8+ when it is a trimmed name) that no other brand of the city has, or any exact name the account's posts named (`postedAbout`: brands its mentions were matched to, and the brands of the posts that tagged it) when no other brand they named has it; a near name (trigram Dice ≥ 0.85) or a contained one (the handle is a run of the name's words, or holds the name on its word breaks: "midori" in "east_of_midori", never "vegang" in "vegan.garden") links only when the posts named it. Anything else close is a **suggestion** (`suggestedBrand`), for the Social page's "To review" tab or the brand page. A name made only of food words ("Ramen", "Dönerhaus") is never linked or suggested by itself. On dev Berlin (2026-10-19) this linked 340 accounts by handle and 178 by website, with ~40 suggestions; the old name check had "matched" 774 handles city-wide, many wrongly. The matcher never touches an admin's, a website's, a site's, a profile's or outreach's link, and never a brand it was rejected for (`AccountRejectedBrand`, an admin's "Not theirs", final); its own earlier links it re-decides each run. A chain's branch account: the handle names one branch and not the brand.
- **An account we never saw post is still found three ways** (a brand's own account rarely posts where our scrapes look; on 2026-10-25 4,110 of 4,309 Berlin brands with their own website had no Instagram linked, Munchies among them, whose site links @munchiesde):
  - **Its website links it** (free): the favicon job's home page fetch (cut at 2 MB, the links are often in the footer) also stores the profiles the page links or writes out (`profileLinks`: posts, embeds, the CDN's `/o1/v/…`, app pages and template placeholders such as `instagram.com/deinprofil` are no profile), else those of its contact, Impressum or about page (`contactPageUrls`, `SITE_CONTACT_PAGES` (2), same registrable domain), as `Venue.siteProfiles`; a failed read keeps the stored list, and a venue whose website changed or was never read (`siteProfilesAt` null) is due. `match` then gives each brand its branches' site profiles (`siteProfileVerdicts`): one named after the brand (the matcher links it with the site as evidence, or `siteNamedAfter` the brand or a branch) is linked (`site`) when it is the only one so named on its platform, or an exact name among several ("theboxdonut.berlin" beside "theboxdonutdebrecen", which is suggested); the only profile on a platform that isn't named after the brand is suggested (a footer may credit the web agency); a profile the sites of `GENERIC_PROFILE_BRANDS` (3)+ of the city's brands link is a web agency's credit or a group's account and left (a group links its account from each venue's page on one domain: on dev Berlin `amanogroup` from 7 venues, `studierendenwerkberlin` from 5 canteens; a chain's branches are one brand). A sample of 52 Berlin home pages (2026-10-25) found a profile on 29; sites built entirely in JavaScript show none to a plain fetch.
  - **Creators tag it** (free, Instagram only): `taggedHandles` (caption @-mentions, co-authors, photo tags) of Instagram posts matched to a brand are evidence for that brand like the account's own posts. A tagged handle we have no account for becomes one only when `matchAccount` links it to a brand its post named and the handle carries that brand's whole name at `EXACT_MIN_LENGTH`+ letters (exact, near, or holding it: "eat.kanaan"), never a part of it: a caption's `@[\w.]+` cuts "@Frieda-Schlamassel.de" to "frieda", and "@samurai" or "@garden" is someone else. TikTok's tags are never used: clockworks' `mentions` are display names as the caption shows them ("@Friendly Fish - Berlin"), not handles. A handle never ends in a dot on either platform; `normalizeAccountHandle` drops a caption's full stop. On dev Berlin (2026-10-25) this made and linked 167 accounts; a check of 50 found one doubtful (`erdinger.de`, the brewery, for "ERDINGER am Gendarmenmarkt"). A first, looser pass (TikTok tags, parts of names) had made 274, a third of them wrong.
  - **The same handle on the other platform proves itself** (`FIND_OWN_ACCOUNTS`, weekly, $1): for an own account whose brand (with an open branch in the city) has none on the other profile platform, highest `leadScore` first, as many as the budget covers (~$0.003 an Instagram profile, ~$0.004 a TikTok one at `resultsPerPage: 1`), the handle is looked up there; the profile is linked (`profile`) when its website link or a domain in its bio is on one of the brand's sites (registrable domain), its bio names a branch's street (`hintNamesStreet`), or it links or names (after "IG" / "TikTok") the first account; one only named like the brand is suggested. A missing profile comes back as an item (`error: not_found`, `errorCode: NOT_FOUND`), so a lookup stamps `twinCheckedAt`, found or not; a handle the run never returned stays to look up. Other spellings of a handle are not tried.
- **Creators are people**: every account belongs to a `Creator`. A new account joins the creator of the one account on the other platform with the same letters (`creatorKey`, 6+ letters: `polina_iz_berlina` / `polina.iz.berlina`), at creation only, so an admin's split stays split; an admin merges ("Theirs too") and splits ("Not the same person") on the creator page. Creator counts (the brand list's and summary's `creators`, the lead score's buzz, trends' creators table) count creators, so one person's two platforms count once. A brand's own accounts have creators too (named like the account); creator lists leave out creators whose every account is a brand's unless asked.
- **The month's report brands are `PipelineService.topBrands`**: the top N (`City.topBrands`) of `topBrandIds` plus every `WON` prospect's brand with a branch in the city whatever its rank (`ProspectQueryService.wonBrandIds`), since the report is what a subscriber pays for; each `{ brand, branches }` (its open branches in the city, most reviewed first). Every place step takes every branch (`TRACK_SEARCH_RANKS`, `SAMPLE_BUSYNESS`, `SCRAPE_DELIVERY`, `SCRAPE_REVIEWS` with its TripAdvisor side, `REFRESH_VENUE_DETAILS`); the steps that search by the brand's name run once a brand: `SCRAPE_VENUE_SOCIAL` (one venue a brand carries the search), `SCRAPE_PRESS` (`WebMentionService.searchMonth` takes every branch, runs one query per brand on its first one and stamps `pressSearchedAt` on all), `SCRAPE_COMMENTS` and `SCRAPE_ACCOUNTS` (by brand, through the watch list); `pipeline/tests/pipeline-per-branch.pglite.spec.ts` covers a three-branch chain. A chain's branches cost the per-branch steps about a third more on dev (September 2026: 21 report brands, 28 open branches); competitors are `competitorVenueIdsOf`. A `LOST` or `OPTED_OUT` brand still gets a report when it ranks (the data serves neighbours and trends); only outreach stops. `PipelineModule` imports `OutreachModule` for this, so outreach never imports the pipeline.
- **A brand gets a report per active configuration, each keeping what it was built with** (`ReportConfigService.targets`: every active `BrandReportConfig`, oldest first; none active, the default template's, else `FULL_REPORT_SECTIONS` with no template). Templates are starting points only: a configuration copies a template's sections and is edited for its brand alone, so editing a template never moves a brand, and deleting one leaves its configurations and reports with a null link. An inactive configuration is paused, not deleted: the monthly run skips it, its reports stay, and a rebuild of its own report (`configId`) still works. The configuration chooses what a report shows, never what is collected or stored: `ReportService.buildAll` computes `ReportData` and the reply drafts once and stores them whole on each report, because the portal's series and patterns read every report as the brand's history (`PortalService` takes one report per month, as a month's reports share one data; the patterns would otherwise count a week twice). Each report copies its configuration's `name` and `sections` and links `config` and `template` (`Report` is unique per brand × city × month × config; a default report has no config); the view draws `Report.sections`, and each report gets its own highlights call, given only the data its sections show (`trimForSections`: a finding about a block the report leaves out would point at nothing; social and review figures stay while a cover tile or section shows them). A posts section with `platforms` lists and counts only their posts; the cover still counts every platform. Sections are checked by `toSections`: the cover first, each kind once; the view draws the appendix last wherever it is listed, and own accounts, comments and press right after the posts read as part of them. Each report of the newest month is mailed on its own (`ReportDeliveryService.deliverBuilt`). `build` is `buildAll` returning the first report. Relations to a template or a configuration load with `strategy: 'select-in'`: MikroORM hydrates a joined polymorphic embedded array as null, and the next flush writes the null back. Admins only: templates on `/admin/reports/templates`, a brand's configurations on its Reports tab (`/admin/brands/[brandId]/report-configs/[configId]` edits one; both editors preview a built report laid out as the draft). Four templates are seeded (`Migration20261024172411_seed_report_templates`): Full report (the default), Social buzz, Reviews and reputation, Competitor watch.
- **Reports are data, rendered by the frontend.** `ReportData` is computed once at build; rebuilding replaces it. Highlights must only state numbers present in the data — the input groups noticed changes per review so the model counts reviews, not changes. The view shows them by what changed (`ReportReviewStats.changeThemes`, pure `groupChanges`: per theme the reviews noticing it and how they put it, a catch-all theme and unfiled changes last).
- **Topic counts are sources, not points**: `praise` / `complaints` count the reviews (or posts) that raised a topic, once each, however many points the LLM split it into. The prompt gets them as `"19 of 34 written reviews"` strings so the model can't call them complaints; only written reviews can raise a topic. Dishes count once per post or review, grouped accent-insensitively.
- **What posts and reviews say is rows, never copied brand or venue** (`FeedbackPoint`, `FeedbackService` the one writer): one row per praise point (`positive`) or complaint point (`negative`), with its theme, topic, phrase, the dish it is about and the number it states, plus one dish-only row (no topic, no detail, the dish's own sentiment) per listed dish no point names (same `dishKey`), so a reader counting dishes per source counts what the dish list did, plus one row per change a reviewer noticed since before (`change`, `neutral`, the change as `detail`, each spelling once: `changeRows`), filed under a theme by the `feedback-themes` backlog like any point (its input says `kind: change`), never asked a dish or an amount. **A change row is no praise or complaint**: `praiseOf` / `complaintsOf` leave it out, `changesOf` reads it, and every SQL reader that splits rows by side says `not f.change` (theme counts, the reviews' topic filter and facets, the dish and amount passes); the lead score's "changes noticed" counts reviews with one. `city` and `month` are the source's and never change; brand and venue are joined through the source (`review.venue.brand`, `mention.brand` / `mention.venue`) because matching, an admin's correction, a branch move or a merge changes them: a re-match moves a mention's feedback in every reader with no row rewritten. Own posts are decided at read time (`ownPostSql`), never stored. Writers: `ReviewService.analyse` and `MentionExtractionService.extract` hand the agent's answer to `replaceFor` (re-analysing replaces the rows; deleting the source deletes them). The agents answer `dish` (as written in the dishes list) and `amount` per point (`FEEDBACK_POINT_INSTRUCTION`): only a number the text states, a range its upper end, "an hour" 60, money in euro (`City` has no currency; every city so far is in the euro). `dishKey` is the report's key (accents and plurals folded; a name in another script keeps its own letters) and `foodTopic` the topic of its dish name (`DishName`), so the city's dish table groups by `foodTopic ?? dishKey` and a venue's dish list by `dishKey` alone. A source's sentiment for a dish is its dish-only row's, else its points' on that dish (praised and criticised: `mixed`). **Points stored before a pass existed are read a little each night, never by extracting a source again**: the dish pass asks only food and drinks points of a source that names dishes (a point whose phrase names exactly one of them was linked for free), the amount pass only wait and price points, parsing the phrase first; each marks a point decided (`dishCheckedAt`, `amountCheckedAt`), found or not, so nothing is asked twice; linking a point drops the source's dish-only row of that dish. `realign` after editing the vocabulary: topics follow their theme, points of a removed theme go back to filing. The dish pass answers a dish only when the phrase names it or plainly describes it: general praise ("always fresh", "everything is fresh", "taste was good") is null even when the source names one dish, since the theme × dish rows would count it; on dev (2026-10-23) a hand check of 50 Berlin September links found the phrase's links all right and the pass's about two in three, its misses still general praise tied to one dish.
- **A dish name has one food topic, decided once** (`DishName`, `DishNameService` the one writer): every row naming a dish points at its name's topic (`apply` copies `dish_names.food_topic_id` onto `feedback_points` by `dishKey`), so all spellings of a name count for one topic. A new name is stored by `sync` (from the backlog, the re-link and the admin list; the rows' most used spelling), decided at once by a name match when it is a dish or drink topic or one of its other names (`FoodTopicIndex.dishTopic`), else by `DishTopicAgent` in the `feedback-dish-names` backlog (the most noted first, 100 names a call; it sees the dish and drink topics only, answers a kind of ("Spaghetti Vongole", a misspelt "Spaghettiiiossso": pasta; "vegan döner": kebab; a burger bun no burger), else a suggested label for a missing topic, "Fries", or none for a word too general, "meat"; a suggestion naming a dish or drink topic is dropped, since the agent passed it over: "Königsberger Klopse" suggested "Dumplings"); an admin's choice wins and nothing changes it. The Trends tab groups suggestions across plurals (`dishKey`: "Sauce" and "Sauces"). A writer links a new row through `DishTopics` (its decided name, else the name match), so a name seen before links at once. On a topic change (`relink`): a name that is now a topic or one of its other names takes it over the agent's answer, one whose match is gone or whose topic is no dish or drink waits for the agent again, an agent "none" takes a topic created after it that its suggestion names (creating "Fries" links the names that suggested it), drops a suggestion naming an older one, and waits for the agent again when a dish or drink topic was created after it was decided. On dev (2026-10-24): 6,564 names, 94 by a name match (the old whole-name rule: 4,440 of 15,945 rows linked); the first agent pass (13 batches, ~270k tokens) linked 3,032 more, 9,330 rows (59 %); a hand check of 50 of its links found about 48 right. Most of the rest wait on missing topics: fries, coffee as a drink ("coffee" is the café place type's alias, so a new topic needs another label, "Coffee drinks"), bread, sauces, salads, soups.
- **Every praise and complaint point has a theme from a fixed vocabulary** (`FEEDBACK_THEMES` in `venues/constants/feedback-themes.constant.ts`: ~60 themes under the nine topics, e.g. `price` → `price_level`, `value_for_money`, `price_increase`, `extra_charges`, `price_other`), so "prices went up" and "the dürüm is now €9.50" are one thing and a city can sum them. Free details don't group: on dev (2026-10-21) 4,050 review complaint points had 3,816 distinct details. A theme is what a point is about, never its polarity (`portion_size` praised is generous, criticised small); changes against an earlier visit are themes too (`price_increase`, `quality_change`, `ownership_change`). The agents answer `{ theme, detail, dish, amount }` (`feedbackPointSchema`, with `FEEDBACK_THEMES_INSTRUCTION` and `FEEDBACK_POINT_INSTRUCTION` in their instructions, since the legend in the schema would repeat for praise and complaints) and the row's topic is the theme's (`feedbackRows`), so the two never disagree. A point without a theme (stored before themes, or sent back by `realign`) is filed from its phrase alone by `FeedbackThemeService` (`FeedbackThemeAgent`, 120 phrases a call, newest month first), the daily LLM backlog `feedback-themes`, never by extracting a source again, which would drop a mention's match and corrections; `POST /admin/feedback-themes/backfill` runs `realign` and one batch for a city that can't wait. The vocabulary is code (the schema is typed from it): a new or moved theme is a commit, then that route. A theme the agent picks under another topic moves the point's topic with it. The agents file by wording, so a hint says what its theme is not wherever a word is shared across topics ("a room too hot or too cold is atmosphere" under `temperature`): that is where their misses are. Reports count `praiseThemes` / `complaintThemes` per source next to the topics (`countThemes`), **absent while any point of the set has no theme**, so a theme count is whole or missing, never short; the prompt gets them keyed `"price / price increase"`, the topics' catch-alls left out. Across a city the themes are summed by `ThemeInsightService` (trends), which counts a review or a post with an unfiled point apart ("not filed yet") instead of leaving the table out, since one stray point would blank a whole city.
- **Competitors**: `ReportService.collect` summarizes each competitor's same month with `summarizeReport` and cuts it down with `summarizeCompetitor` into `ReportData.competitors`. `reviewsTracked` says whether we scrape its reviews at all, so an untracked competitor shows "—", never 0. The monthly `SCRAPE_REVIEWS` step adds the report brands' competitors inside the same budget. The prompt compares by name, on percentages, only with tracked competitors and groups of 5+ written reviews; the report's matrices use the same 5-review floor. Stored `ReportData` missing a required field (`competitors`, `creators`, `reviews.list`, `branches`, …) fails the response type: rebuild old reports after changing `ReportData`.
- **Google Maps details** (`Venue.details`, typed `VenueDetails`; read it, don't re-derive it from items): hours, amenities (the actor's `additionalInfo` flattened to section → option → offered), the all-time star distribution, photos count, reservation link, temporarily closed, places tags, `reviewsTags` ("people often mention": all-time counts of what reviews name — dishes, drinks, staff — detail page only; the place page lists them) — and, from detail pages only, popular times (histogram per `Mo`…`Su`, live percent), menu, delivery platforms (`orderBy`), `peopleAlsoSearch` (titles, no place id). Every `saveGooglePlace` merges what the item carries: an empty placeholder (`{}`, `[]`, `null`) counts as absent, so a cheap list scrape never erases a detail page's fields, and amenities merge section by section (a list scrape returns mostly Accessibility). An absent field is unknown, never "none"; popular times are often missing even on a detail page. `detailsAt` is when details last changed from any scrape. The monthly `REFRESH_VENUE_DETAILS` step (before `BUILD_REPORTS`, $2 default) re-scrapes `topBrands` ∪ their competitors by `placeIds` with `scrapePlaceDetailPage: true` and `scrapeOrderOnline: true` (the "Order online" links have their own flag), `maxReviews` / `maxImages` / `maxQuestions` 0 (Q&A is personal data); it also writes the day's rating snapshot. `bin/backfill-venue-details.mjs` filled dev from the old repo's saved list-scrape datasets. Each `ReportBranch`'s `amenities` (`offeredAmenities`: our section order, options alphabetical, since jsonb keeps no key order) and `reviewsDistribution` are optional, absent when unknown; the cover shows a one-branch brand's, a chain's go in each place's header.

- **Models by volume** (each OpenAI account's daily complimentary allowance: 10M tokens on `gpt-5.4-mini`, 1M on `gpt-5.4`; past it the account is blocked for that model, and once every account is, the step fails): the bulk agents run on `openai/gpt-5.4-mini` — `MentionExtractionAgent` (~3,000 tokens a post with the theme legend and topic labels: 1,100 posts took 3.3M on 2026-10-22; 641 posts took 0.9M on the full model before them), `ReviewAnalysisAgent` (batches of 10 reviews, since mini reads a long batch less closely), `VenueMatchAgent`, `CommentIntentAgent` (80 comments a call), `PressClassificationAgent`, `FeedbackThemeAgent`, `FeedbackDishAgent` (40 points a call), `FeedbackAmountAgent` (20 a call, with the source's text): the nightly feedback backlogs. What reaches an owner stays on `openai/gpt-5.4`: `ReportHighlightsAgent`, `ReviewReplyAgent`, `PitchHookAgent` (and `CitySourceSuggestionAgent`); ~20 reports a month fit its 1M. Moving an agent between them is a cost decision: check the volume first.
- **Website icons are plain fetches, never Apify, one per website** (the same home page fetch gives the site's profile links, see "An account we never saw post"): `VenueFaviconCron` runs hourly for up to `FAVICON_RUN_SECONDS` (20 min; a site started by then finishes inside the job's 30), reading due open venues `FAVICON_PAGE` (200) at a time and fetching `FAVICON_CONCURRENCY` (8) websites side by side, each a different host. A due venue's website host (`hostOf`: no `www.`, any scheme or path) brings every other open venue on it into the same refresh, due or not, so a chain's branches on one site share one fetch and one upload: one ImageKit file per icon, one `Image` row per venue on it (`ImageService.duplicate`), and a branch joining later takes the stored file without an upload. The key is the website, not the brand: a brand's branches may have sites of their own. Due order: never looked for first (`saveGooglePlace` clears `faviconCheckedAt` when the website changes), then found ones after `FAVICON_REFRESH_DAYS` (30), then ones where nothing was found after `FAVICON_RETRY_DAYS` (7). A website that is a platform profile (Instagram, Linktree, Lieferando, …: `faviconSite`), an IP or a local name has no icon of its own (`no_site` drops a stored one). Candidates, best first: the page's `<link rel=icon|apple-touch-icon>` by declared size (top `FAVICON_MAX_LINKS`), `/favicon.ico` on the host the page redirected to, Google's `s2/favicons` copy (404 = none). Every fetch has a 10 s timeout, a size cap and checks each redirect hop (`isPublicWebUrl`); a file is an icon by its bytes (`faviconFormat`), not its name or content type. Unchanged bytes (sha256 `faviconHash`) are not uploaded again; a replaced icon's `Image` rows are removed together (`ImageService.removeImages`), and the file goes once no row outside them shares it: removed one at a time, each row would see the others and leave the file behind. A failed fetch keeps the stored icon. ImageKit reads no dimensions from some ICO files: `createImageFromBytes` deletes the upload and throws `UnreadableImageError`, and the next candidate is tried. Any other upload error (missing keys, an outage) is no verdict on the site: the run stops, the venues it didn't finish stay due (no `faviconCheckedAt`), and the job fails after flushing what it stored. **The same bytes on `GENERIC_FAVICON_HOSTS` (3) different hosts are a platform default** (WordPress's logo, Wix's `pfavico.ico`), not the venue's: they are dropped from every venue holding them and the hash is kept, so the count stays and the venue waits the refresh period; a chain's branches on one host count once. The admin venue list, competitor lists and venue header show `favicon`; every route serializing it must populate it (`forceObject` turns an unpopulated relation into `{ id }`, which fails `ImageOutputDto`).
- **Lead score** (0–100, `/admin/venues` column and "Prospects only" at `LEAD_PROSPECT_SCORE` 60, breakdown on the venue Overview tab): five weighted signals in `LEAD_SCORE_WEIGHTS` over the report month and the one before — buzz (posts by others, views, creators as percentiles among venues others posted about, since scrape budgets vary), already spends (own posts, promotional posts by others, website), nameable problem (half the strongest, half the mean of complaint share, unanswered critical reviews, reply rate under 50 %, noticed changes, negative or mixed posts; definitions as in `summarizeReport`), reachable, press (best classified press of the window: a feature 1, a listicle 0.75, a passing mention 0.25; unknown for a venue `SCRAPE_PRESS` never searched in the window, and `missing` says "press not searched"; `press` is optional in `LeadSignals`, since older scores lack it) — plus a new-place bonus. **Without reviews in the window the problem signal is read from the rating** (`LeadInputs.districtRatingPercentile`: the lowest district percentile among its open branches, as the report's benchmark computes it; `cap((50 − p) / 50)` next to the negative-post share, half the strongest, half the mean), **and is unknown (`null`, counted at half) only without one (fewer than 5 district peers); `missing` says "reviews not scraped" either way**: missing data neither sinks nor lifts a venue. `LeadSignal.value` is optional in the type because `serialize({ skipNull })` drops that null inside the jsonb too, and a required `number | null` fails the response type. A name on the city's `chainDenyList` keeps half its score, and `LeadSignals.denyListed` says so (the Sales tab's panel shows it). A brand without an open branch has none. The score is the brand's (`Brand.leadScore`): each city's `SCORE_LEADS` scores the brands with an open branch in the city over all their open branches in every city (posts, reviews, press summed; a phone or website on any branch; new only when every branch is), against the buzz of the city's brands; the venue list shows and sorts by its brand's. The frontend mirrors the threshold in `lib/culinary.ts`.
- Months are `YYYY-MM`, UTC.
- **A shared report link is the token alone**: 16 random bytes as 22 base64url characters, unique; anyone holding it reads the report with no login. Revoking is final (a new link replaces it), and a revoked or unknown token answers the same 404. Every share route goes through `ReportShareService`.
- **Opens are counted from the browser, not from reading the report.** `GET /reports/shared/:token` counts nothing, because the SSR'd `/r/[token]` page and link previews (WhatsApp, Slack, mail scanners) read it too; the page's `onMount` calls `POST /reports/shared/:token/opens`, which skips a viewer with the ADMIN role (`OptionalAuthenticationGuard`, so an admin checking a link — or a browser still holding an admin cookie — doesn't count). The count is one `update … open_count + 1`.
- The public PDF (`/reports/shared/:token/pdf`) is public Chrome work, so `SharedReportsController` keeps the last 8 printed PDFs per report version (id + `updatedAt`) and shares an in-flight print; the repo has no rate limiter. The `/r` page links to it with a plain `<a download>` (a phone handles an attachment better than a blob).
- **Log as sent**: the admin report page's Share panel logs a link through the outreach admin routes (`listProspects({ brandId })`, then `logOutreachTouch` → `OutreachService.log`, kind EMAIL or DM, the link in the body, template `report-link`) when the brand is a prospect that hasn't opted out. `OutreachModule` imports `ReportsModule`, so the reports module never imports outreach.

- **Busyness** (Google popular times as a foot-traffic proxy): `busynessIndex` = mean of the histogram's ten busiest hours, 0–100. Google scales each histogram to the venue's own peak, so the index compares a venue with itself over time, never venues with each other. A sample is a detail-page scrape (`VenueScrapeService.scrapeDetailPages`, which also refreshes `Venue.details`) of the report venues and their competitors; a venue whose page shows no popular times gets a row with `histogram: null`, one the scrape returned nothing for gets none. The report's section ("Did the buzz fill the place?") is absent without a sample with popular times; its weeks are Mondays in the city's time zone (`monthWeeks`, `weekStart`), each measure on its own row and scale (views, busyness, booked out), never two on one axis.
- **Booked out compares between venues; busyness only as a change.** A booked-out share is the same question (a table for two, 18:00–22:00) asked of every venue, so it is shown as a level next to others'; Google's busyness index is scaled to each venue's own peak, so a competitor's busyness is only ever its change on the month before (points, "+6" / "−3"), never a level beside another venue's. `ReportSignalsService.sections` also returns `competitors` with `demand` (`{ booked, evenings }`, the month's mean over its sampled evenings) and `busyness` (`{ index, previousIndex? }`), each absent without samples, never zero. `ReportDemand.district` / `city` (`{ median, venues }`) is the median of the other venues' booked-out shares on the venue's own sampled evenings (`VenueReservationService.cityEvenings`: one query, latest sample per venue and evening; pure `bookedMedian`, `eveningsBooked`, `meanBooked` in `util/reservations.ts`), absent below `BOOKED_MEDIAN_MIN_VENUES` (5). The report's "Weekend tables" tile lists the competitors' median, the district and the city under the venue's figure (the weekly grid stays the venue's own); the competitor table gains "Weekend tables" and "Foot traffic" columns when any row has them. The prompt gets `weekendTables` only when the venue, a sampled competitor and the district all have figures, and `competitorFootTraffic` as changes in points.
- **Open tables** (`SAMPLE_RESERVATIONS`, Thursdays 19:00 city time, for the coming Friday and Saturday): every open venue of the city whose `Venue.bookingUrl`, Google `reserveTableUrl` or website (in that order, `reservationUrlOf`) is a page we can sample (`reservationPlatformOf`): Google's own booking page (`google`), TheFork or OpenTable. `PipelineService.reservationVenues` orders them won prospects, the month's other report venues, their competitors, then the rest by `leadScore` in tens, each band shuffled, and `RESERVATION_SAMPLE_MAX_VENUES` (600) cuts the list; the run result gives `venues`, `noPlatform`, `capped`, `samples`, `byPlatform`. The evening is 18:00–22:00 in quarter hours (16 slots); `booked` = share of closed slots. **Google's booking page** (`GoogleReserveAvailabilityService`, free, no Apify, no browser): a plain fetch of `google.com/maps/reserve/dine?c=<merchant id>&ld=<YYYYMMDDTHHMMSS>&ps=2&hl=en`, whose first response holds the slot grid; `ld` is read as **UTC** (`zonedTimeToUtc` of 19:00 and 21:00 local: two loads per date, each grid an hour either side); from Europe it needs the `SOCS` consent cookie or it redirects to consent.google.com. The pure `util/google-reserve.ts` reads the date label, one grid per bookable area (slot buttons' `aria-label`: "8:15 PM", "Selected: 8:00 PM"; a closed slot has none; some venues list 5- or 10-minute slots, rounded down to their quarter hour) and the footer's partner ("in partnership with OpenTable", or "Booking providers supply these times" + several, stored sorted and comma-joined); a quarter hour is open when any area lists it in either load; a page showing the date with no grid is 0 open, one showing another date is no sample. Every Google sample rewrites `Venue.reservationPartner`. Pacing: two loads in flight, a jittered 1–2 s pause before each (~1 h for 600 venues); a failed load, a redirect or the captcha ("unusual traffic", `/sorry/`) stops the provider, keeps the samples taken (`PartialAvailabilityError`) and fails the run. `bookingUrl` is the hand-set link (a column, not in `details`, which scrapes merge into), edited on the admin venue page's "Demand and delivery" panel through `PATCH /admin/venues/:id` → `VenueReservationService.setBookingUrl`, which accepts only an http(s) Google booking, TheFork or OpenTable page (400 otherwise); owners don't set it. Quandoo shut down on 30 September 2026 and has no provider. The paid providers split the step's budget: TheFork (`clearpath/thefork-scraper`, restaurant URLs, reviews and photos off, ~$0.006 a venue) gives seven days of bookable windows without a party size (`partySize` null; a date it lists with no window is fully booked, a date it doesn't list is not sampled); OpenTable (`canadesk/opentable`, `process: "ga"`, by exact name, for two at 20:00, kept for hand-set OpenTable links) is one run per date and domain, each charged at least 1,000 units (~$0.26). One platform failing keeps the others' samples and fails the run. The report names the platform "Reserve with Google (via OpenTable)" (`ReportDemand.partner`).
- **Delivery and prices** (`SCRAPE_DELIVERY`, in the monthly run after `REFRESH_VENUE_DETAILS`, $7: Lieferando's actor refuses a cap under $5): Wolt per restaurant from the Wolt link in `Venue.details.orderBy` (`needy_hammock/wolt-restaurant-menu-scraper` direct-URL mode, prices in cents, rating = `ratingScore / 2`); Lieferando for the venues not on Wolt from one city-wide listing (`studio-amba/takeaway-scraper`, which takes no restaurant URL), matched by name and postcode, rating and fees only (its menus come only for a whole search). A month's scrape replaces that month's `MenuItem`s. **Menu items are linked to topics when the scrape stores them** (`MenuTopicService.linkItems` over `resolveMenuItem`: the name, else the menu section; a non-main course gives drinks only, a kind of place never; re-linked by the job), and a topic's menu price is the median of each venue's own median for it (a long menu weighs as one venue), districts at `MENU_DISTRICT_MIN_VENUES` (5); menus exist only for report venues and competitors, so the topic page says its figures lean to them. `pricePosition` (optional `topic`, `PricePosition.topic`: the venue's primary food against the same on the other menus, the line to lead with; the report gets it through `MenuTopicService.pricePosition`, which `ReportSignalsService.prices` calls with each branch and its others, so `ReportPrices.menu.topic` is that branch's own primary topic, and `priceSentence` and `ReportPrices.svelte` lead with it only when `othersMedian` is there: another menu lists it): median main course (drinks, desserts, sides, extras left out by `isMainCourse`) against the median of the competitors' medians, else the district's menus; dishes (the report's top five) match menu items as whole-word runs. Price complaints read against tracked competitors with 5+ written reviews.
- **Correlations** (`util/correlations.ts`, pure, `ReportData.correlations`): buzz to reviews (views by week against new Google reviews by week; the week after the most viewed week against the venue's usual pace from the six months before, over months that have any review, so unscraped months don't count as zero), complaint times (by the hour a review was posted, the report month and the five before; a topic is placed only where 3+ of its reviews gather at 1.5× the share of all reviews posted then, since evenings carry most reviews of any kind), paid against organic (median views, reviews in the seven days after). Floors: 10 reviews, 3 posts; below them a relation is absent. Wording is "followed by", never "caused"; the prompt gets the pre-written sentences (`signalsPromptInput`) and is told to quote them.

## Portal

The venue's own product: `/portal` on the site, `modules/portal` on the API (`PortalController`, `PortalService`; imports auth, reports, outreach, venues and social, none of which import it).

- **Who gets in is a brand membership** (`BrandMember`, `BrandMemberService`, `BrandAccessGuard`, all in `auth`: `domain-identity-and-auth`); an admin invites from the prospect page and opens any brand's portal. Every `/portal/brands/:brandId/*` route carries the guard (the portal spec checks it), and a report id of another brand answers 403 `NO_BRAND_ACCESS`, an unknown one 404.
- Routes: `GET /portal/brands` (the person's memberships, each brand with its open branches and cities), `/brands/:id` (the brand card), `/reports` (every city's months, no bodies, each with its city), `/reports/:reportId` (the shared link's DTO: `toOutput`, no `sentAt`), `/reports/:reportId/pdf` (printed per request), `/series?months=&cityId=`, `/patterns?months=&cityId=` (6 by default; one city, the brand's first without `cityId`: its reports; `branchId` picks the branch whose rating, busyness and open tables they show, the most reviewed by default, 404 for one that isn't an open branch there; both answer `branches` and `branchId` for the picker), `GET/PATCH /settings` (the prospect's `contactEmail` and `language` through `OutreachService.forBrand` / `update`; `editable: false` when the brand is no prospect), `/reviews` (paginated; `month`, `stars`, `topic`, `answered`, `language`, `needsReply` = written, ≤3 stars, unanswered, lowest first), `/review-filters` (months, languages, topics with counts, the needs-reply count), `PUT /reviews/:reviewId/replied`, `/posts` (`month`, `platform`; `paid` = ad or promotional, `own` = the post's account is the brand's), `/creators`, `/post-filters`, `GET/POST /competitors` (a place or a whole brand, optionally for one branch; + suggestions while under the cap), `DELETE /competitors/:competitorId` (its list id), `/competitor-search?q=` (places in the brand's cities, each with its brand's branch count).
- **The portal is the whole brand**: reviews, their filters and "I answered it" cover every branch (each review's `branch`: name · street, for a chain), posts and creators are the brand's mentions (`BrandPostsService`), reports are per city (Months shows the city when there are several); Trends and Patterns read one city at a time (`?city=`, `CityPicker`, which drops the branch) and a chain's place charts (rating, foot traffic, weekend tables) one branch at a time (`?branch=`, `BranchPicker`, the most reviewed by default; the charts' notes name the branch, and a stale `?branch=` falls back to the default). A stored report without the picked branch (built before it joined) gives that branch nothing for its month: another branch's figures never stand in; the brand's counts stay the brand's.
- **A review's draft and "answered"**: `answered` is `ownerResponse` or the owner's `markedRepliedAt` (kept even after a scrape confirms it); `replyDraft` shows on unanswered written reviews only, the review's own (the alert's) before a report's. No reviewer names: the entity stores none.
- **The series reads stored reports only** (`util/portal-series.ts`, pure): one row per month, oldest first, ending with the last finished month or a newer report; `null` where a month has nothing; a month without a report but followed by one gets the counts from its `previous`; the all-time rating comes from every report's `ratingTrend`, else the report's own figure. Fields a stored report may lack (`previous`, `ratingTrend`, `competitors`, …) are optional there. Booked out is read from `VenueReservationSample`, not reports (`addBooked`: the venue's month `booked`, each competitor row's `booked`, and `districtBooked`, the district median on the venue's own evenings at 5+ venues; all absent without samples). Busyness is read from `VenueBusyness`, not reports (`addBusyness`: the month's mean index over samples with popular times, the peak of their mean histogram; absent, not zero, for a month without one); own-account followers are read from `BrandAccountSnapshot` (`addOwnAccounts`: each month's accounts with followers, posts and views per post; absent for a month without a snapshot). The series' `calendar` (one `MonthContext` per month) comes from `CityDayService.months`, not from reports; the Trends tab shades holidays and events inside each month's band on the count charts.
- **The patterns read stored reports, never recompute** (`util/portal-patterns.ts`, pure, `PortalService.patterns`): per month the report's `correlations` as stored (a relation absent where the report lacks it, `reportId` null without a report); per week (Mondays, city time, every week touching the window) posts by others and views from the reports' `posts`, new Google reviews from `reviews.list` (TripAdvisor items skipped), booked out from `demand.evenings`, busyness from `VenueBusyness` samples. A week spanning two reports sums both (each report counts only its own month); a week with days in a month without a report is `partial`, one with none has no counts, and a covering report without a review list leaves its reviews unknown — absent, never zero. The page (`[brandId]/patterns`) draws them with the shared `MonthChart` (`components/charts`) (its `tickLabel` / `longLabel` / `tableLabel` / `othersLabel` props put weeks on the x axis): views, reviews, busyness and booked out by week; the buzz ratio and paid-against-organic reviews after a post by month; complaint times as a table; the picked month's sentences above.
- Frontend: `routes/(portal)` (English, its own layout: `setUiLabels`, `lang="en"`), `portal/login`, `portal/(app)` (`requireLogin`; `/portal` opens the only brand or lists several), `[brandId]` (header with the brand switcher and its places, link tabs Report · Months · Reviews · Posts · Trends · Patterns · Settings; Reviews puts "Needs a reply" above the filtered list, each card with copy-the-draft and "I answered it" (undo on the toast); Posts toggles posts and creators; Settings holds the competitor picker), the report through `ReportView` as is, PDFs fetched as a blob with the session's token. Trends (`trends/components`): one measure per chart with the venue in `primary-darker` and competitors as `strip-muted` lines (emphasis, one y axis; a `dashed` comparison is a baseline, the district median on the "Weekend tables" chart under Foot traffic), sentiment as 100% columns in the report's colours, topics and dishes as rows of sparklines on a shared scale; a month chip or a tap picks the month on every chart and opens its report; every chart has a table and arrow-key month stepping.
