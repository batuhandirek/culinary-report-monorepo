## Rules

- **The pipeline is operations that govern themselves** (the user's call, 2026-11-06: no central workflow, no ordering by the clock): each operation is its own folder and workflow, its schedule starts it for every cron city, and what it depends on it calls by that dependency's one run id (`runOperation`), never by waiting on, polling or waking another run. Its steps only call domain services: a query or a write belongs to the module owning the data (`BrandService.openBranchesIn`, `CompetitorService.competitorVenuesOf`, `VenueReservationService.sampleOrder`, `ReviewService.scrapeReportMonth`, `MentionExtractionService.saveReading`, …), and an agent's use (its input, its call, merging its answers) belongs to the operation that calls it. A step is named after its `PipelineStepEnum` value. Suites commented out while the tests are left alone: `pipeline/tests/*.pglite.spec.ts` (they drove the removed `PipelineService`; task `pipeline-operation-suites`), `social/tests/account-followers.pglite.spec.ts`, `account-tracking.pglite.spec.ts`, `own-account-discovery.pglite.spec.ts`, `mentioned-profiles.pglite.spec.ts`, `durable-workflows/tests/llm.workflow.spec.ts`.
- **Every city is a row, never code.** Everything a step, the cron, a query or a prompt knows about a place comes from its `City`: sources (`CitySource`, enabled ones only), `locationQuery`, `tiktokRegion`, `instagramEnabled`, districts and borders, name stopwords, chain deny-list, budgets, `topBrands`, the name the prompts use (extraction, the analysis and the editor; reports stay English until the `report-language` decision reads `City.language`). No `BERLIN_*`-style constant under `apps/`; a new city is the `/admin/cities` form. Venues, posts, lookups and pipeline runs carry their city (not null); mentions and reviews reach it through the venue or the post, reports carry it (brand × city), prospects reach it through their brand's branches. Matching, `topBrandIds`, `topBrands` (won prospects too), neighbours, competitor suggestions, trends and the lead score stay inside one city: a district name or a namesake in another city never counts. A known venue keeps its city when another city's scrape finds it again, and a new place is created only by a scrape that names its city (`savePlaces` with a city; the detail pages, `newPlaces: 'none'`, update known venues only).
- **Sources keep their yield, from every find**: each time a source finds a post, new or known, the post gains a `PostSource` row (`SocialPostService.upsert`; a scrape's rows of one post are merged with `mergeFinds` first, so two tags or queries of one run are two rows), linked to the `CitySource` it is by `linkPostSources` after the flush (`finishSave`) and whenever a city gains a source, so a source added again gets its history back. Finds that are no city source keep `kind` + `value`: a venue's TikTok search (`venue_search`, `venueSearchQuery`) and Instagram place page (`venue_place`), with the `venue`. `CityService.sourceYield` counts per source over the last three months its posts, those with a matched mention, **the matched posts only it found** (no other enabled source, no venue search and no watched brand profile found them: what turning it off loses) and views (the Sources tab on `/admin/cities/[cityId]`, "Only it found"). Finds before `ALL_FINDS_SINCE` (2026-10-23) kept only a post's first source, so unique counts read high until three months after; the tab says so. Dead sources are disabled, not deleted, so the history stays readable.
- **Hashtags are rows** (`Hashtag`, one per tag across platforms: lowercase, no `#`; `PostHashtag` in the caption's order, written with a new post; the extraction prompt reads them through `hashtagLinks`). `HashtagService.relink` is the one writer of what a tag names, pure, no LLM, over every tag after each save, after a matching run, from `TopicRelinkJob` (re-linker `hashtags`) and from `BrandChangedWorker`: the food topic it names whole (`TopicIndex.exact`: #smashburger is burgers), else the one brand whose name or own handle it is, letters and digits only, 4+ characters, and only once a post carrying it is matched to that brand or by its own account (`hashtagLinks`); a city's name or alias is neither (#berlin is no German food, though "berlin" is a cuisine alias). A hashtag source points at its tag (`CitySource.hashtag`). `CityService.hashtagSuggestions` (`GET /admin/cities/:id/hashtag-suggestions`, the Sources tab's "Hashtags from the data") lists, free, the tags on the city's matched posts of the last three months with 5+ posts of 3+ brands that the city has no source for on that platform, not `ignored` (`PATCH /admin/hashtags/:id`, undo on the toast) and not the city's own name; "Add" creates the source switched off.
- **Suggestions are never enabled by themselves**: "Suggest sources" (hashtags and searches only: a model's blog URLs and handles are guesses, so blogs and creators are added by hand) stores the agent's hashtags and searches (city language and English, existing values skipped case-insensitively) as disabled sources marked `suggested`, the reason in `note`; switching one on or off is its review and clears the flag. "Suggest from venues" saves nothing: each postal code goes to the district most of the city's venues under it are in (a code is listed once), a new name needs 3+ venues, a district without borders gets those it shares a postal code with; the admin ticks what to keep and the list replaces `City.districts`.
- **The month's context is free data, never a cause**: `CityDay` is one row per city and local date, refetched for the last 7 days every morning (the archive revises its newest days; an empty newest day keeps the old figures); holidays are the country's nationwide ones plus `City.region`'s. A weekend is rainy when its Saturday or Sunday had ≥ 1 mm; weather figures are absent under 90 % coverage, so a gap never reads as dry. `ReportData.context` is set at build; the prompt may use it in one clause only to place a quiet or busy month.
- **Google search interest is per term, never across terms**: each term is pulled alone over the 12 months up to the month, so 100 is its own peak; a month is the mean of the weekly points that start in it and have data, needing at least `MIN_WEEKS_WITH_DATA` (2) of them (in Berlin a third of the topics, small cuisines, have data in a stray week only), and no data is `null`, never 0. Seasons move these lines too (ice cream falls every autumn): a "rising" claim beyond month on month needs the same months a year earlier. The terms are every `Topic` with a `trendsTopicId` (else `trendsQuery`) plus won prospects' names (`SEARCH_INTEREST`, `search-interest.month`, $2 default). The page draws searches and mention share as two lines on their own axes.
- **Levels across kinds of food come from anchored comparisons, never from the per-term rows** (`FoodTrendsReportService.startPull` / `savePull`, the `FoodTrendsPullWorkflow`): Google compares up to five terms on one scale, so every searched kind of food (kinds of place left out) goes in a group of four plus the anchor `ANCHOR_TOPIC_KEY` (pizza), over the 24 months ending with the quarter (one window, so a quarter compares with the same quarter a year earlier on one scale), in `City.region` and Food & Drink. The blog is the post's account (platform `blog`, handle its host without `www.`, `normalizeHandle`), never a brand's own (`AccountMatchingService` skips blogs; own accounts are `ProfilePlatform`s), and joins the creator of an Instagram or TikTok account with the same letters without its top-level domain (`creatorKeyOf`: `berlinfoodstories.com` = `berlinfoodstories`). An article has no views, likes or embed: views show "—" (`viewsLabel`), it never is a highlight's top post (`embedUrlOf` null), and it ranks last wherever views order posts. Extraction reads it as an article (`Platform: blog article on <host>`, the text) and names every venue it says something about, so a list of eleven restaurants mentions eleven. An aggregator blog (taste & stories' radar cites tip, Cee Cee, Berlin Food Stories) counts again what it cites. On dev Berlin (2026-10-22) the nine blogs gave 266 articles over six months.
- **Matching**: names are compared after `normalizeVenueName` (accents, punctuation and generic words like "restaurant" and the city's `nameStopwords` (its own name, local words like "imbiss") removed) with trigram similarity, boosted when the creator tagged that place or the hint names its district or street. A sure name the location hint places elsewhere is never accepted in code (`hintElsewhere`: the hint names a district or street the city's venues are in, and none of the brand's branches is there): the LLM decides, since a square names a street near the branch ("near Rosenthaler Platz" for Torstraße) and a border area sits in the next borough; a hint naming nothing the venues know, or calling itself implied, speaks against nothing. Auto-accept at 0.9 and 0.1 ahead of the runner-up; otherwise the LLM picks among up to 8 candidates (`MAX_CANDIDATES`: one-branch namesakes must not crowd the meant one out) or says none; a sure chain whose hint the code couldn't place (a hint that is only the city's name asks nothing) is asked for the branch, and its none (no branch fits) drops the match; an answer naming one of the chain's branches by id is the chain (a chain whose branch is unknown is offered as the brand, with its branch count and its branches' `locations`, and the post's `author` goes along: a mention giving an address none of a candidate's branches is on, or posted by an account spelling another name, is another venue — "Vegan Garden, Frankfurter Allee 21" by @vegan.garden.berlin is not the chain Vegang). A wrong LLM match also hides the place from `LOOKUP_VENUES`, which searches only unmatched (`none`) names. Only food and drink categories are candidates (`isFoodVenue`); a chain's branches are one (see the brand rule). A mention in another city is `other_city` and never retried; a `none` is retried only after venues were added later than its last attempt (a lookup).
- **The brand is the unit; the venue is the address.** Every venue is a branch of exactly one `Brand` (a brand of its own until an admin merges it into a chain). Reports (brand × city × month), prospects (one per brand), portal access (`BrandMember`), the lead score, the monthly top list, competitors and the brand-name searches are keyed by the brand; Own accounts are the brand's too (`SocialAccount.brand`, several, a branch's own marked). Google data, reviews, snapshots, busyness, open tables, delivery, status changes, press and food-topic tags stay on the venue. There is no main branch, and no branch stands in for the brand's place figures: the pipeline samples every open branch of a report brand (busyness, delivery, TripAdvisor) and the report and portal show each branch's on its own, never summed or averaged across branches (each busyness index is scaled to its own place's peak; a rank or a neighbourhood belongs to one address). Where one contact must be picked (outreach's phone and website) or a suggestion needs an anchor (`CompetitorService.suggest`), it is the most reviewed open branch, worked out when needed (`busiestBranch`, `BrandService.branchesIn(...)[0]`), never stored.

- **Matching gives the brand always, the branch when known**: `Mention.brand` is set on every match, `Mention.venue` only when the post says which branch (a brand-only mention has no venue; `match` retries `brand: null`). Candidates carry `brandId`, `brandName` and `branchCount` (the brand's open branches in the city); a one-branch brand is a plain venue. A chain's branches are one candidate (`rankCandidates`): the branch is known when the creator tagged exactly one, or — when the post names more than the brand ("Burgermeister Kottbusser Tor") — the closest name is `BRANCH_LEAD` (0.05, uncapped) ahead, or — when it names only the brand — a hint naming one branch's district or street puts it that far ahead (on the name alone a branch called just like the brand would win). A street counts without its house number and with "Straße" / "Str." and "Platz" / "Pl." spelled one way (`streetKey`, `hintNamesStreet` in `util/venue-names.ts`; keys under 5 letters never count). **What the code can't place, the LLM may**: each chain location it is shown carries the branch id, and `VenueMatchAgent` returns `branchId` (the street, the neighbourhood it is in, a landmark next to it; null when the post names several, none or only the city). A sure name match to a chain (no LLM for the name) whose hint the code couldn't place gets one call with that chain alone; a hint with no words ("/", a postcode) gets none. A hint naming the streets of two or more branches gets no call, and any `branchId` for it is dropped (`namesSeveralBranches`): mini picked one of the listed addresses in 5 of 7 such posts despite the prompt. A `branchId` of another brand is ignored. Extraction's `locationHint` is only what the post states; on 2026-10-19 ten dev hints were the model's own guesses ("Schöneberg (implied from venue reputation, not in source)"), which the match agent is told to ignore. Counting by brand (`topBrandIds`, the lead score, the report, the portal's posts, trends) reads `m.brand_id` with the post's city (`p.city_id`); reading by place (districts, neighbours, a venue's tags) reads `m.venue_id` and leaves brand-only mentions out. A brand change rematches both brands' non-admin mentions in code (`rematchBrands`, from the `brand-changed` job, no LLM); a close call among them keeps its match with `matchedAt` cleared, so the city's next matching run asks the agent.
- **Merging is the only way to make a chain** (`BrandService.merge`, the Suggested tab's confirm, and `addBranches`): the brand that stays has the prospect, else the most reports, else the biggest venue; the others' venues, mentions, reports (a clash of city and month keeps the kept brand's), prospect, members (no doubles), competitors (no doubles; competition inside the new chain goes; rivals pointing at a merged brand point at the chain), the places a merged brand excluded and the accounts said not to be a merged brand's (they are not the chain's either, except the chain's own account) move over in one transaction (`absorb`), search interest rows are dropped, and the brands are deleted. Two prospects among them is 409: take one off first. `removeBranch` and `split` give the places brands of their own (named like them, their own mentions follow them); the records stay with the old brand; a brand's last branch can't be taken off.
- **Searches by name use the brand's name** (press, TikTok search, Google Trends for won brands: `venueSearchName` with `brand` populated), since `@burgermeister` is not found in "Burgermeister Schlesisches Tor" and a one-branch brand may be renamed.
- **Competitors are a brand's** (`BrandCompetitor`, `CompetitorService`): each a place or a whole rival brand, for the whole brand or for one branch (a chain's branch competes locally). A place or rival is on a brand's list once; `MAX_COMPETITORS` (5) per subject (the brand-wide list, each branch's own). Never one of the brand's own branches. A report in a city compares the brand-wide ones there plus the branch-only ones whose branch is there (`inCity`); a rival counts with its open branches in the city together (`ReportCompetitor.branches`), a branch-only one is labelled (`against`) and the prompt compares it with that branch.
- **A brand's report is all its branches in one city** (`ReportService.build(brandId, cityId, month)`): `social`, `posts`, `ownPosts`, `reviews`, `dishes` and `previous` cover every open branch there (brand-only mentions included), and so do `creators`, `creatorsNearby` (over every branch's district), `competitors`, `correlations` (the brand's posts against every branch's Google reviews; complaint times carry popular times only for a one-branch brand), `ownAccounts`, `comments` and `press` (the brand's: `WebMentionService.forReport(brandId, cityId, month)`). `ReportData.brand` is the business (`id`, `name`, `category` most of its branches share, `brandOnlyPosts`) and `ReportData.branches` holds one `ReportBranch` per open branch, most reviewed first (a one-branch brand has one): where it is, its own month cut like a competitor from the posts that named it and its reviews, its Google details (`googleUrl`, `amenities`, `reviewsDistribution`), `ratingTrend`, `neighbours` (the district's other food venues by posts by others, each post once per venue, so a neighbour's figures match its own report's place row) and its place sections (`busyness`, `demand`, `prices`, `searchRanks`, `neighbourhood`, `ratingBenchmark`), its prices and its weekend-tables comparison against the competitors picked for the whole brand or for it (`ReportCompetitor.branchId`). Nothing in `ReportData` is "described": no branch stands in for another. The view draws the brand first ("Branch by branch", `views/report/ReportBranches.svelte`, for a chain), then each place's sections, one place after another (a "Place n of N" header for a chain, which opens a new page in the PDF so it never strands at a page foot), every place in the PDF; the cover's all-time rating is a chain's stars over all its places' reviews. The prompt gets `places` (each place's figures, never compared or summed across places) and, for a chain, `brand.postsNamingNoBranch`; it compares branches on percentages among those with 5+ written reviews and never credits `postsNamingNoBranch` to one. **A brand in several cities gets a report per city, or one across all of them** (`Brand.reportScope` `all`, the brand page's "Reports" picker): `build(brandId, null, month)` covers every open branch (posts of every city, `ReportBranch.city`), its `Report.city` is null (Postgres lets two null keys through, so `build` looks the row up first), and it has no `data.city` (one city's peers don't compare with a brand across cities); each branch's place figures are in its own city, there is no city calendar (`context`), and competitors are `inCities` (a rival counts with its branches in all of them). Reports are built by hand only (the brand page's "Build report"), so the admin builds the across-cities one when every city's month is in. The portal's series and patterns read the across-cities reports for such a brand (no city picker); the mail goes out for the newest of the same scope.
- **Brand suggestions save nothing** (`suggestBrands`, worked out on read, most mentioned first): venues of one city that are a brand of their own (one branch), with the same `brandKey` (parentheses, slogans and city stopwords dropped), or a key and longer names starting with it ("Burgermeister Leopoldplatz"), or one website (not a platform page) with the same first word, or one Instagram or TikTok profile whatever the names (reason `same Instagram` / `same TikTok`: the profile is the business's own; on dev Berlin `kinza.berlin`, `thenoodletown` were listed by brands of other names). A profile group whose names share no words is keyed by the profile (`instagram:kinza.berlin`, `isProfileKey`), so its dismissal sticks, and `autoMerge` never merges it: one owner's two concepts may list one profile. A one-word key extends only at `BRAND_PREFIX_MIN_LENGTH` (8) letters and when it is not a common word (inside `COMMON_WORD_NAMES` (2)+ other names not first: "Cafe & Bäckerei Olive"), since a word for food sweeps in strangers ("burger" → "Burger Vision"). Keys of chains and of `BrandDismissal`s are skipped; a dismissal is final. Same-name independents ("Asia Imbiss") still come up: the admin dismisses them.

First every chain takes the city's lone food venues of its name (`isBrandName`) whose site has the letters of one of its branches' named sites ("losteria.net" = "losteria.de"; "roma-berlin" ≠ "romaberlin"), or no site where the chain has a branch and its name is distinctive (`isDistinctiveBrand`: not only food and place words — "Asia Haus" is not), never one it excluded (`BrandExcludedVenue`). Then each suggestion with `oneBrandSite` becomes a chain: one named site given by 2+ of its venues, no second domain or profile, a profile beside the domain named after the brand too, venues without a site only for a distinctive name. One `BrandChangedJob` for the run; two prospects or a taken key are logged and left to the admin. On dev Berlin (2026-10-19) it merged 126 of 287 suggestions. **An admin's word stays**: `split` dismisses the chain's keys (`BrandDismissal`), and `removeBranch` always excludes the branch (`BrandExcludedVenue`), so neither is undone by the next run.
- **A search whose order is read runs alone, on a map of the city** (the places atom's `map` mode: the dish searches). `compass/crawler-google-places` outputs each place once per run (checked 2026-11-05: "pizza Mitte Berlin" then "pasta Mitte Berlin" in one run leaves Piazza Rossa out of pasta, and the item `rank`s skip it; no input turns it off): in the one shared run of 2026-10-25, 403 of 528 dish searches had lost a place. The runs that only save places (the city list, lookups, brand and OSM searches) still share one run: a place another search found is saved all the same.
- **Every Google Maps search saves what it returns** (the places atom's save step, `VenueScrapeService.savePlaces` with `newPlaces: 'food-in-city'`): known venues are updated from the item, and a new place joins the city when Google's `city` is the city's (`isInCity`; a search with the city in its text can cross the border) and its category serves food (`isFoodVenue`); never an opening (`source: lookup`). Dish searches, OSM lookups, name lookups and profile-address searches keep their places that way; the city's own list and a brand search keep every place (`'all'`); only detail pages, which fetch known place ids, add none (`'none'`). A venue is only ever written from Google items (`saveGooglePlace`).
- **The city-wide list is a capped slice, OpenStreetMap the checklist** (`SYNC_OSM_PLACES` free, `LOOKUP_OSM_PLACES` $10, both monthly: the lookup only pays for places never searched, so once the gap is closed a month costs cents): `SCRAPE_VENUES` stops at 1,000 places a term ("restaurant", "bar", "cafe"), and Google picks which, so places like Baba Pirzola (3,000 reviews) were missing. The sync lists every food amenity and shop inside the city's OSM relation through Overpass (Berlin, 2026-10-21: 13,786 named places against 4,991 venues), stores them as `OsmPlace` and links each unlinked one to a venue within `OSM_MATCH_RADIUS_M` (150 m) whose name is alike (`osmNameScore` ≥ 0.6 over `venueAliases`, or one name holding the other at 5+ letters; closed venues count, a link stays). On dev Berlin it linked 3,092; the wrong ones seen ("Hackescher Hof" to "Hackescher Markt") only spare a place its search. The lookup (`OsmPlaceService.planLookups`, a `places.run` of typed searches, `saveLookups`) searches Google Maps for the unlinked places by name and address (`osmSearchString`, one result, no `locationQuery`), sit-down kinds first (`osmLookupOrder`), as many as 80 % of the budget covers at $0.004, stamps `lookedUpAt` (never searched twice, even when Google had nothing that close) and matches again. A place OSM stops listing keeps its row; `coverage` counts only the last sync's (`listed`, `matched`, `notFound`, `toLookUp`), the step results say it. **OSM data never reaches a venue, a report or the portal**: it is a to-do list, so its licence (ODbL) asks nothing of us; showing an OSM field anywhere would need its attribution, and publishing a database built on it its share-alike.
- **Branches are found by a brand search; only those on the brand's site join by themselves** (the operation `brand-branch-search`, `pipeline/brand-branch-search/`: "Find branches" starts `brand-branch-search.brand` from `POST /admin/brands/:id/searches` (`AdminBrandBranchSearchController`, one in flight per brand and city, `$1`), never awaited: a run takes ~30 s and the page polls `branchesSearchedAt`): the search is one `places.run` on the city's grid for the brands' names, `BRAND_SEARCH_RESULTS` (40) places each (Berlin, 2026-10-18: Burgermeister 15 branches + 25 look-alikes, Brammibal's 9 + 3, $0.21 both), every place saved as a lookup (`newPlaces: 'all'`, never an opening); then `BrandService.saveBranchSearch` runs `autoMerge` (the places on the brand's own site join it), stamps each searched brand as it is now (a brand of one branch may itself have joined a chain), and counts `candidates`: the other open food venues named like the brand (`isBrandName`), minus the ones it excluded (`BrandExcludedVenue`), which `candidates` (on read, any city) offers on its page. Deleting a leaf aspect (`TopicService.remove`) re-points its aspect parts at its parent by SQL before the row goes, so counts stay whole without an LLM; a term is never deleted (a food part points at it): an alias dropped in the dialog is reopened (`setAliases`), and `StatementPart.term` cascades only in theory. **The vocabulary is rows, edited in the admin's topic dialog** (`TopicService.create` / `update`: key = `aspectKey` of the label (`tip_prompts`), parent an aspect, `hint`, no aliases, no Trends fields; `remove` behind `DELETE /admin/topics/:id` deletes a leaf aspect, never a kind of food, a root or one with children); every change queues the relink job. The agent files by wording, so a hint says what its aspect is not wherever a word is shared across roots ("a room too hot or too cold is atmosphere" under `temperature`): that is where its misses are. Aspects are never in the cuisine lookup (`TopicIndex.food` is what cuisines, categories, menus and hashtags resolve to; `all()` returns both, so a caller that means food filters). Reports count `praiseThemes` / `complaintThemes` per source next to the roots (`countThemes`), **absent while any claim of the set has no aspect**, so a count is whole or missing, never short; the prompt gets them keyed `"price / price increase"` (`TopicIndex.promptLabel`), the roots left out; labels travel in `ReportData.aspects` (`{ key, label, root }` per aspect, from the index at build; absent in older reports, whose view shows the key) since the report view has no admin API. The pure readers (`summarize-report`, `city-themes`, `editor-pack`, `report-observations`, `report-playbooks`, `theme-counts`, `landing`) take the index (or a `Pick` of it, `ReportAspects`) as a parameter; the services fetch it once per build (`ReportService.compute`, `ReportEvidenceService.context` → `EvidenceContext.aspects`) and pass it down. Across a city the aspects are summed by `ThemeInsightService` (trends), which counts a review or a post with an unread claim apart ("not filed yet") instead of leaving the table out, since one stray claim would blank a whole city. Pages read labels from the API (`StatementOutputDto.parts[].topic: { key, label, kind, parent? }`, populated on every route that serves `statements`), never from a copy of the vocabulary.
- **Claims have vectors, a search and discovery layer, never a count** (`StatementEmbeddingService`, the one writer): every claim with text gets `Statement.embedding`, `text-embedding-3-large` shortened by OpenAI's `dimensions` to 1024 (`EmbeddingsService.embedMany(values, { dimensions })` in `modules/llm`: Mastra's router hands `providerOptions.openai.dimensions` to the AI SDK, which puts it in the request body; it throws on any other count or length, so a wrong vector is never stored), `halfvec(1024)` with an HNSW `halfvec_cosine_ops` index, hidden and **lazy** (no other read of statements selects it). - **Models by volume** (each OpenAI account's daily complimentary allowance: 10M tokens on `gpt-5.4-mini`, 1M on `gpt-5.4`; past it the account is blocked for that model, and once every account is, the step fails): the bulk agents run on the `mini` tier (`gpt-5.4-mini` on the allowance) — `MentionExtractionAgent` (~3,000 tokens a post with the theme legend and topic labels: 1,100 posts took 3.3M on 2026-10-22; 641 posts took 0.9M on the full model before them), `ReviewAnalysisAgent` (batches of 10 reviews, since mini reads a long batch less closely), `VenueMatchAgent`, `CommentIntentAgent` (one comment a call; `CommentDisagreementAgent` then one sentence a post on what its disagreeing comments say), `PressClassificationAgent` (one page a call), `ReviewClaimsAgent` (A, one review a call), `PostGateAgent` (the gate, one post a call on the start of its text), the nightly `terms` backlog. `PostClaimsAgent` (A for posts, one call a short post, 1 + its parts a long one) is on `full` since 2026-11-05: copying out foods and numbers too, mini found fewer post claims (90% → 81% in the eval), gpt-5.4 kept them; Berlin's ~170 posts a day are ~0.8M of the two accounts' 2M a day. The claims' labels are no tier: the claim-labels classifier on Jev (one claim a call, its source's other claims beside it, `claimPartsQuestions`), paid by the call, also for the nightly `statement-parts` backlog (whose tokens don't count against the day's LLM budget). Embeddings are `text-embedding-3-large`, paid per token ($0.13 a million at list price; no complimentary allowance assumed), on their own account (`openai.embedding-account.ts`, never held by a limit), every request filed in the ledger under it. What reaches an owner stays on the `full` tier (`gpt-5.4`): `ReportAnalysisAgent` (one tool-calling call per report: on dev Berlin's Nhas City Chicken September 2026 report, 3 moves with their checks, 1 tool call, 2 model steps, ~10k tokens, ~32 s; ~20 reports a month is a rounding error on the allowance), `ReportEditorAgent` (one tool-calling call per configuration: on the same report, 4 tool calls, 2 model steps, ~25k tokens, ~20 s), `PitchHookAgent` (and `CitySourceSuggestionAgent`); ~20 reports a month fit its 1M. Moving an agent between tiers is a cost decision: check the volume first. Past the allowance both tiers go on to Claude and cost money, so a step that runs out of allowance on a heavy day keeps going on the credit instead of failing.
- **Website icons are plain fetches, never Apify, one per website** (the same home page fetch gives the site's profile links, see "An account we never saw post"): `VenueFaviconCron` runs hourly for up to `FAVICON_RUN_SECONDS` (20 min; a site started by then finishes inside the job's 30), reading due open venues `FAVICON_PAGE` (200) at a time and fetching `FAVICON_CONCURRENCY` (8) websites side by side, each a different host. A due venue's website host (`hostOf`: no `www.`, any scheme or path) brings every other open venue on it into the same refresh, due or not, so a chain's branches on one site share one fetch and one upload: one ImageKit file per icon, one `Image` row per venue on it (`ImageService.duplicate`), and a branch joining later takes the stored file without an upload. The key is the website, not the brand: a brand's branches may have sites of their own. Due order: never looked for first (`saveGooglePlace` clears `faviconCheckedAt` when the website changes), then found ones after `FAVICON_REFRESH_DAYS` (30), then ones where nothing was found after `FAVICON_RETRY_DAYS` (7). A website that is a platform profile (Instagram, Linktree, Lieferando, …: `faviconSite`), an IP or a local name has no icon of its own (`no_site` drops a stored one). Candidates, best first: the page's `<link rel=icon|apple-touch-icon>` by declared size (top `FAVICON_MAX_LINKS`), `/favicon.ico` on the host the page redirected to, Google's `s2/favicons` copy (404 = none). Every fetch has a 10 s timeout, a size cap and checks each redirect hop (`isPublicWebUrl`); a file is an icon by its bytes (`faviconFormat`), not its name or content type. Unchanged bytes (sha256 `faviconHash`) are not uploaded again; a replaced icon's `Image` rows are removed together (`ImageService.removeImages`), and the file goes once no row outside them shares it: removed one at a time, each row would see the others and leave the file behind. A failed fetch keeps the stored icon. ImageKit reads no dimensions from some ICO files: `createImageFromBytes` deletes the upload and throws `UnreadableImageError`, and the next candidate is tried. Any other upload error (missing keys, an outage) is no verdict on the site: the run stops, the venues it didn't finish stay due (no `faviconCheckedAt`), and the job fails after flushing what it stored. **The same bytes on `GENERIC_FAVICON_HOSTS` (3) different hosts are a platform default** (WordPress's logo, Wix's `pfavico.ico`), not the venue's: they are dropped from every venue holding them and the hash is kept, so the count stays and the venue waits the refresh period; a chain's branches on one host count once. The admin venue list, competitor lists and venue header show `favicon`; every route serializing it must populate it (`forceObject` turns an unpopulated relation into `{ id }`, which fails `ImageOutputDto`).
- **Lead score** (0–100, `/admin/venues` column and "Prospects only" at `LEAD_PROSPECT_SCORE` 60, breakdown on the venue Overview tab): five weighted signals in `LEAD_SCORE_WEIGHTS` over the report month and the one before — buzz (posts by others, views, creators as percentiles among venues others posted about, since scrape budgets vary), already spends (own posts, promotional posts by others, website), nameable problem (half the strongest, half the mean of complaint share, unanswered critical reviews, reply rate under 50 %, noticed changes, negative or mixed posts; definitions as in `summarizeReport`), reachable, press (best classified press of the window: a feature 1, a listicle 0.75, a passing mention 0.25; unknown for a venue `SCRAPE_PRESS` never searched in the window, and `missing` says "press not searched"; `press` is optional in `LeadSignals`, since older scores lack it) — plus a new-place bonus. **Without reviews in the window the problem signal is read from the rating** (`LeadInputs.districtRatingPercentile`: the lowest district percentile among its open branches, as the report's benchmark computes it; `cap((50 − p) / 50)` next to the negative-post share, half the strongest, half the mean), **and is unknown (`null`, counted at half) only without one (fewer than 5 district peers); `missing` says "reviews not scraped" either way**: missing data neither sinks nor lifts a venue. `LeadSignal.value` is optional in the type because `serialize({ skipNull })` drops that null inside the jsonb too, and a required `number | null` fails the response type. A name on the city's `chainDenyList` keeps half its score, and `LeadSignals.denyListed` says so (the Sales tab's panel shows it). A brand without an open branch has none. The score is the brand's (`Brand.leadScore`): each city's `SCORE_LEADS` scores the brands with an open branch in the city over all their open branches in every city (posts, reviews, press summed; a phone or website on any branch; new only when every branch is), against the buzz of the city's brands; the venue list shows and sorts by its brand's. The frontend mirrors the threshold in `lib/culinary.ts`.
- Months are `YYYY-MM`, UTC.
- **A shared report link is the token alone**: 16 random bytes as 22 base64url characters, unique; anyone holding it reads the report with no login. Revoking is final (a new link replaces it), and a revoked or unknown token answers the same 404. Every share route goes through `ReportShareService`.

The count is one `update … open_count + 1`.
- The public PDF (`/reports/shared/:token/pdf`) is public Chrome work, so `SharedReportsController` keeps the last 8 printed PDFs per report version (id + `updatedAt`) and shares an in-flight print; the repo has no rate limiter. The `/r` page links to it with a plain `<a download>` (a phone handles an attachment better than a blob).
- **Log as sent**: the admin report page's Share panel logs a link through the outreach admin routes (`listProspects({ brandId })`, then `logOutreachTouch` → `OutreachService.log`, kind EMAIL or DM, the link in the body, template `report-link`) when the brand is a prospect that hasn't opted out. `OutreachModule` imports `ReportsModule`, so the reports module never imports outreach.

- **Busyness** (Google popular times as a foot-traffic proxy): `busynessIndex` = mean of the histogram's ten busiest hours, 0–100. Google scales each histogram to the venue's own peak, so the index compares a venue with itself over time, never venues with each other. A sample is a detail-page `places.run` of the report venues and their competitors (which also refreshes `Venue.details`), its items read by `VenueBusynessService.saveSample`; a venue whose page shows no popular times gets a row with `histogram: null`, one the scrape returned nothing for gets none. The report's section ("Did the buzz fill the place?") is absent without a sample with popular times; its weeks are Mondays in the city's time zone (`monthWeeks`, `weekStart`), each measure on its own row and scale (views, busyness, booked out), never two on one axis.
- **Booked out compares between venues; busyness only as a change.** A booked-out share is the same question (a table for two, 18:00–22:00) asked of every venue, so it is shown as a level next to others'; Google's busyness index is scaled to each venue's own peak, so a competitor's busyness is only ever its change on the month before (points, "+6" / "−3"), never a level beside another venue's. `ReportSignalsService.sections` also returns `competitors` with `demand` (`{ booked, evenings }`, the month's mean over its sampled evenings) and `busyness` (`{ index, previousIndex? }`), each absent without samples, never zero. `ReportDemand.district` / `city` (`{ median, venues }`) is the median of the other venues' booked-out shares on the venue's own sampled evenings (`VenueReservationService.cityEvenings`: one query, latest sample per venue and evening; pure `bookedMedian`, `eveningsBooked`, `meanBooked` in `util/reservations.ts`), absent below `BOOKED_MEDIAN_MIN_VENUES` (5). The report's "Weekend tables" tile lists the competitors' median, the district and the city under the venue's figure (the weekly grid stays the venue's own); the competitor table gains "Weekend tables" and "Foot traffic" columns when any row has them. The prompt gets `weekendTables` only when the venue, a sampled competitor and the district all have figures, and `competitorFootTraffic` as changes in points.
- **Open tables** (`SAMPLE_RESERVATIONS`, Thursdays 19:00 city time, for the coming Friday and Saturday): every open venue of the city whose `Venue.bookingUrl`, Google `reserveTableUrl` or website (in that order, `reservationUrlOf`) is a page we can sample (`reservationPlatformOf`): Google's own booking page (`google`), TheFork or OpenTable. `VenueReservationService.sampleOrder` orders them won prospects, the month's other report venues, their competitors, then the rest by `leadScore` in tens, each band shuffled, and `RESERVATION_SAMPLE_MAX_VENUES` (600) cuts the list; the run result gives `venues`, `noPlatform`, `capped`, `samples`, `byPlatform`. The evening is 18:00–22:00 in quarter hours (16 slots); `booked` = share of closed slots. **Google's booking page** (`GoogleReserveAvailabilityService`, free, no Apify, no browser): a plain fetch of `google.com/maps/reserve/dine?c=<merchant id>&ld=<YYYYMMDDTHHMMSS>&ps=2&hl=en`, whose first response holds the slot grid; `ld` is read as **UTC** (`zonedTimeToUtc` of 19:00 and 21:00 local: two loads per date, each grid an hour either side); from Europe it needs the `SOCS` consent cookie or it redirects to consent.google.com. The pure `util/google-reserve.ts` reads the date label, one grid per bookable area (slot buttons' `aria-label`: "8:15 PM", "Selected: 8:00 PM"; a closed slot has none; some venues list 5- or 10-minute slots, rounded down to their quarter hour) and the footer's partner ("in partnership with OpenTable", or "Booking providers supply these times" + several, stored sorted and comma-joined); a quarter hour is open when any area lists it in either load; a page showing the date with no grid is 0 open, one showing another date is no sample. Every Google sample rewrites `Venue.reservationPartner`. Pacing: two loads in flight, a jittered 1–2 s pause before each (~1 h for 600 venues); a failed load, a redirect or the captcha ("unusual traffic", `/sorry/`) stops the provider, keeps the samples taken (`PartialAvailabilityError`) and fails the run. `bookingUrl` is the hand-set link (a column, not in `details`, which scrapes merge into), edited on the admin venue page's "Demand and delivery" panel through `PATCH /admin/venues/:id` → `VenueReservationService.setBookingUrl`, which accepts only an http(s) Google booking, TheFork or OpenTable page (400 otherwise); owners don't set it. Quandoo shut down on 30 September 2026 and has no provider. The paid providers' runs are children of the `sample-reservations` run (started before Google's pages are fetched, so they run meanwhile) and split the step's budget: TheFork (`clearpath/thefork-scraper`, restaurant URLs, reviews and photos off, ~$0.006 a venue) gives seven days of bookable windows without a party size (`partySize` null; a date it lists with no window is fully booked, a date it doesn't list is not sampled); OpenTable (`canadesk/opentable`, `process: "ga"`, by exact name, for two at 20:00, kept for hand-set OpenTable links) is one run per date and domain, each charged at least 1,000 units (~$0.26). One platform failing keeps the others' samples and fails the run. The report names the platform "Reserve with Google (via OpenTable)" (`ReportDemand.partner`).
- **Delivery and prices** (`SCRAPE_DELIVERY`, `scrape-delivery.month`, which calls `refresh-venue-details` first, $7: Lieferando's actor refuses a cap under $5; one Wolt run per linked restaurant, started together and awaited in order, then Lieferando's one listing on what the budget has left, every run a child on the `apify` queue): Wolt per restaurant from the Wolt link in `Venue.details.orderBy` (`needy_hammock/wolt-restaurant-menu-scraper` direct-URL mode, prices in cents, rating = `ratingScore / 2`); Lieferando for the venues not on Wolt from one city-wide listing (`studio-amba/takeaway-scraper`, which takes no restaurant URL), matched by name and postcode, rating and fees only (its menus come only for a whole search). A month's scrape replaces that month's `MenuItem`s and `VenueDeliverySnapshot` (rating, review count, fee, minimum order; one per listing and month, since the listing row is overwritten each run and is the only other copy; the listings scraped before it, all for September 2026, were copied by `Migration20261029200000_delivery_snapshots_fill`). **A report reads a listing's figures as of its month** (`VenueDeliveryService.figures`: the latest snapshot up to the month, the listing row only for a listing with none, so a rebuilt report never shows a later scrape's rating), and each `ReportDeliveryListing.previous` is the month before's rating and count, present only when both that month and the report month were scraped; `priceSentence` gives the prompt the change ("your Lieferando rating is 4.8 from 17 ratings, 4.6 from 12 ratings in August 2026") and `ReportPrices.svelte` shows it under the rating. **Menu items are linked to topics when the scrape stores them** (`MenuTopicService.linkItems` over `resolveMenuItem`: the name, else the menu section; a non-main course gives drinks only, a kind of place never; re-linked by the job), and a topic's menu price is the median of each venue's own median for it (a long menu weighs as one venue), districts at `MENU_DISTRICT_MIN_VENUES` (5); menus exist only for report venues and competitors, so the topic page says its figures lean to them. `pricePosition` (optional `topic`, `PricePosition.topic`: the venue's primary food against the same on the other menus, the line to lead with; the report gets it through `MenuTopicService.pricePosition`, which `ReportSignalsService.prices` calls with each branch and its others, so `ReportPrices.menu.topic` is that branch's own primary topic, and `priceSentence` and `ReportPrices.svelte` lead with it only when `othersMedian` is there: another menu lists it): median main course (drinks, desserts, sides, extras left out by `isMainCourse`) against the median of the competitors' medians, else the district's menus; dishes (the report's top five) match menu items as whole-word runs. Price complaints read against tracked competitors with 5+ written reviews.
- **Correlations** (`util/correlations.ts`, pure, `ReportData.correlations`): buzz to reviews (views by week against new Google reviews by week; the week after the most viewed week against the venue's usual pace from the six months before, over months that have any review, so unscraped months don't count as zero), complaint times (by the hour a review was posted, the report month and the five before; a topic is placed only where 3+ of its reviews gather at 1.5× the share of all reviews posted then, since evenings carry most reviews of any kind), paid against organic (median views, reviews in the seven days after). Floors: 10 reviews, 3 posts; below them a relation is absent. Wording of the sentences is "followed by", never "caused"; the prompt gets the pre-written sentences (`signalsPromptInput`) and is told to quote them.
- **Reasons are hypotheses with a confidence word, never a proven cause** (the user's call, 2026-10-25): the moves and the standard checks about them are found in code, the model only labels possible reasons likely / possible / unclear with evidence for and against, worded "could" / "may have"; `checkAnalysis` drops a line saying because / caused / due to, a banned word or a number the model was not given (the observation, its checks, the prompt, every tool answer), and downgrades a likely without evidence. The editor keeps each label's word in its own words and never upgrades one. Resolution is never finer than the data: follower counts are the snapshots' days (`OwnAccountService.followerPoints`: every scrape that gives a count writes a `SocialAccountSnapshot`, so a watched report brand gets a weekly point), busyness and tables weekly, posts and reviews daily, dish searches every `City.dishSearchEveryDays` and ratings monthly. Data gaps are a task, never UI: `p2.chore.report-data-gaps-and-scrape-frequency`.

- **The ingestion agents are scored, not eyeballed** (`ingestion-eval` skill): agents A, B and C and matching build their inputs and read their answers through pure functions the eval shares (`readPostClaims`, `reviewClaimsInput`, `claimPartsInput`, `claimPartsOf`, `termTopicInputTopics`, `termAnswerLinks`, `decideMatch`, `toCandidateVenues`), so a change there is what the eval scores; `npm run eval -- run --label <name>` before and after a prompt or model change, against Opus-written references on ~370 stored sources and ~500 wordings.

## Portal

The venue's own product: `/portal` on the site, `modules/portal` on the API (`PortalController`, `PortalService`; imports auth, reports, outreach, venues and social, none of which import it).

- **Who gets in is a brand membership** (`BrandMember`, `BrandMemberService`, `BrandAccessGuard`, all in `auth`: `domain-identity-and-auth`); an admin invites from the prospect page and opens any brand's portal. Every `/portal/brands/:brandId/*` route carries the guard (the portal spec checks it), and a report id of another brand answers 403 `NO_BRAND_ACCESS`, an unknown one 404.
- Routes: `GET /portal/brands` (the person's memberships, each brand with its open branches and cities), `/brands/:id` (the brand card), `/reports` (every city's months, no bodies, each with its city), `/reports/:reportId` (the shared link's DTO: `toOutput`, no `sentAt`), `/reports/:reportId/pdf` (printed per request), `/series?months=&cityId=`, `/patterns?months=&cityId=` (6 by default; one city, the brand's first without `cityId`: its reports; `branchId` picks the branch whose rating, busyness and open tables they show, the most reviewed by default, 404 for one that isn't an open branch there; both answer `branches` and `branchId` for the picker), `GET/PATCH /settings` (the prospect's `contactEmail` and `language` through `OutreachService.forBrand` / `update`; `editable: false` when the brand is no prospect), `/reviews` (paginated; `month`, `stars`, `topic`, `answered`, `language`, `needsReply` = written, ≤3 stars, unanswered, lowest first), `/review-filters` (months, languages, topics with counts, the needs-reply count), `PUT /reviews/:reviewId/replied`, `/posts` (`month`, `platform`; `paid` = ad or promotional, `own` = the post's account is the brand's), `/creators`, `/post-filters`, `GET/POST /competitors` (a place or a whole brand, optionally for one branch; + suggestions while under the cap), `DELETE /competitors/:competitorId` (its list id), `/competitor-search?q=` (places in the brand's cities, each with its brand's branch count).
- **The portal is the whole brand**: reviews, their filters and "I answered it" cover every branch (each review's `branch`: name · street, for a chain), posts and creators are the brand's mentions (`BrandPostsService`), reports are per city (Months shows the city when there are several); Trends and Patterns read one city at a time (`?city=`, `CityPicker`, which drops the branch) and a chain's place charts (rating, foot traffic, weekend tables) one branch at a time (`?branch=`, `BranchPicker`, the most reviewed by default; the charts' notes name the branch, and a stale `?branch=` falls back to the default). A stored report without the picked branch (built before it joined) gives that branch nothing for its month: another branch's figures never stand in; the brand's counts stay the brand's.
- **A review's "answered"**: `answered` is `ownerResponse` or the owner's `markedRepliedAt` (kept even after a scrape confirms it). No reviewer names: the entity stores none.
- **The series reads stored reports only** (`util/portal-series.ts`, pure): one row per month, oldest first, ending with the last finished month or a newer report; `null` where a month has nothing; a month without a report but followed by one gets the counts from its `previous`; the all-time rating comes from every report's `ratingTrend`, else the report's own figure. Fields a stored report may lack (`previous`, `ratingTrend`, `competitors`, …) are optional there. Booked out is read from `VenueReservationSample`, not reports (`addBooked`: the venue's month `booked`, each competitor row's `booked`, and `districtBooked`, the district median on the venue's own evenings at 5+ venues; all absent without samples). Busyness is read from `VenueBusyness`, not reports (`addBusyness`: the month's mean index over samples with popular times, the peak of their mean histogram; absent, not zero, for a month without one); own-account followers are read from `SocialAccountSnapshot` through `OwnAccountService.snapshots` (`addOwnAccounts`: each month's accounts with followers, posts and views per post at the month's close; absent for a month no snapshot closes). The series' `calendar` (one `MonthContext` per month) comes from `CityDayService.months`, not from reports; the Trends tab shades holidays and events inside each month's band on the count charts.
- **The patterns read stored reports, never recompute** (`util/portal-patterns.ts`, pure, `PortalService.patterns`): per month the report's `correlations` as stored (a relation absent where the report lacks it, `reportId` null without a report); per week (Mondays, city time, every week touching the window) posts by others and views from the reports' `posts`, new Google reviews from `reviews.list` (TripAdvisor items skipped), booked out from `demand.evenings`, busyness from `VenueBusyness` samples. A week spanning two reports sums both (each report counts only its own month); a week with days in a month without a report is `partial`, one with none has no counts, and a covering report without a review list leaves its reviews unknown — absent, never zero. The page (`[brandId]/patterns`) draws them with the shared `MonthChart` (`components/charts`) (its `tickLabel` / `longLabel` / `tableLabel` / `othersLabel` props put weeks on the x axis): views, reviews, busyness and booked out by week; the buzz ratio and paid-against-organic reviews after a post by month; complaint times as a table; the picked month's sentences above.
- Frontend: `routes/(portal)` (English, its own layout: `setUiLabels`, `lang="en"`), `portal/login`, `portal/(app)` (`requireLogin`; `/portal` opens the only brand or lists several), `[brandId]` (header with the brand switcher and its places, link tabs Report · Months · Reviews · Posts · Trends · Patterns · Settings; Reviews puts "Needs a reply" above the filtered list, each card with "I answered it" (undo on the toast); Posts toggles posts and creators; Settings holds the competitor picker), the report through `ReportView` as is, PDFs fetched as a blob with the session's token. Trends (`trends/components`): one measure per chart with the venue in `primary-darker` and competitors as `strip-muted` lines (emphasis, one y axis; a `dashed` comparison is a baseline, the district median on the "Weekend tables" chart under Foot traffic), sentiment as 100% columns in the report's colours, topics and dishes as rows of sparklines on a shared scale; a month chip or a tap picks the month on every chart and opens its report; every chart has a table and arrow-key month stepping.
